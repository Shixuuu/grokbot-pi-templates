# PA power rate review

Read only. Never switch suppliers, enroll in anything, pay, or contact the utility without the owner's explicit OK. Keep account numbers and dollar figures in the private app chat.

1. **Pull account data.** In the utility's self-service portal (the owner signs in; a captcha means handing over the screen), list every account with: service address, rate schedule (e.g. RS residential, GS-1 small general service), current supplier, 12 months of kWh and bill totals, and the latest bill's line items (customer charge, distribution per kWh, any demand charge per kW, generation and transmission per kWh, price to compare, taxes). For net-metered accounts, also capture delivered vs exported kWh, the banked kWh, and the annual excess payout.
2. **Pull the market.** Get the current price to compare and its end date (PPL resets Jun 1 and Dec 1), plus the distribution tariff. Get offers for the owner's ZIP and rate class from PAPowerSwitch (https://www.papowerswitch.com): rate, term, cancellation fee, monthly fee, renewable %, and teaser or variable flags. Cite every source and never invent a figure.
3. **Solar/net-metered accounts.** Under 52 Pa. Code §75.13, default service credits exports at full retail, banks them, and pays out the leftover at the price to compare at the end of the May 31 year. Switching cashes out the bank mid-year. On a supplier, net metering is optional, and many suppliers give nothing or a lower rate for exports. Recommend a supplier only if its own written terms credit exports at full retail. Otherwise estimate the loss from exports billed at supply rate.
4. **Low-usage accounts.** Check whether the fixed customer charge (the minimum bill) dominates. A supplier changes only the generation charge, so its savings are usually pennies, and any supplier monthly fee wipes them out. Look for real levers instead: a wrong rate schedule (e.g. a personal-use building billed as commercial GS-1, with a higher customer charge, demand charge, and sales tax), combining meters, closing unneeded meters, or virtual meter aggregation (§75.14(e): same name, within 2 miles, offsets at retail).
5. **Watch for traps.** Flag 2-4 month teaser terms that roll into variable rates, introductory prices, monthly fees, cancellation fees, and auto-renewal to month-to-month variable. Also flag unpaid balances or late fees seen on bills.
6. **Report.** Give a recommendation and estimated annual savings per property, plus what's unverified. Then offer next steps: draft a re-rate or meter request for the owner to send or call in, and optionally a one-time check when the next price to compare is published.
