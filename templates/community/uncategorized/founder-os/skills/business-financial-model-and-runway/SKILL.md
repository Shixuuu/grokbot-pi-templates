# Business financial model and runway
Profit is an opinion, cash is a fact. A good model is built from drivers you can act on (leads, conversion, price, churn, headcount), so every decision shows up as cash in a specific month. For the week-by-week view of dated cash events, use thirteen-week-cash-calendar.

## Inputs
- 12-24 months of actuals: revenue, COGS, operating expenses, headcount, cash balance (bookkeeping, bank, billing). Read-only.
- Drivers: leads, conversion rates, price and mix, churn, sales cycle (funnel-and-conversion-audit, unit-economics).
- Planned hires, spend, debt payments, tax obligations, and capex.

## Steps
1. **Actuals first:** a monthly P&L and cash-flow view for the trailing period. Reconcile cash to the bank. Separate one-offs.
2. **Driver tree:** revenue = (new customers from each channel × price) + retained customers × price + expansion. New customers = leads × conversion. Costs = COGS per unit × units + headcount plan + fixed opex.
3. **Monthly forecast for 12-24 months:** P&L, cash flow (with collection timing, payment terms, deferred revenue, inventory where relevant), and ending cash.
4. **Scenarios:**
   | Scenario | Key assumptions | Revenue month 12 | Lowest cash point | Runway |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | Downside | e.g., conversion −30%, churn +50% | | | |
   | Base | current trend | | | |
   | Upside | plan hits | | | |
5. **Runway:** months until cash hits the minimum safe balance (not zero) at current net burn, under each scenario. Flag the month a decision must be made (raise, cut or slow) at least 6 months before cash runs out.
6. **Decision tests:** for each proposed hire or spend, show its cost, the driver it must move, the break-even month, and its effect on runway.
7. **Rule of 40 (later stage, recurring revenue):** growth % + profit margin % ≥ 40 as a balance heuristic.
8. **Sensitivity:** which 2-3 drivers move ending cash most? Those become scorecard metrics (company-operating-system).
9. **Compare to actuals monthly**, explain variances, and update assumptions. Never quietly change history.

## Output
Model workbook (assumptions tab, monthly P&L, cash flow, scenarios, runway chart) plus a one-page summary: cash today, burn, runway per scenario, decision month, top 3 sensitivities, and recommended moves.

## Metric it moves
Months of runway, forecast accuracy (actual vs. plan variance), and cash efficiency (net new revenue ÷ net burn).

## Re-measure
Monthly close: actual vs. forecast, re-forecast, runway refresh. Quarterly: rebuild the scenarios.
