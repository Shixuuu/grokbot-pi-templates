# Negotiation prep
People decide emotionally and justify logically. Win by making the other side feel understood, then asking questions that make them solve your problem. Tactical empathy, labels, mirrors, calibrated questions, the accusation audit and Ackerman bargaining come from Chris Voss (Never Split the Difference); BATNA comes from Fisher and Ury (Getting to Yes).

## Inputs
- What's being negotiated, with whom, and the decision deadline.
- Your goals, your walk-away point, and your alternatives.
- What's known about the other side: their goals, pressures, constraints, decision-maker, history. Mark unknowns; they become questions.

## Steps
1. **Goal and walk-away:** an ambitious but defensible target, and the point where you walk. Write both down before talking.
2. **BATNA:** your best alternative if there's no deal, and how to strengthen it before the talk (another bidder, another vendor, another candidate). Estimate theirs.
3. **Their world:** what they need, fear and must report to their boss; what would make them look good; what's cheap for them to give and valuable to you, and vice versa. List possible "black swans", the unknown facts that would change everything, and how to surface them.
4. **Accusation audit:** list every negative thing they might think about you or the proposal and say it first ("You may feel this price is aggressive…"). It defuses fear before it grows.
5. **Labels and mirrors:** draft 5 labels ("It seems like…", "It sounds like…", "It looks like…") and plan to mirror (repeat their last few words) to keep them talking. Aim for them to say "That's right."
6. **Calibrated questions:** 5-8 open "how" and "what" questions that get them to solve your problem: "How am I supposed to do that?", "What's the biggest challenge you face on this?", "How would you like us to proceed?", "What makes this a good deal for you?" Avoid "why" (it sounds accusatory).
7. **Concession plan (Ackerman for price):** set the target, first offer at 65% of it, then 85%, 95% and 100% in shrinking steps; use precise, non-round final numbers; add a non-monetary item at the end to show you're at the limit. Trade every concession for something; never give one for free.
8. **Get to a real yes:** confirm implementation with "how" questions (who else needs to approve, what could get in the way). A "yes" without a "how" is a counterfeit yes.
9. **Debrief after:** outcome versus target and walk-away, what they revealed, what to do differently. Draft the follow-up summary.

## Output
One-page prep sheet: goal · walk-away · BATNA (ours and theirs) · their world · black-swan hypotheses · accusation audit · labels · calibrated questions · concession ladder · implementation questions. Plus the drafted opening and follow-up messages.

## Metric it moves
Value captured versus target (price, terms, time); deals closed versus walked; time to close.

## Re-measure
Debrief within 24 hours of each negotiation. Over 5+ negotiations, track value captured versus target and which tools moved the other side.
