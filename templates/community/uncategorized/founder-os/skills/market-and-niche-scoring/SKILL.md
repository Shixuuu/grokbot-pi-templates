# Market and niche scoring
Market beats offer beats persuasion. A great offer in a dead market loses to an average offer in a starving crowd, so pick the crowd first and prove it with a test, not a spreadsheet.

## Inputs
- 3-10 candidate markets, each written as **who + painful problem** (e.g., "independent dental practices that can't fill hygiene chairs"), not a product idea.
- The owner's unfair advantages: skills, access, audience, capital, credibility.
- Constraints: time, money cap (default $0), walls (employer, legal, licensing).

## Steps
1. **Write each candidate as an avatar + problem.** If you can't name a specific buyer and the problem they already spend money on, it's a lead, not a candidate.
2. **Score on Hormozi's four market tests (1-5 each), with evidence per score:**
   | Test | Mechanism | Evidence that counts |
   |---|---|---|
   | Massive pain | Pain drives urgency, and urgency drives price tolerance | Money or hours already being spent on bad fixes; complaints in forums and reviews |
   | Purchasing power | They have to be able to pay a premium | Revenue per buyer, budgets, existing spend on tools or services |
   | Easy to target | Cheap reach = cheap CAC | Named communities, lists, associations, events, ad audiences |
   | Growing | A rising tide lowers effort | Search trend, category revenue growth, new entrants funded |
3. **Score the owner fit (1-5):** advantage (access, skill, credibility), interest that will survive 3+ years, and wall conflicts (a wall conflict = disqualified).
4. **Size it roughly:** count of reachable buyers × realistic annual spend on this problem. Show the math and sources; mark gaps Unknown. Note whether the ceiling supports the target stage (see a long-range strategy and moat plan).
5. **Check the competition signal:** competitors charging real money is a good sign (proven demand). Note the gap you could own (speed, niche, guarantee, price model). If there are no competitors and no spend, that's usually no market.
6. **Rank:** total = pain × buying power × reachability × growth × fit (multiply so one zero kills it). Pick #1 and a backup.
7. **Design the proof test** with prove-or-kill-experiment: e.g., 20 discovery conversations (customer-interview-synthesis), then a paid pilot or pre-sale. A waitlist doesn't count.
8. **Commit or kill:** commit to one niche for the test window. Don't run several niches at once.

## Output
| Market (who + problem) | Pain | Power | Reach | Growth | Fit | Total | Size math | Top evidence | Biggest risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Plus: chosen niche, backup, proof test, and the date.

## Metric it moves
Time-to-first-paying-customer, and the quality of the market chosen (measured later by close rate and CAC in that niche).

## Re-measure
At the proof-test date: paying customers, close rate, and cost per qualified conversation versus the bar. Miss → re-score with the new evidence, then switch to the backup or narrow the niche.
