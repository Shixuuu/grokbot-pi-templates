# Company operating system
Strategy only matters if it turns into weekly numbers someone owns. A light, repeatable rhythm (vision → annual targets → quarterly priorities → weekly scorecard → weekly meeting) beats heroics. The mechanics follow EOS (Traction) and Scaling Up's one-page plan; quarterly planning runs the quarterly planning session itself.

## Inputs
- Vision and long-term targets (a long-range strategy and moat plan), the current stage and constraint (growth-constraint-finder).
- Team and roles, current metrics (unit-economics, funnel numbers, retention).
- Existing meetings and reports.

## Steps
1. **One-page company OS** (fill each in a line or two):
   ```
   Purpose / why we exist:
   Core values (3-5, each with a behavior that shows it):
   Niche / who we serve (positioning):
   10-year target (one number):
   3-year picture (revenue, profit, customers, team, what it looks like):
   1-year plan (3-7 goals with numbers):
   This quarter's priorities (3-7, each owned by one person):
   Scorecard metrics (5-15 weekly numbers):
   Accountability chart (functions → one owner each):
   Issues list (open):
   ```
2. **Accountability chart:** functions first (sales and marketing, delivery/operations, finance, product, people), then one owner per seat with 3-5 outcomes each. One person can hold several seats early; no seat has two owners.
3. **Weekly scorecard:** 5-15 leading numbers, each with one owner, a weekly target, and red/green. Prefer leading indicators (outreach volume, booked calls, activation rate) alongside lagging ones (revenue, churn). Source every number; Unknown is allowed but turns red.
4. **Quarterly priorities (rocks):** 3-7 per company, each with an owner, a measurable done line (a written goal, scope, and done line), and milestones. Everything else waits.
5. **Weekly meeting (same day and time, ~60-90 min):**
   | Segment | Time | What |
   |---|---|---|
   | Good news | 5 min | One personal and one business win each |
   | Scorecard | 5 min | Read numbers; off-track goes to issues |
   | Priorities | 5 min | On or off track only; off goes to issues |
   | Headlines | 5 min | Customer and employee news |
   | To-dos | 5 min | Last week's done or not done |
   | Issues | most of it | Identify the root cause, discuss, solve (IDS), top 3 first |
   | Conclude | 5 min | Recap to-dos and messages, rate the meeting 1-10 |
6. **Quarterly planning:** score last quarter's priorities, refresh the 1-year plan, pick the next priorities, and resolve the biggest issues. Annual: refresh the 3-year picture and the values.
7. **People check each quarter:** does each seat-holder get it, want it and have the capacity for it, and do they live the values? Act on gaps (a structured hiring process).
8. **Keep it light:** if a meeting or report doesn't change a decision, cut it (a first-principles process audit).

## Output
The one-page OS, accountability chart, scorecard template, meeting agenda, and quarterly plan, filed where the team works.

## Metric it moves
% of quarterly priorities completed, % of scorecard metrics green, and issue resolution time; ultimately revenue and profit versus the 1-year plan.

## Re-measure
Weekly via the scorecard; quarterly via the priorities completion rate (aim for most of them, about 80%). Rating of the weekly meeting is the cheap early-warning signal.
