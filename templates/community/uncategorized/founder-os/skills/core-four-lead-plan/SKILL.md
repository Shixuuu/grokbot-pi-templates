# Core Four lead plan
There are only four ways to let people know about something: reach out or post, to people who know you or people who don't (Hormozi's Core Four in $100M Leads). Volume beats cleverness early. Most businesses are under-doing the basics, not missing a secret channel.

| | One-to-one | One-to-many |
|---|---|---|
| **Warm** (know you) | Warm outreach | Free content |
| **Cold** (don't) | Cold outreach | Paid ads |

## Inputs
- The offer and avatar (grand-slam-offer-design), the target revenue and close rate, and the lead magnet if any.
- Unit economics: allowable CAC (unit-economics).
- Owner hours per day available, team, budget cap (default $0 until the owner approves spend).
- Current channel numbers: actions per day, reply rate, leads, booked calls, closes. Unknown is fine; it gets measured in week 1.

## Steps
1. **Work backward from the goal:** customers needed ÷ close rate = qualified calls ÷ show rate = booked ÷ booking rate = engaged leads ÷ reply rate = daily outreach actions. Show the math.
2. **Pick the starting channel by stage:** with no audience and no money, start with warm outreach (contact list, "who do you know who…" asks), then cold outreach. Free content compounds slowly, so start it now. Paid ads come after the offer converts and CAC is known (paid-ads-test-plan).
3. **Lead magnet:** a free, specific win that solves a narrow problem and reveals the next one, which the paid offer solves (quiz, template, audit, mini-training, free trial). Draft it.
4. **Rule of 100:** commit to a fixed daily volume of primary actions (e.g., 100 outreach messages, or 100 minutes making content, or a daily ad budget) for at least 100 days. Consistency of volume is the plan. Set the target well above what feels comfortable; most people under-estimate the activity a goal needs (Grant Cardone's 10X Rule: size targets and actions at 10× normal).
5. **Scripts and assets** (drafts only): a warm reach-out that personalizes and asks for help or referrals, not a pitch; a cold sequence (personalize → big fast value → call to action, 4-6 touches); a content calendar (content-engine-pillar-to-micro); ad drafts (paid-ads-test-plan).
6. **Lead getters (later, to scale past the owner's hours):** customers (referral program), employees (SDRs, setters), agencies (to learn, then in-house), affiliates and partners (partner and affiliate deals). Each needs an SOP (SOPs and delegation).
7. **Order of scaling (More, Better, New):** first do more of what works, then make it better (conversion), then add a new channel. Don't add a channel until the current one hits a ceiling.
8. **Tracker:** a daily row per channel (actions, replies, leads, booked, shows, closes, cost). It feeds the company scorecard.

## Output
| Channel | Daily actions | Reply/engage % | Leads/wk | Booked/wk | Close % | CAC | Owner | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Plus: backward math, lead magnet draft, scripts (drafts), 100-day commitment, and the first lead getter to add.

## Metric it moves
Engaged leads per week and qualified calls booked per week, at or below the allowable CAC.

## Re-measure
Weekly, review the tracker against the backward math. After 2 weeks, fix the weakest ratio (reply, booking, show or close) with funnel-and-conversion-audit before adding volume or a channel.

Walls (always): draft only, never send, post, spend, book, sign, or trade; no PII in shared places; never invent a number (mark it Unknown); keep employer systems and material out unless the owner opts in. Cold outreach must follow anti-spam and privacy law (e.g., CAN-SPAM, TCPA, GDPR); route questions to an attorney.
