# Customer interview synthesis
Opinions are cheap and compliments are noise. Ask about what people did, what it cost them, and what they tried, then let the pattern decide (The Mom Test and Jobs-to-be-Done mechanisms).

## Inputs
- The decision the research must inform, in one line (e.g., "which problem to build the first offer around").
- Target segment and how to reach 10-20 people in it (warm intros, communities, existing customers, churned customers).
- Any existing transcripts, support tickets, reviews, sales-call notes, or survey answers.

## Steps
1. **Pick the decision and the sample.** 10-15 interviews per segment usually saturate. Include buyers, non-buyers, and churned customers if any.
2. **Draft the outreach** (drafts only): short, specific, no pitch. "I'm researching how [role] handles [problem]. 20 minutes, no sales." The owner sends.
3. **Script past-behavior questions** (no hypotheticals, no pitching):
   - "Tell me about the last time [problem] happened. What did you do?"
   - "What did that cost you in time or money?"
   - "What have you tried? Why did you stop?"
   - "Who else is involved in fixing it? Who signs off on spend?"
   - "If you had a magic wand, what would be different?" (job and outcome, not features)
   Avoid "Would you buy…?" and "Do you like…?"
4. **Capture verbatim** pains, desired outcomes, triggers ("what made you look now"), current alternatives, and the exact words they use. Record or transcribe only with consent.
5. **Code the transcripts:** tag each statement as a job, pain, trigger, alternative, objection, or buying-process note. Count frequency and intensity (spent money? lost a customer? emotional language?).
6. **Synthesize:**
   - Top jobs ranked by frequency × intensity.
   - The "hair on fire" pain: frequent, costly, already being paid for.
   - The current alternatives and why they fail. That gap is your positioning (positioning-and-category-design).
   - The voice-of-customer phrase bank for headlines, hooks and offer names.
7. **Commitment test:** an interview that ends with a concrete next step (a pilot, a deposit, an intro to the budget holder) is stronger evidence than ten "sounds great"s. Track how many interviews ended in a commitment.
8. **Feed forward** into grand-slam-offer-design (problems → solutions), pricing-and-packaging (willingness-to-pay signals), and the content engine (phrases → hooks).

## Output
| Job / pain | # mentions | Intensity (1-5) | Money already spent? | Current alternative | Verbatim quote (anonymized) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Plus: top 3 insights, the decision recommendation, the phrase bank, and the commitment count. Anonymize: role and company size, never names.

## Metric it moves
Close rate and message-market fit (ad CTR, reply rate) once the insights are used in copy; commitments per 10 interviews.

## Re-measure
After the insights ship into an offer or copy, compare reply, CTR and close rate to the pre-research baseline. Re-run 5 interviews each quarter, and immediately when churn or close rate moves more than about 20%.
