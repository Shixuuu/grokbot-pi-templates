# Sales call script and objections
Selling is helping someone make a decision that's good for them. Most lost deals come from weak discovery and unhandled fear, not from price. Revenue fixes most early-stage problems, so selling well is the first skill to build (Mark Cuban's "sales cures all" principle). Structure the call so the buyer sells themselves.

## Inputs
- The offer one-pager (grand-slam-offer-design) and price (pricing-and-packaging).
- Avatar pains and phrases (customer-interview-synthesis).
- Call recordings or notes, and lost-deal reasons, if any. Current show and close rate (or Unknown).

## Steps
1. **Pre-call:** qualification criteria (pain, budget, authority, timing), a short research checklist, and a pre-call message that boosts show rate (drafts only).
2. **Script the call with CLOSER** (Hormozi):
   | Step | Purpose | Example prompts |
   |---|---|---|
   | **C**larify | Why are they here, now? | "What made you take this call today?" |
   | **L**abel | Name the problem back so they agree | "So it sounds like the real issue is ___. Is that right?" |
   | **O**verview past pain | What they tried and why it failed | "What have you tried? How did that go? What did it cost?" |
   | **S**ell the vacation | Paint the outcome, not the process | "If this worked, what changes for you in 90 days?" |
   | **E**xplain away concerns | Handle objections (step 4) | |
   | **R**einforce | Lock in the decision, set next steps, cut buyer's remorse | "Here's exactly what happens next…" |
3. **Discovery questions:** open, calibrated "what" and "how" questions (negotiation-prep). Use labels ("It seems like…") and mirrors (repeat their last few words) to go deeper. Let them talk for most of the call.
4. **Objection bank:** group by root cause.
   - **Circumstances** (time, money, timing): reframe to the cost of inaction and the priority; offer payment terms only as a trade.
   - **Other people** (partner, boss): "What will they ask? How do you usually make decisions like this together?" Offer to equip them, or get the decision-maker on.
   - **Self-doubt** ("I've tried before", "will it work for me?"): use proof from similar customers, the guarantee, and what's different this time.
   - **Stall** ("I need to think about it"): "What specifically do you want to think through?" and solve it on the call.
   Treat objections as information. Write the 10 most common with a response, proof and a follow-up question each.
5. **Close:** ask directly, then stay silent. Offer a clear choice (tier A or B), not yes or no.
6. **Follow-up cadence** (Cardone's persistence principle): most sales happen after several touches. Draft a 5-8 touch sequence over 2-3 weeks that adds value each time, then a final "close the file" message.
7. **Practice:** role-play the script with the hardest objections before live calls, and review recordings weekly (talk-time ratio, questions asked, where calls stall).
8. **Log** every call: show, outcome, top objection, next step. Update the objection bank weekly.

## Output
Call script (CLOSER), qualification checklist, objection bank table (objection · root cause · response · proof · follow-up question), follow-up sequence drafts, call log template.

## Metric it moves
Close rate on qualified calls, show rate, and revenue per call; sales-cycle length.

## Re-measure
Weekly: show and close rate versus baseline across at least ~20 calls. Find the step where calls die (listen to 5 recordings) and change one script element at a time.
