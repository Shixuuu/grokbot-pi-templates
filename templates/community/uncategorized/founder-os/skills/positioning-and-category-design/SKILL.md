# Positioning and category design
Positioning is the context that makes your value obvious. If buyers lump you in with the wrong alternatives, they'll price you against them. Reason from the alternatives the customer actually considers, not from how you see yourself (April Dunford's positioning components; Play Bigger's category design).

## Inputs
- customer-interview-synthesis output (jobs, alternatives, verbatim phrases) or at least 5 recent won and lost deals.
- Current messaging: homepage, pitch, ad copy, offer name.
- Competitor and alternative list, including "do nothing", "hire someone", and spreadsheets.

## Steps
1. **Competitive alternatives:** what would your best customers do if you didn't exist? List them with real evidence from won and lost deals.
2. **Unique attributes:** capabilities only you have versus those alternatives (speed, guarantee, method, data, access, price model). Must be provable.
3. **Value:** translate each attribute into an outcome the buyer cares about (attribute → so that → measurable value). Drop attributes with no value.
4. **Best-fit customer:** the segment that cares most about that value. This is who gets the message, the ads and the sales time first.
5. **Market frame:** pick the context that makes the value obvious:
   - **Existing category, win it:** be the clear best for a named niche ("X for Y").
   - **Subcategory:** reframe an existing category around your strength.
   - **New category:** only when the problem is real but unnamed and you can afford to educate. Name the problem first, then the category, then your company as the answer.
6. **Contrarian edge (counter-positioning):** is there a move incumbents can't copy without hurting their own model (transparent cost-plus pricing, a performance guarantee, no contracts)? If yes, lead with it.
7. **Write the positioning statement and message hierarchy:**
   - For [best-fit customer] who [trigger/pain], [brand] is the [frame] that [key value], unlike [alternative], because [unique attribute + proof].
   - Headline, subhead, three proof points, objection killers, and one-line brand promise.
8. **Brand codes:** consistent name, promise, visual and verbal cues, and the founder's story (pitch prep). Draft only; design goes to the design tool.
9. **Test the message** with prove-or-kill-experiment: two to three positioning variants on the same traffic (ads, cold email or landing page), with a fixed sample.

## Output
Positioning canvas: alternatives · unique attributes · value · best-fit customer · market frame · contrarian edge · statement · message hierarchy · test plan.

## Metric it moves
Close rate, average price realized (less discounting), ad CTR and landing-page conversion, and share of inbound that's best-fit.

## Re-measure
After the test sample: win rate and price realized versus baseline. Re-check positioning each quarter, and whenever a new competitor or alternative shows up in more than ~20% of lost deals.
