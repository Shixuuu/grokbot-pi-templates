# Pricing and packaging
Price is the fastest profit lever: a price rise drops almost entirely to profit, and a higher price funds better delivery and better results (a virtuous cycle). Price off the value of the outcome and the buyer's alternatives, never off your cost.

## Inputs
- The offer stack (grand-slam-offer-design) and the value of the outcome to the buyer, in dollars or hours (estimated, labeled).
- Current price, close rate, discount rate, churn and gross margin (unit-economics). Mark missing ones Unknown.
- Competitor and alternative prices with dates (a sourced fact check).
- Willingness-to-pay signals from interviews and calls.

## Steps
1. **Value anchor:** estimate the annual value of the outcome to the buyer. A common target is a price at 10-20% of value delivered (a heuristic; justify it per market).
2. **Floor and ceiling:** the floor is fully loaded cost per customer plus the target gross margin (the contribution has to cover CAC fast). The ceiling is the value anchor and the next-best alternative. Price inside that band.
3. **Pricing model:** pick the metric that grows with the customer's value (per seat, per usage, per location, % of results, flat retainer). Prefer recurring when value recurs.
4. **Package:**
   - **Good/Better/Best:** three tiers built around who the buyer is, not feature dumping. The middle tier is the target. The top tier anchors and serves whales.
   - **Ascension path** (Hormozi money-model sequence): an attraction offer that gets customers in profitably, upsells at the moment of need, a downsell for those who say no, and continuity (recurring) to stretch LTV.
   - **Fences:** what separates the tiers (speed, access, done-for-you level, volume) so customers self-select without cannibalizing.
5. **Discount policy:** no ad-hoc discounts. Trade price only for something (annual prepay, case study, referral, longer term).
6. **Price-raise test:** raise for new customers first, on a fixed sample. Keep the gain if revenue per lead (close rate × price) rises, even if close rate dips. Grandfather or give notice to existing customers (drafts only).
7. **Model it:** revenue per lead, gross margin, payback and LTV at each candidate price (unit-economics). Pick the price that maximizes contribution per lead within capacity.
8. **Write the price page and sales-call price script** (drafts only), stating the price with confidence after value is established.

## Output
| Tier | Who it's for | Includes | Price | Value anchor | Gross margin | Expected mix | Fence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Plus: model chosen, ascension path, discount policy, test plan (sample, dates, keep/cut rule).

## Metric it moves
Revenue per qualified lead, gross margin %, and average revenue per account; secondarily LTV.

## Re-measure
After the test sample (e.g., 20-30 pitches or 30 days): close rate, price realized, revenue per lead and churn versus baseline. Review pricing at least yearly and after any big value upgrade.
