# Growth constraint finder
At any moment one constraint caps the whole business. Effort anywhere else is waste (Goldratt's Theory of Constraints: identify, exploit, subordinate, elevate, repeat). The constraint moves as the company grows, so re-find it at each stage instead of copying last year's plan.

## Inputs
- Current numbers: leads, conversion by step, capacity and delivery quality, churn and NRR, cash runway, team capacity, founder hours (unit-economics, funnel-and-conversion-audit, retention-churn-and-expansion, business-financial-model-and-runway).
- Revenue stage and growth trend.

## Steps
1. **Run the constraint checklist** in order. The first "no" is usually the constraint.
   | # | Question | If no, the constraint is | Go to |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 1 | Do people want it? Is retention flattening and pull organic? | Product/market | product-market-fit-measurement |
   | 2 | Do we get enough qualified leads to hit the goal? | Leads | core-four-lead-plan |
   | 3 | Do leads convert at a solid rate? | Conversion/offer | funnel-and-conversion-audit, grand-slam-offer-design |
   | 4 | Can we deliver more without quality dropping? | Capacity/delivery | SOPs and delegation, a structured hiring process |
   | 5 | Do customers stay and expand? | Retention | retention-churn-and-expansion |
   | 6 | Does each customer pay back fast enough to fund growth? | Unit economics/cash | unit-economics, a capital plan |
   | 7 | Do decisions and work flow without the founder? | Founder/people | SOPs and delegation, company-operating-system |
2. **Stage pattern check** (typical, not law):
   | Stage (annual revenue) | Usual constraint |
   |---|---|
   | $0-1M | Offer and leads; the founder sells everything |
   | $1-10M | Founder bandwidth, first managers, a second channel, delivery systems |
   | $10-100M | Leadership team, multiple channels and products, capital efficiency |
   | $100M-1B | Moat, new markets and products, M&A integration, org scale |
3. **Quantify it:** show the number that proves the constraint (e.g., "close rate 8% vs 20% target", "delivery backlog 3 weeks"). No number = gather it first.
4. **Exploit:** get more out of the constraint with what exists (remove waste at that step, prioritize best-fit leads, protect the bottleneck person's time).
5. **Subordinate:** pause projects that don't help the constraint. Write the stop list.
6. **Elevate:** if exploiting isn't enough, invest at the constraint (hire, tool, spend). Draft the proposal with the cost, expected lift and payback (lever sizing). The spend needs the owner's explicit OK.
7. **Set one 30-90 day target** for the constraint metric on the company scorecard (company-operating-system).
8. **Repeat:** once it's broken, the constraint moves. Re-run the checklist.

## Output
Constraint memo (one page): the constraint · the proving number · exploit moves · stop list · elevate proposal · target and date · what's likely next.

## Metric it moves
The constraint metric itself and, through it, revenue growth rate.

## Re-measure
Monthly re-run of the checklist; immediately when the constraint metric hits target or growth stalls for 2 months.

## Related skills

Walls (always): draft only, never send, post, spend, book, sign, or trade; no PII in shared places; never invent a number (mark it Unknown); keep employer systems and material out unless the owner opts in.
