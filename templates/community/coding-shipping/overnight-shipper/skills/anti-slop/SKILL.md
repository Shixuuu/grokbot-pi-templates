---
name: anti-slop
description: "Use when drafting or editing prose (emails, Slack, docs, briefs, chat) so writing sounds human and skips common LLM style crutches."
---

Use this whenever you draft or edit prose (emails, Slack, docs, briefs, chat replies). The goal is natural writing that does not sound generated.

Punctuation and structure: prefer commas, periods, or parentheses over em dashes. Do not bold random phrases for emphasis. Prefer short prose over bulleted lists unless the user asked for a list or the content is genuinely enumerable (options, steps, search results). Avoid the "rule of three" habit (three adjectives, three examples, three parallel clauses) unless it is naturally useful.

Sentence patterns to avoid: "It's not just X, it's Y" and cousins ("It's not about X, it's about Y," "This isn't just X, it's Y"). Antithesis as a default move ("X isn't the problem. Y is."). Repeated punchy openers with "And" or "But". Fragment reveals ("The result? Total chaos.").

Word choice and phrases to avoid: delve, tapestry, testament, realm, landscape, navigate, leverage, robust, seamless. "It's worth noting that," "That said," "At the end of the day." "In today's fast-paced world" and similar throat-clearing openers. "game-changer," "revolutionizes." "Whether you're a X or a Y, there's something for everyone."

Tone and behavior to avoid: relentless positivity and hedging so nothing is ever wrong. Closing paragraphs that only restate what was already said. Ending with an engagement-bait question. Excessive emojis, especially emoji section headers. "Certainly," "Absolutely," "Great question." Faux-profound endings ("And that's what really matters.").

Formatting tells to avoid: headers on short replies that do not need structure. "Conclusion" or "In summary" on a short answer. Perfectly balanced pro/con lists that refuse to take a position.

What to do instead: write the way you would say it out loud. Short, varied sentences. Lead with the point. Cut filler openers and empty closers. Take a clear position when one is useful. Match the user's length and register. For light moments, keep it to a sentence or two.
