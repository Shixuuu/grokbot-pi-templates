# Flip check

Goal: a verdict (flip or pass), a list price, and a max buy price, all grounded in real SOLD data.

## Steps
1. **Identify the item exactly.** Brand, model, variant, size or capacity, and color. From a photo, read labels and model numbers; ask only if a detail that changes price is truly unknown.
2. **Note condition and completeness.** New sealed, open box, used, or for parts; box, manuals, accessories, cables. Price against comps in the same condition.
3. **Pull SOLD comps, never asking prices.** eBay sold/completed listings (Sold Items filter, last 30 to 90 days) first; if eBay blocks access, use sites that record completed eBay sales (such as PriceCharting for games and consoles) or other marketplaces with visible sold history. Collect 5 to 15 matching sales. Drop outliers such as lots, bundles, parts-only, different colors or variants, or mislabeled items. Record the median sold price and the typical range, and whether each sale's price included shipping.
4. **Estimate costs.**
   - Marketplace fee: about 13 to 15% of the total the buyer pays on eBay, plus any per-order fee (use the owner's real rate if saved in memory).
   - Shipping: a real estimate from weight and size for the carrier the owner uses, plus packing materials.
   - Supplies and any other per-item cost the owner saved.
5. **Apply the fee-safe free-shipping rule.** If the owner offers free shipping, the list price is about the median sold price plus the real shipping cost, so the fee is charged on the full amount and the shipping still gets paid.
6. **Do the math.**
   - Net payout = list price minus fees minus shipping minus supplies.
   - Max buy = net payout minus the owner's target profit per item.
   - Also check a weaker sale at the low end of the typical range.
   - Verdict: flip if the asking price is at or under max buy and the item sells regularly (several sales a month); otherwise pass, or "flip only at $X".
7. **Report briefly.** Verdict first, then list price, max buy, expected profit at the asking price (if given), the number of comps and their date range, and one line on risks (slow seller, auctions dragging prices, condition sensitivity, fakes).

## Rules
- Never invent prices, fees, or sales counts. If sold data is thin or unavailable, say so plainly and give a cautious range or ask for more detail instead of guessing.
- Use the owner's saved marketplaces, target profit, shipping policy, and categories when they exist in memory.
