# Local leads workflow

1. Find. Look in the sources the owner picked: bid invites in email, public bid sites, and LinkedIn, X, and Facebook posts. Stay inside the owner's region and trade.
2. Qualify. Every lead must pass all three checks: they buy what the owner sells; a real decision-maker is named (owner, estimator, PM, or purchasing); and they are in the region or clearly buy from it. Skip trade publications, recruiters, competing suppliers, and job seekers. Owning trade-specific equipment is a strong sign someone buys materials.
3. Screen. Before proposing anyone, check the company name, email, and domain against every customer source the owner has (customer export, CRM, and email marketing audience). Strip stray spaces from the emails before comparing. Any match means existing customer, so don't cold-pitch them. Mark them existing_customer, and flag past customers who owe money as do-not-contact. Suggest the owner refresh old exports about once a month.
4. Find a real email. Use the company website, bid-document signatures, or public listings. Do not scrape private profile data or use paid lists.
5. Log. Add each lead to the lead list (Contacts, BidWatch, Activity) with its source, status, and why it qualifies. Back the file up before every write. Use these bid stages: watching, soft_touch_sent, awarded, pricing_sent, won, lost, dead.
6. Draft. While a job is out to bid, write a short intro that leads with something useful, not a sales pitch. Send hard pricing only after the job is awarded, and aim it at the winning contractor. Name the business only when the prospect shows buying intent.
7. Hand off. Give the owner editable drafts to send. Never send, post, reply, DM, or connect on their behalf without explicit approval for that exact message. On social sites, read only.

Rules: never invent leads; never buy paid seats; never retry-flood a failed post; never paste keys or tokens; stay quiet on scheduled runs when nothing new turned up. If you run a separate lead bot on another computer, treat its output as intake to screen, not as qualified leads.
