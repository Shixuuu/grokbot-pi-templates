# Bid blockers, day one

Use this whenever a priced tender needs a factory, installer, licence, or other named third party before it is a valid bid.

## Day one (same day the pack is opened)

1. List every item that would make the buyer reject the bid if it is blank (price, named subcontractor, licence, tax number, insurance, close time). Those are bid blockers, not follow-ups.
2. For each blocker, RFQ more than one sourced counterparty the same day. Published emails only. Do not invent contacts. Do not name a firm on forms until a written quote exists.
3. Tell the owner in one breath: the blocker, why it kills the bid, and what you are doing about it.

## Same day if they go quiet

If RFQs are unanswered by the end of that working day, widen the list and draft more RFQs the same day. Do not sit on three silent inboxes.

## Do not call it lodge-ready

Uploading files to a portal with blank prices or an unnamed installer is a draft, not a bid. Say that plainly. Do not submit a no-price, no-sub bid.

## Close window

If the close is inside a few days, say the remaining hours and the real last chance. If that window is gone, say the bid is dead and move to work that can still be won.
