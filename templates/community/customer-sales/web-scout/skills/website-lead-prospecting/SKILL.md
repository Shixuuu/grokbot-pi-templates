# Website lead prospecting

Find local businesses that need a better website, confirm the problem by looking at it in a real browser, and draft a friendly cold email for the user to review and send.

## Before searching
Use what's saved in memory about the user's web design business: service area (city and radius), services and price range to pitch, business types to target or skip, and sign-off. If anything is missing, browse the user's own site first and ask only what it doesn't answer. Save answers to memory so you never re-ask.

## What counts as a lead (one or more)
- No website at all (a Google Business or Facebook page only, or a dead or parked domain).
- A basic free-builder site on a platform subdomain or with builder branding showing (Wix, Weebly, GoDaddy Builder, Squarespace or Google Sites defaults).
- An obviously dated site: not mobile friendly, a years-old copyright year, Flash or table layouts, broken images, no HTTPS.
- Visible typos, broken links, lorem ipsum, or other errors a visitor would actually see.

## Find and check
1. Use web search and map listings to build a candidate list in the service area. Skip chains, franchises, and the user's existing clients.
2. Check every candidate with the visual-site-audit skill on desktop and at a phone-sized width. A problem only counts if you saw it on the rendered page. Never judge from fetched HTML or page source.
3. Screenshot each confirmed issue and keep it with the lead. Drop any candidate you can't confirm visually, or that is closed or already has a newer site on its own domain.
4. Find a public contact email on the business's own site or listing; if there isn't one, note the phone number or contact form instead.

## Drafting
Write each email in a warm, helpful, neighborly voice. Introduce the user as a local designer and developer, name the specific issues you saw in plain words (kindly, never insulting), explain briefly why a professional site matters for that kind of business (trust, mobile customers, showing up in search), and close with a short, compelling offer and an easy next step. Keep it short, personal to that business, and free of hype. When Gmail is connected and the user asks, save each one with an email address as a Gmail draft to that address; otherwise give the plain text with recipient and subject.

## Report
A short list per lead: business, town, URL, what you saw, the screenshot, the contact, and where the draft is. If a search turns up nothing worth pitching, say so in one line.

## Never
- Send an email, submit a contact form, call, message, or reach out to any business in any way; the user sends.
- Invent an issue, a contact address, or a business detail.
- Pitch businesses outside the service area or ones the user said to skip.
- Redesign sites, write code, or do other writing.
