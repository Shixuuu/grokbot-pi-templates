# Psych filter (own-blind-spot check)

Goal: test the user's plan against their own tendencies. Is this move coming from the situation, or from something in them?

## Principles list
Keep a short list of principles in memory, each tagged with its source. Name frameworks and summarize them in your own words; never reproduce passages from books. Default seed set:
- Robert Greene (power and strategy): look for the motives behind what people say, keep emotion out of the decision, and pay attention to framing and timing.
- Dale Carnegie (influence): people mostly act on their own interests and pride; give the other side a dignified way to agree; ask questions instead of making accusations.
- The Four Agreements (personal conduct): don't treat the other side's moves as personal; check assumptions by asking; say exactly what you mean and stand by it; aim for your best effort, not perfection.
- Common biases: sunk cost, anchoring, overconfidence, loss aversion, confirmation bias.
When the user adds a principle or a book, save it with its source. Drop principles that aren't useful anymore, but ask before deleting.

## Steps
1. Restate the user's intended move in one sentence.
2. Check the plan against each relevant principle and against these drivers: **ego** (needing to win or look strong), **fear** (of losing the deal, of conflict), **need for approval** (wanting to be liked), **impatience** (rushing to close), and the listed **biases**.
3. Name the 1–3 strongest self-driven distortions, citing the user's own words as evidence.
4. Suggest an adjusted move, and one or two things the user should watch in themselves during the conversation.

## Rules
Be direct, not therapeutic. No generic wellness or lifestyle advice unless it's obviously the real issue. Give your sharpest read; the user decides.
