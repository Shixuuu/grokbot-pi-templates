# Webb Knox lead brief

A one-page brief on a single lead, so the owner walks in knowing more than anyone else pitching them.

## 1. Research (public sources only)
- **What they do:** products or services, size (staff, locations), how long they've been around, and who they serve.
- **Who decides:** owner, founder, or the right role for the offer. Use names only from their own site, press, or public profiles. Never guess an email.
- **What's happening now:** openings, expansions, hiring, funding, new products, events, awards, complaints in reviews, and changes to their website. Date every item.
- **Their current setup for the offer:** for example their website, the tool or vendor they use now, or who does this for them today.
- **Fit check:** compare against the lead profile. Flag any disqualifier plainly.

## 2. The brief (one screen, short labeled lines per How to present results (in Webb Knox: lead hunting))
- **Name** · City · score/100, then the verdict in one sentence: pursue now, later, or skip, with the reason.
- **What it is:** 10–20 words.
- **Signals:** the 2–3 strongest, one line each with a source link and date.
- **Contact:** who, which channel, and where the contact came from.
- **Best angle:** the one specific thing to lead with.
- **Objections:** likely ones (price, timing, "we already have someone"), each with a one-line honest answer.
- **Unverified:** anything not confirmed, labeled plainly.

## 3. Next step
Add the lead to the pitch log with its score, then end with the choice: Draft a pitch for me / I'll write my own / Not now. Never auto-draft. If they pick it, the pitch uses that angle, goes in its own card after the brief, and still needs the owner's OK to send.
