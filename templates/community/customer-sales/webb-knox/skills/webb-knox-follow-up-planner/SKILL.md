# Webb Knox follow-up planner

Most replies come from a follow-up, not the first message. Plan them so nobody is forgotten and nobody is pestered.

## 1. The plan (per pitched lead)
- **Follow-up 1:** about 7 days after the pitch. It's short and adds one new, useful thing (a relevant idea, a quick example, or a fresh signal you spotted). Never just "bumping this."
- **Follow-up 2:** about 14 days after follow-up 1, through a different channel if the owner has one (phone or DM instead of email). One line, easy to answer.
- **Close-out:** about 14 days later: "I'll stop reaching out, but here's how to find me if the timing changes." Then mark the lead lost (no reply) in the pitch log.
- Stop the plan at once for any reply, bounce, or "not interested," and mark do-not-contact for any "stop." Never exceed 3 touches in total after the pitch.
- Owners can change the timing (for example faster for a B2B team, slower for small shops). Save their preference in the lead profile.

## 2. Track it
Add to each lead in the pitch log: the next follow-up date, which step it's on, and the channel. The reply-watch routine checks these dates.

## 3. When one is due
- Batch due follow-ups: one line to the owner ("3 follow-ups are due today") with the choice Draft follow-ups for me (then a multi-select of which) / I'll write my own / Not now. Not one ping each.
- Draft only the ones picked, per the email draft rules in How to present results (in Webb Knox: lead hunting): 40–70 words, refer back to the original pitch, add the one new thing, soft opt-out, full-name-plus-phone signature, no day job or employer. Reply in the same thread (Subject "Re: ..." in the To/Subject block).
- Each draft is its own card after that message, with Send / Edit / Skip options, in the owner's saved voice.
- Send only after the owner OKs that exact message (or presses Send on the card), and count it toward the 10-a-day send cap.
