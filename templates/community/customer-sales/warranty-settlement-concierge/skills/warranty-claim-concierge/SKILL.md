# Warranty claim concierge

Run a warranty claim from "this broke" to "replacement in hand." Never send, submit, or sign anything to a company without the owner's explicit OK.

## 1. Intake
Get the product (brand, model, color/size), where and when it was bought, the order number or receipt, and what's wrong. Ask for photos of the defect and the proof of purchase if they exist. Check the owner's email (if connected) for the order confirmation and any earlier claim threads before asking them.

## 2. Coverage check
Find the manufacturer's official warranty page and read the terms: length, what's covered (defects vs damage vs wear), whether the purchase needs to be from an authorized seller, registration requirements, and shipping costs. Also check card-issuer extended warranty and retailer return windows as backup paths. Tell the owner plainly: covered, probably covered, or not covered, and the best route.

## 3. Prepare the claim
Find the official claim form or support channel. Draft every answer (description of defect, purchase details, shipping address) and list the attachments. Show the draft to the owner and wait for approval. Fill the form only after approval; stop before any payment, signature, or final submit the owner hasn't confirmed.

## 4. Track it
Record in memory: brand, product, claim/RMA number, date filed, what was promised, and any return shipping label or deadline. Remind the owner of anything they must do (ship the old item, register, upload a photo). When a replacement tracking number arrives, create a temporary routine that checks the carrier every few hours and pings the owner only when the status changes (shipped, out for delivery, delivered, exception).

## 5. Close out
When the carrier or the brand confirms delivery, tell the owner, delete the tracking routine, and mark the claim closed in memory. If a claim stalls past the brand's promised timeline, draft a polite follow-up for the owner to approve.

## Rules
- Draft first; the owner presses send.
- Never invent warranty terms. Quote the real policy page.
- Keep one line per open claim in memory so a status question can be answered instantly.
