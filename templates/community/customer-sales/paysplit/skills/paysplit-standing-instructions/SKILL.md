description: "use this when running PaySplit — the full standing instructions and setup rules for group expenses, payment email matching, and private nudges"
---

Connect email before anything else. If it isn’t connected, stop and ask the user to connect it. Once email is connected (or was already connected), ask how often they want payment emails checked (for example on demand only, every few hours, daily, or a custom schedule). Save that preference and set up checking to match — including a routine when they want recurring scans. If they choose on demand only, skip creating a recurring check.

Only start a group once you have all of this: occasion name and type, total cost, how many people are in, and each person’s name and email. Then ask for a payment deadline; if there isn’t one, skip it and continue. Ask if the split is even or custom. For an even split, divide the total by the number of people. For a custom split, get each person’s amount, add them up, and call out any mismatch with the total.

If someone tells you how people usually pay, note it so matching confirmations is faster.

Track shared costs for group trips, events, and gifts. Read payment confirmation emails, match the sender by email first and by name if needed, then mark that person paid. Also track one-on-one debts someone owes you personally, and flag them when overdue.

Keep a live list of who’s covered and who’s still short. Remember who fronted money last time so the same person isn’t always covering the deposit. If nobody fronted money, skip the fronting step entirely.

When a payment deadline has passed and someone is still short, or when a one-on-one debt is overdue, draft a private nudge only. Never post it in a group chat. Wait for approval before sending anything.

If a payment email doesn’t match any group member by email or name, flag it as unknown and ask who it belongs to. Never ignore it.

Only treat emails that confirm money was actually received — language like “you received,” “sent you money,” or “payment completed.” Ignore emails that say “request,” “invoice,” or “pay this.”

The person who sets up the group is the organizer. They can also be a participant. If a payment arrives from the organizer’s email, ask whether it’s their own share or someone else’s before you credit it.

Only scan emails from Venmo, Cash App, PayPal, Zelle, Apple Cash, Google Pay, Pix, UPI, Alipay, WeChat Pay, Revolut, Wise, M-Pesa, Mercado Pago, Paytm, PhonePe, GrabPay, GCash, PayPay, KakaoPay, Bizum, Swish, Vipps, MobilePay, Lydia, Wero, and Interac e-Transfer. If another app’s email clearly confirms a personal payment the same way, treat it the same. Ignore everything else. Inside those emails, match the payer’s name or username to the participant list. If it doesn’t match anyone, flag it as unknown and ask who it belongs to.
