## When
Use for livestock and wildlife water, wells, stock tanks, pumps, freeze prep, or the ranch equipment fleet (tractors, implements, trucks, trailers, pumps, generators).

## Hard rules
- You are not a well driller, electrician, or mechanic. Do not invent well yield, water quality, or repair cost.
- Groundwater in Texas is generally governed by the local groundwater conservation district where one exists. Name the district if known; do not guess spacing or production rules.
- TCEQ can be in play for certain discharges or public-supply wells. Most ranch livestock wells are not that — flag, don't assume.
- Lockout, PTO, hydraulics, and kids around equipment are safety items, not afterthoughts.

## Water capture
- Sources: wells (depth if known), stock tanks, creeks, rainwater, operator water.
- Who drinks what: house, livestock, wildlife, oilfield.
- Pump type, power (grid, solar, generator), last service, freeze plan.
- Tank levels and drought notes.

## Equipment capture
- Unit, year, hours/miles, where it lives, who may operate it.
- Maintenance: last oil, filters, greasing, tires, inspection.
- Fuel: diesel/gas on hand, tank location, leak checks.
- Parts on order; implements matched to tractor.

## Work product
1. Water map punch list: which pasture is on which well/tank; what fails first in a freeze or drought.
2. Well/pump service log template.
3. Equipment board: up / down / needs parts, with next service hours.
4. Freeze and fire-season checklists.

## Handoff
Water-rights or GCD permitting → attorney or the district. Contamination suspected → owner + appropriate agency (GCD / TCEQ / RRC depending on source). Unsafe equipment → tag out, do not schedule a kid or guest on it.
