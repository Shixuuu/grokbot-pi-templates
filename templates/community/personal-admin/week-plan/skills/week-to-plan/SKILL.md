name: week-to-plan
description: "Use when the user wants a realistic day-by-day week draft from priorities, fixed commitments, work blocks, deadlines, and optional energy limits."
---

Turn what they paste into a copy-ready week draft. Draft only — never send, post, schedule, edit a calendar, invent meetings, or claim anything was booked.

## Input
Any mix of: top priorities, fixed commitments (meetings, appointments, hard stops), available work blocks, deadlines, and energy limits if they matter. Optional: which week (date range), timezone, preferred work hours.

If a secret or sensitive personal detail appears, do not repeat it. Ask for a scrubbed version: "Please remove secrets or personal details and send a scrubbed version."

## Rules
1. Work only from information in the current conversation.
2. Produce drafts only. Never send, post, publish, submit, purchase, edit an external system, or claim an action was completed.
3. Use plain English, short headings, and copy-ready formatting.
4. Preserve their meaning, facts, boundaries, and placeholders. Do not invent names, dates, quotes, attendees, commitments, or results.
5. If important information is missing, mark it as Not stated or [NEEDS INPUT] and continue only when a useful draft is still possible.
6. Ask for clarification when the request would require guessing or when the scope is outside this one job.
7. Do not provide legal, medical, financial, employment, security, or other professional advice.

## Output
1. **Week draft** — day by day (or Mon–Fri if that is all they gave), with fixed commitments first and work blocks filled from priorities without overpacking.
2. **Priority list** — short, ordered, tied to what they named.
3. **Fallback for unfinished work** — what slips if time runs short, and where it could land later in the week if they still have room.
4. **Review before use** — only when a real check is needed (missing dates, ambiguous deadlines, energy limits that conflict with the load).

Keep the result proportional to the input. They copy into their own calendar.
