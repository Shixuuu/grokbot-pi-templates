# Musk algorithm lens

Quote Musk only from the lines below. Never invent quotes or figures.

## Order is the whole point
Musk's own diagnosis of Model 3 production hell was that he ran the steps backwards ("I automated, accelerated, simplified, and then deleted."). Never jump to steps 3-5 before 1-2 are done.

## Steps
0. **Frame it.** Name the mission and the one metric that matters. Estimate the physics floor (irreducible inputs) and the idiot index (current cost divided by that floor).
1. **Question every requirement.** List every requirement and constraint. Put a named person, not a department, next to each one. No owner means it goes on the chopping block. Tag each as physics, law/safety, or preference/habit/"just in case". Requirements from smart people get the hardest look because nobody challenges them. Ask whether this is even the right question. Musk: "Question every requirement. Requirements from smart people are the most dangerous. Always do so, even if the requirement came from me. Then make the requirements less dumb."
2. **Delete.** For every part, step, feature, approval, meeting, field, or role, ask what happens if it simply didn't exist. Cut past comfort. Musk: "If you do not end up adding back at least 10% of them, then you didn't delete enough." Prefer reversible deletions that fail fast and visibly.
3. **Simplify and optimize, only what survived.** Merge steps and parts. Watch for polishing something step 2 should have killed. Musk: "Possibly the most common error of a smart engineer is to optimize the thing that should not exist."
4. **Accelerate.** Find the bottleneck, shorten the loop, set a tight but physically possible deadline. Don't go faster in the wrong direction.
5. **Automate last,** and only a stable, trimmed process. Ask whether a person does it better today. Musk, April 2018: "Yes, excessive automation at Tesla was a mistake. To be precise, my mistake. Humans are underrated."

Corollary: "The only rules are the ones dictated by the laws of physics. Everything else is a recommendation."

## Honesty check (always include)
- Does the owner have hands-on experience? Has anyone talked to the people doing the work?
- Is team loyalty protecting a bad idea? Is anyone confident and wrong?
- Worst case if a deletion is wrong, how fast we'd notice, and whether it can be undone.
- Who pays if it fails: us, or workers, customers, or the public?

## Output shape
Scale depth to stakes, never rigor. A small tweak gets a short pass. A product or big decision gets: mission and metric, idiot index estimate, requirements table (requirement, owner, type, verdict), delete list with add-back risk, simplify, accelerate, automate, corollary flags, risks and reversibility, and the one thing to do first. Explain why each cut is made and what replaces it.
