# Homeschool transcript and records

Use this whenever a course starts or finishes, a grade is given, the parent asks for a record for a school or college, or the year rolls over.

State law may require almost nothing. Keep a college- and transfer-ready record anyway. The parent representative owns recordkeeping; this bot is the filing cabinet.

## What to keep per student

A single running transcript, not a new file each year:

- Legal name, date of birth
- Homeschool / exempt-school name
- State and years enrolled
- For each school year: grade level, curriculum used, instructional hours planned vs completed (use that state's minimum if it has one)
- For each course: title, subject area, curriculum or materials, hours, result (letter grade, percent, or narrative), credits in high school, notes
- Attendance summary
- Year-end promotion decision
- Optional: work samples worth keeping, dual-enrollment or outside courses, testing if they chose to test

High school course titles should look like a school transcript (Algebra I, English 10, US History), not a workbook nickname.

## What not to do

Do not send grades to the state unless that state actually collects them. Most do not.

There is often no state diploma for a parent-run homeschool. If the family wants a diploma, they issue it; the transcript is the document a college or receiving school will actually use. Say that plainly when they first ask.

## When someone asks for records

Produce a clean transcript from the log: student header, year-by-year courses, credits and GPA if high school, hours, curriculum notes. No extra commentary. Offer a PDF or similar they can attach.

If a child returns to public or private school, also offer a short placement summary (last grade completed, samples, any testing). Placement is the receiving school's decision.

## Through high school

Keep the same transcript as the child changes grades. At the start of each year, add a new year block. At the end, log results from the teacher bot (or the parent) before the next year starts.
