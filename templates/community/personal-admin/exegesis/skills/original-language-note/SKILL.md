# Original-language note

Use the original-language note only to show what a word or form in the source text is, when that changes or supports the meaning or grade. Keep it short.

1. **Open the original text** (public domain only):
   - Hebrew Old Testament: the Westminster Leningrad Codex, from the Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible, `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openscriptures/morphhb/master/wlc/<Book>.xml` (for example `Jer.xml`). Each word carries a Strong's number (`lemma`) and a morphology code (`morph`). For example, a suffix `Sp2mp` means "your/you", second person masculine plural. The morphology tagging is CC BY 4.0. Credit "Open Scriptures Hebrew Bible" when you use it.
   - Greek New Testament: the Textus Receptus, the Greek text behind the KJV, at `https://ebible.org/Scriptures/grctr_vpl.zip` (same `BOOK C:V text` layout as the English files). Other Greek editions, including the one the WEB translators used, differ at some points. The WEB's publisher says its New Testament follows the Greek Majority Text. If the point depends on a word where editions may differ, say so and don't rest it on the TR alone. To compare, you can open:
     - a Byzantine or Majority text: Robinson and Pierpont's 2018 Byzantine Textform, freely copyable, at `https://ebible.org/Scriptures/grctcgnt_vpl.zip`;
     - a critical text: the SBL Greek New Testament, CC BY 4.0 (credit "SBL Greek New Testament, Society of Biblical Literature"), at `https://ebible.org/Scriptures/grcsbl_vpl.zip`.
     Describe any difference by edition family (the Textus Receptus, the Byzantine or Majority text, critical editions), neutrally, and don't prefer one. When a KJV and WEB difference comes from this (for example, 1 John 5:7), say plainly that they rest on different manuscript traditions. Some editions also mark whole passages as disputed; for example, the SBL edition prints John 7:53–8:11 in double brackets. Note that neutrally when it bears on the passage.
2. **Look up the word** in Strong's. The 1890 text is public domain. Use `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/openscriptures/strongs/master/greek/strongs-greek-dictionary.js` or `.../hebrew/strongs-hebrew-dictionary.js`, keyed by number (such as `G5365` or `H7965`). Credit it as "Strong's (1890), via Open Scriptures (Open Scriptures edition, CC BY-SA)". Never quote modern licensed lexicons or study-Bible notes. You may name them.
3. **Write at most 60 words:** the word, its transliteration, its Strong's number, the short definition, and why it matters. Say what the text shows, for example "no article on 'root'" or "'you' is plural". Don't build a theory on it.
4. **Guards:**
   - A word's root or etymology isn't its meaning in context. Usage in the passage decides.
   - Don't claim manuscript differences unless you opened a source that shows them.
   - If traditions translate a word differently for doctrinal reasons, name the readings neutrally.
   - If you can't open the sources, leave the note out and say so. Never fill it from memory.
