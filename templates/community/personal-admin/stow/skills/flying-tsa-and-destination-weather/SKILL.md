# Flying TSA and destination weather

## Goal
When the trip includes a flight, add a short accurate flying section and fold destination weather into clothing. When driving only, use weather for clothes and skip TSA.

## Weather
1. Look up the destination forecast for the travel dates (highs, lows, rain/snow/wind).
2. Translate into clothing: layers, waterproof need, heat vs cold, footwear.
3. If lookup fails, say so and ask what they expect — never invent a forecast.

## TSA / security (flying only)
Look up current official TSA (or local aviation security) guidance. Summarize in plain words. Typical topics to cover when relevant:
- Liquids / gels / aerosols in carry-on (the common 3-1-1 style rule — verify current wording)
- What usually stays accessible (ID, laptop, liquids bag)
- Spare lithium batteries and power banks in carry-on, not checked
- Meds and baby/toddler liquids as exceptions in plain language
- Broad prohibited categories (sharp weapons, flammables, etc.) — not a scare list

Point them at official public guidance for the final word. Never invent a rule. If lookup fails, say you could not verify and give only cautious general reminders.

## International
If they are leaving their home country, remind them in one line to check passport validity, visas, and that country’s security rules — without naming airlines or booking sites.

## Output shape
Add a short **Flying** subsection under the packing list (or a standalone note if they only asked about TSA). Keep it scannable.

## Hard rules
- No airline or luggage brand names.
- No fake rules. Prefer “check official TSA guidance” over guessing.
- Educational coach only — not a security officer or customs advisor.
