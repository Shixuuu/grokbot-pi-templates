# Build a trip packing checklist

## Goal
Turn what you know about the trip into a clear, checkable packing list. Match length, activities, and bag limits. Prefer fewer items done well over packing “just in case” clutter unless they asked for extras.

## Before listing
Confirm you know: destination, approximate dates/length, trip type, flying vs driving, and top activities. If a key piece is missing, ask one question — then list.

## Weather for clothes
Look up the destination forecast for their dates when you can. Use highs/lows and rain/snow to choose layers. Never invent temperatures. If lookup fails, say so and ask what climate they expect.

## Checklist structure (use only relevant sections)
1. **Documents & money** — ID/passport if needed, tickets/confirmations (category only), cards, cash, insurance info, emergency contacts
2. **Tech** — phone, chargers, adapters by region, headphones, power bank, laptop if work
3. **Clothes** — by activity and forecast; estimate days + 1; include sleepwear, underwear, socks, one nicer outfit if the trip needs it
4. **Toiletries** — travel sizes; note liquids bag if flying
5. **Meds & health** — prescriptions, basics, any mobility aids they named
6. **Activity gear** — only what the activities need (swim, hike, gym, beach, formal)
7. **Kids / companion** — only if relevant
8. **Bag plan** — personal item / carry-on / checked (or car trunk) based on what they said
9. **Day-before skim** — 5–8 lines they can check the night before

Mark **must** vs **optional** where it helps. Keep language plain.

## Driving trips
Skip TSA section. Add car items only if useful (charger, snacks, roadside basics as categories — no brand names).

## After the list
Offer one tweak pass: “Want this carry-on only?” or “Add a formal night?” Do not nag.

## Hard rules
- No airline, hotel, booking-app, or luggage brand names.
- Do not invent weather.
- Do not overpack them; ask before adding specialty gear they did not mention.
