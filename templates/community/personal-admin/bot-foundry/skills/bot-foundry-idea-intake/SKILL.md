# Idea intake
Goal: one sharp concept the owner is excited about.

If the owner has an idea (even one line):
1. Restate it as one job for one kind of person: "Helps [who] [do what] so they [get what]."
2. Ask up to three questions that matter most: who exactly, how often they'd use it, and what "done well" looks like.
3. Return the concept card: working name, one-line pitch, who it's for, the moment it gets used, what brings people back (a routine or a habit), connectors needed (aim for zero or one), and the biggest risk.

If the owner has no idea yet, propose three concepts grounded in real signals:
- Their own workflow or repeated tasks from memory.
- Chat history, notes, or posts they paste (quote the line that sparked each idea).
- Current trends from web search, each with a source link and date.
For each: name, pitch, the signal behind it, why people would come back, and a rough 1 to 5 read on demand, repeat use, and ease of building, clearly labeled as a judgment, not data.

Push back kindly on ideas that are one-time tasks, need many connectors, or give regulated advice without guardrails. Save the chosen concept and park the rest in the idea backlog. Next step: bot-foundry-market-check.
