# Onboarding

Goal: an honest starting record in one relaxed conversation (about 15 minutes). You write `profile`, `days` and `planned_trips` in `~/nomad-pro-data/daylog.json` (schema `~/nomad-pro-engine/schema/daylog.schema.json`). Run `engine-setup` first if `~/nomad-pro-engine` is missing (mention only if it fails). Follow `nomad-pro-core-rules`.

Ask **one question at a time**; accept "not sure" and "skip" (come back later).

**Calendar offer (right after the intro, before the history questions).** An offer, never required. Send it as it stands:

> Connect your calendar and I do the logging.
> You: keep your calendar up to date.
> Me: read it, propose each day and check it with you.
> Year end: your job is mostly checking it's right.
>
> Add Gmail and I attach your flight and hotel confirmations to each day as its records. Both are optional and read-only. Prefer not to? The daily check-in works fine without them.

Note their answer; connect at step 9 (or now if they'd like). If they decline, carry on: nothing else changes.

1. **Name and eligibility.** What to call them. Confirm British citizenship and nomad life (now or planned); passports (drive stay limits), each passport's expiry date, and any visas or residence permits they hold with expiry dates (stored in `profile.documents_expiry` as `{type, country, label, expires_on, added_at}`; `travel-rules-watch` reminds them 6 and 3 months ahead); home timezone. Not British: explain the template is built around UK rules and stop politely.
2. **Departure.** When they left the UK (or plan to); a possible split year is recorded as a claim only (RFIG21000).
3. **Tax years to track** (6 April–5 April), and for each of the 3 years before the first: recorded as UK resident (yes/no/unsure) and roughly how many UK days (or "more than 90: yes/no/unsure"). Why: Table A/B uses the previous 3 years (RDR3), the 90-day tie the previous 2 (RFIG20570). Store in `profile.prior_years` and `tax_years[*].uk_resident_recorded`. Never infer a status.
4. **UK ties, one at a time, citing each page:** family (RFIG20530: UK-resident spouse, civil partner or partner living together as if married; UK-resident under-18 children seen in the UK, 61-day figure); accommodation (RFIG20550, RFIG22170: every UK place they could stay, as a label, with relationship (own, rented, close relative, other relative, friend, hotel) and whether available for a continuous 91 days; explain 1 night vs 16 nights; create `accommodation_register` entries); work (RFIG20560: any UK work over 3 hours a day; work has its everyday meaning, RFIG20740; RFIG21930 lists emails and meetings); 90-day (from step 3); country (RFIG20580: explained only, the log computes it). Save in `tax_years[YYYY/YY].tie_answers` with `tie_answers_reviewed_at`. Their answer is kept even if the log later differs; flag differences, never overwrite. Also ask: is a UK home their only home (RFIG20330), and do they work full-time in the UK (RFIG20370).
5. **Employment:** employed / self-employed / contractor / none, dates, and any claim of full-time work overseas (RFIG20140), recorded as their claim. If they claim full-time overseas work, ask for the contract and file it via `evidence-and-documents` (or record `requested`).
6. **Work-day rule: their own, never a default.** One short question each, recorded exactly (work / non-work / ask me each time): job periods covered and no-job periods; UK weekdays; weekends; UK bank holidays; travel days into or out of the UK; **travelling for work** (flying in for a work event or client meeting): ask each time / work day / follow the travel-day setting (`work_travel`), and the calendar words that mark a work trip (`work_travel_keywords`); calendar exception keywords and meanings (e.g. `sick` = non-work, `less than 3 hours`), or none; how "more than 3 hours" is decided. If asked whether travel counts as work, answer from `srt-explainer` (RFIG20740, RFIG20750) without a verdict. An example may show the shape only, never as a preset. Tell them their own answer for any day **always takes priority over the rule**. Read the whole rule back in their words, including that sentence, ask "Is that right?", and save only when confirmed and complete: `profile.work_day_rules` entry `v1` with `effective_from`/`effective_to`, `job_periods`, `no_job_periods`, `weekdays`, `weekends`, `public_holidays`, `travel_days`, `work_travel`, `work_travel_keywords`, `exceptions` (`[]` if none), `over_3h_basis`, `user_answers_take_priority: true`, `agreed_at`, `agreed_via: "agreed with user in chat"`, `wording_shown`. Applying it to earlier days needs their OK (`applies_to_earlier_days_agreed: true`). It's kept with their records for any HMRC enquiry and can change any time.
7. **Record sources and documents:** where their records sit (email bookings, calendar, bank apps, maps timeline, photos, tickets); explain the Evidence column (link, stored file or pointer). Offer to file status documents they hold (contract, tenancy end, P85 acknowledgement).
8. **Check-in rhythm:** daily (default) at a time they choose, weekly, or "when I move"; confirm their timezone.
9. **Connections (optional, read-only, nothing is sent).** If they took up the calendar offer, or want to now: Google Calendar (a source of evidence; I propose days for you to confirm, plus a short calendar review with you) and Gmail (links to booking and ticket emails for the Evidence column; screenshots are stored in `evidence/`). If they decline, the daily check-in covers everything.
   - **Calendar rules (calendar connected).** Agree how you read their calendar. Propose these defaults in one short message, for them to edit:
     * **Stays:** an all-day event for each stay with the city and country in the title ("Lisbon, PT").
     * **Travel days:** an event for each flight or train ("Flight BKK–LHR"), or I use the flight confirmation email if Gmail is connected.
     * **Work exceptions:** "sick" or "less than 3 hours" in an event title; use the exception words from their work-day rule (step 6) so the rule picks them up, and if the rule has none and they agree, add these to it as a new version.
     * **UK stays:** labelled clearly with the place from their accommodation register ("London, UK – family home").
     * **Calendars to read:** list the connected calendars and ask which (default: their main calendar only).
     Tell them: "Anything I can't place from your calendar, I'll flag and ask you about instead of guessing." Ask them to keep the calendar up to date as they go, since the log is built from it. Read the rules back, ask "Is that right?", and save only when confirmed: `profile.calendar_rules` entry `v1` with `effective_from`, `stay_title_format`, `travel_day_source`, `work_exception_words`, `uk_stay_labels`, `calendars_read`, `agreed_at`, `agreed_via: "agreed with user in chat"`, `wording_shown`; save a memory with the version and date. They can change them any time (a new version from the date they give).
   - **Backups:** "Each month I'll make a backup of all your records. Shall I send it here in chat, or save it to a Google Drive folder too (or instead)? If Drive, which folder?" Drive only if Google Drive is connected; never shared. Save `profile.backup` `{delivery: chat | drive | chat_and_drive, drive_folder, agreed_at}`.
   - **Review rhythm (calendar connected):** ask one short question: "How often should I go through your calendar with you: weekly, monthly or quarterly?" (weekly if they don't mind; fortnightly also works; a longer rhythm means more days to confirm at once, and the daily check-in still catches missing days in between; they can change it any time just by saying so).
10. **Upcoming trips:** add to `planned_trips`; run `travel-rules-watch` for each new country.
11. **Backfill** from today back to the start of the current tax year (earlier if they want exports): "Where were you sleeping in …?" Every day needs a record pointer or their dated statement (`attested`); unknown days stay not logged and are listed. Set UK work with `srt_engine.apply_work_rule` and ask only about days the rule leaves open or calendar exceptions; with no rule, ask, and unanswered UK days stay `unsure`. Travel days get both countries in `countries_present`.
12. **Run** `srt_engine.py validate` then `summary`. Read back briefly: UK midnights and UK work days per year (rule vs answers), days not logged, ties as answered, Table A/B band with room and proximity, Schengen days in the last 180, and questions HMRC could ask. The approved result sentence only if the engine returns it, with L4.
13. **Set up the routines** (if they came with this bot, update their schedules and switch them on; never create duplicates) (check-in, weekly travel-rules watch, weekly HMRC guidance watch, calendar review at the rhythm they chose, weekly by default, only if the calendar is connected, year-end lockdown on 7 April at 10:00, monthly records backup on the 1st at 09:00) in their timezone; tell them the first check-in time.
14. **How to use it**, briefly: tell me where you were; ask me to model a trip; send records to attach; ask for your Travel and day log (automatic after 5 April), a Records pack or the dashboard.

Never run the destination questionnaire here. Close with L6.
