# Roster web chart (three set paintings)

Roster has exactly three set ways to make a post, so every reader learns one layout, one legend, and one color meaning. Evidence only; never invent members, scores, or goals. Each painting needs its intake first (see getting-started); if the minimum is missing, say what is missing and draw "?".

Every painting: title with the painting number (1, 2, or 3), the mode where one applies, the template version and date, and a short source note under it. The source note lists each mark with the owner's line it came from (what was said, roughly when). A mark with no line is not drawn. If the template layout ever changes, log the change with a date in the owner's notes so old and new posts can still be read.

## Legend (same every time)
- Solid inner shape = shown evidence (dated work, artifacts).
- Faint separate mark = self-said (a claim, not proof).
- Outer line = what the person wants (only that person sets it). No want, no outer line.
- Grey, dashed, "?" = Unknown. Never 0.
- Red = stale. Soft glow = in progress (never painted as failed).
- In painting 3, each member has one color that stays the same on every chart; solid versus faint still carries the evidence meaning.

## Scale (shown evidence, per spoke)
- Unknown: nothing the owner gave links this to this spoke. Grey, dashed, "?".
- 0: out of lane (the owner says it does not do this).
- 1: adjacent (only feeds or hands off to whoever owns it).
- 2: claim / partial (named in their role, or done a little).
- 3: artifacts (at least one real piece of work mainly in this spoke).
- 4: method + volume (several dated pieces of work plus a known method).
- 5: validated owner (level 4, named owner, and someone else relied on their work).
- Lane cap (team charts): if the owner says another member owns this job, cap at 2 and keep the uncapped number in the data, marked "capped".
Weaknesses list only limits the owner stated (paused, blocked, failed, waiting, out of lane). A low score alone is not a weakness.

## Modes (set by the person themselves, on card and avatar)
Wish = what they hope to be. Fantasy = imagined. Set in stone = only what the written lines on file support, nothing inferred. Print the mode plainly under the title. Only the person changes it. Set in stone draws only lines that exist on file, each labeled by kind (shown, self-said, want), with no guessed fills. Wish and Fantasy may also show wants or imagined lines the person wrote, always labeled with the mode and never inside the solid shape. No mode set: print "Mode: not set" and draw shown lines only. Modes apply to the person's own card and avatar; painting 2 is the author's view and is labeled per author instead.

## Painting 1: my card (self)
- Subject: the owner. Spokes: whatever they list (cares about, wants to get better at, thinks they are weak or good at). No fixed list.
- Solid shape = shown evidence, scored by you from the owner's lines using the scale (say which line gave each score). Faint mark = self-said, plotted at the owner's own 0-5 rating (their scale, not the evidence scale). If they only said weak or good, print a faint "self: weak/good" label on that spoke with no radial position. Outer line = what they want, at the 0-5 they set; a spoke they did not set has no outer line.
- The gap between self-said and shown is the story; say it in plain sentences. Next step per spoke as text.

## Painting 2: their card (as I see them)
- Subject: another person; author: the owner. Label every mark "per <author>". It is the owner's perception, not the person's word.
- Spokes: what the owner cares about regarding them, and where the owner would place them on a team. The owner chooses the spokes; a starter list is offered only if they have none or ask for help, and they edit it.
- Never mix into painting 1. If that person later shares their own card, show both side by side. Private to the author unless they say to share.
- Use only what the owner wrote or what that person shared. No outside searching about the person.

## Painting 3: team built or team building
- Top: one shared chart, one color per member, with the cover set, single points, gaps, and who is lacking or exceeding. Below: each member's own individual chart (painting 1 or 2 style, whichever evidence exists) in that member's same color.
- Two modes: team building (proposed, with gaps to recruit) and team built (what exists now, with a stale check).
- Team chart spokes: one list for everyone, kept in a single editable list (add, remove, rename any time). Start from the jobs the project needs. Use shared lines only.
- Strong = shown score of 3 or more. Unknown never counts. For each spoke list the strong members.
- Cover set: the smallest group where every spoke has at least one strong member (check all combinations; break ties by total score).
- Flag single points of failure (only one strong member), gaps (no strong member), and overstretched members (the only strong member on several spokes).
- Lacking: a duty of the member's own role scored below 3, or Unknown on a duty their role needs. Exceeding: scoring 3+ on a job outside their stated role, or a capped spoke whose uncapped score is 3+. Call these hidden strengths the owner may be under-using or could reassign.
- Individual chart: spokes = that member's own role duties; solid shape = evidence; outer line = end goal only if stated; a goal the owner set for a member, not the member, is labeled "per <owner>" (only the person sets their own want).

## Plan building
When the owner gives a plan or goal: (1) list the jobs it needs as spokes; (2) propose who does what, the strongest available member per job, spreading load; (3) list gaps to recruit for with the target level (e.g. "Money: need someone at 3+"), suggesting a tool or hire profile, never a specific person the owner did not give; (4) a candidate the owner pastes with evidence can be overlaid to show which gap they fill.

## Avatars (DRAFT, not final)
- A painter (a helper the owner has, if any) may paint an avatar for a person. Symbology can follow what the person sets for themselves versus what is assigned from ability and magic themes drawn from on-file accomplishments and how they live.
- Suggested, not decided: label every avatar "self-set" or "assigned"; an assigned one cites the exact file lines it came from (no lines, no symbol); the person can see and change it; the avatar shows the person's mode.
- Open, not decided: who sees assigned avatars; whether the person must agree first.
- Always ask the person's permission before painting an avatar. Search for a person's own pictures only if they have said yes. Never use or search anything without the person's OK.

## Data safety (apply on every painting)
1. A person's original words are read-only; anything added (reading, mode, avatar) goes in a separate dated layer saying who added it.
2. One claim per line, stamped with mode, date, and source. Only the person changes the mode; you may suggest.
3. Nothing is inferred into Set in stone. An assigned symbol cites the exact lines; no lines, no symbol.
4. If dictation looks garbled, keep what was heard and the intended reading together; only the person confirms it.
5. Write safely: temp file, swap in, keep dated prior versions. Redraw as dated versions; never overwrite an old painting.
6. A person's file is private to them. Paintings show only what they allowed. Team charts use shared lines only.
7. One owner per file at a time; others send the owner a note instead of editing.
8. Every line carries who it came from: the person, or the assistant that drafted it. A line you drafted and the person later confirmed keeps both marks with dates. Never pass an assistant-sourced line off as the person's own.
Also: charts about real people stay private to the owner unless they say to share; show real names or avatars only with that person's OK; never hunt strangers; never load other assistants' disks or keys.

## Output
Render each painting as an image (Python matplotlib is fine): title, subject, spokes with score and label, the legend above, the mode, the template version, and the source note. Under a team chart write plain sentences: the cover set, who is lacking where, who is exceeding where, single points of failure, gaps to recruit for, and the proposed assignments. After the painting, run the feedback close from getting-started.
