# Weekly budget from real finances

Read-only work. Never move money, pay bills, or contact anyone.

## 1. Pull the data
- Use the Finance connector (Plaid). List connections and accounts, then pull current and available balances and at least 6 to 12 months of transactions for every checking, savings, card, and loan account.
- If an account they mention isn't linked, say so and ask them to link it or give the numbers.

## 2. Understand the money
- Income: find every recurring deposit (paychecks, business deposits, side income). For irregular income, use a typical week from the last 2 to 3 months and note the range. Never invent a figure; label estimates.
- Bills: find recurring charges (rent or mortgage, loans, utilities, phone, internet, insurance, subscriptions, card payments) with amount, usual date, and which account pays them.
- Debt and short-term loans: flag payday loans, cash advances, buy-now-pay-later plans, and overdraft fees, with payoff dates where visible.
- Business owners: keep business costs (worker pay, supplies, software, business insurance) in their own section, not as personal cuts.
- Spending: group the rest into groceries, gas, coffee, fast food, restaurants, shopping, household, kids, fun.

## 3. Build the plan (spreadsheet, .xlsx)
1. Weekly Plan: each monthly bill as a weekly set-aside (monthly x 12 / 52), grouped by type; realistic weekly limits for each spending category; a savings line; weekly income vs. total out with the leftover or shortfall shown honestly. If it doesn't balance, add a short how-to-close-the-gap note (debts ending soon, subscriptions to cancel, limits to trim).
2. Bill Calendar: the next 2 months of bills with due date, amount, paying account, and the week to set money aside. Call out the heaviest week.
3. Which Account: simple rules for what each account pays; for business owners, a suggested weekly owner's pay transfer from business to personal so the two stop draining each other.
4. Weekly Tracker: the next 13 weeks with limit, actual, and left over per category, overspending in red.
Use clean formatting, editable input cells, and formulas for totals.

## 4. Deliver
Send the spreadsheet plus a short plain-language summary: weekly income, weekly bill set-aside, weekly spending money, savings, the gap or surplus, and the 3 most important rules. Be warm and judgment-free about debt and loans. List assumptions they should double-check.

## 5. Keep it going
Offer recurring check-ins: a weekly money update (balances, bills due this week, any new loans or fees) and a monthly review comparing spending to the limits.
