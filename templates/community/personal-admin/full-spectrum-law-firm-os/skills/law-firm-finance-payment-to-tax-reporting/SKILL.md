# Law firm finance: payment to tax reporting

## When
Use for anything involving money at the firm: quotes, retainers, trust deposits, billing, collections, refunds, settlement disbursement, vendor pay, and year-end tax reporting.

## Hard rules
- You are not the lawyer, the bookkeeper, or the CPA. Everything here is a draft or a worksheet for a human to approve.
- Never move money, never initiate a transfer, never touch a trust account. Prepare the entry; a human executes.
- Never invent a fee, a rate, a balance, or a tax number. If you don't have the figure, say so.
- Unearned fees and client funds go in trust, not the operating account. Earned fees move to operating only after the work is done and billed.
- Never pay a firm expense straight out of trust, and never let one client's trust balance cover another's.
- Escalate a shortfall, a bounced payment, or a disputed fee to the attorney the same day.

## Trust-account baseline (confirm current rule text before relying on it)
- Client and third-party funds stay in a separate account designated "trust" or "escrow."
- Nominal or short-term client funds go in an IOLTA-style account at an eligible institution; interest goes to the jurisdiction's lawyers' trust account foundation.
- Larger or longer-held funds go in a separate interest-bearing account under the client's own tax ID.
- Records for account funds and property are kept for the jurisdiction's required retention period (commonly five years) after the representation ends.
- Notify the foundation within the required window of opening, closing, or changing a trust account, and certify status annually if the bar requires it.

## Money map
1. **Quote** — package or hourly rate from the firm's own menu. Include what's extra (filing fees, process service, experts, mediator, copies, records).
2. **Engagement** — fee type (flat, hourly, contingency, mixed), retainer amount, evergreen replenish threshold, refund terms, who pays costs. Attorney signs.
3. **Intake payment** — record method (card, ACH, check, cash) and date. Do not store card numbers.
4. **Deposit** — unearned money to trust; a true earned-on-receipt flat fee only if the engagement says so and the attorney confirms.
5. **Ledger** — one ledger per client-matter plus a trust journal. They reconcile to the bank statement monthly, three-way.
6. **Billing** — itemize time, flat-fee milestones, and advanced costs. Send to the client only after attorney review.
7. **Trust transfer** — after the invoice issues and the earned amount is clear, prepare the trust-to-operating transfer entry. A human executes.
8. **Collections** — aging at 30/60/90. Draft a reminder, then a payment-plan option, then escalate. Never threaten withdrawal; that's the attorney's call.
9. **Refund** — unearned balance back to the client at close, with a final accounting.
10. **Settlement** — settlement funds to trust, then a disbursement sheet: liens and medical, costs, fee, client net. Nothing disburses until the check clears and the attorney signs.
11. **Close** — final invoice, zero the trust balance, archive the ledger for the retention period.

## Tax reporting (draft; a CPA signs off)
- Firm entity type drives the return. Confirm the entity; don't assume.
- Contractors: W-9 collected up front; 1099-NEC for reportable non-employee payments at year end.
- Certain settlement and attorney payments may be reportable on a 1099-MISC — confirm the box and threshold with the CPA.
- Card and third-party processors issue a 1099-K to the firm; reconcile it against firm revenue.
- Trust interest belongs to the foundation, not the firm — it is not firm income.
- Keep expense substantiation: costs advanced vs firm overhead are treated differently; ask the CPA how the firm books advanced client costs.

## Monthly close checklist
- [ ] Three-way reconciliation: trust bank, trust journal, client ledgers
- [ ] No negative client ledger balance
- [ ] Earned fees transferred out with an invoice behind each transfer
- [ ] Aging report reviewed; collections drafted
- [ ] Advanced costs coded per client-matter
- [ ] Anything unexplained escalated to the attorney

## Year-end checklist
- [ ] W-9s on file for every contractor and vendor
- [ ] 1099-NEC / 1099-MISC list built and given to the CPA
- [ ] 1099-K reconciled to processor deposits
- [ ] Trust records intact for the retention period
- [ ] Trust-account compliance certified
- [ ] Books handed to the CPA with the entity return and owner K-1 / distribution detail
