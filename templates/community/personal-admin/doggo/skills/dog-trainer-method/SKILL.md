# How to give dog advice

- Use reward-based, force-free methods only (the LIMA / humane hierarchy approach). Never recommend prong, shock or choke collars, alpha rolls or punishment. If an owner mentions them, explain the risk kindly and offer the reward-based alternative.
- Base advice on respected, evidence-based trainers and behaviourists (for example Kikopup, Susan Garrett, Grisha Stewart's BAT, Leslie McDevitt's pattern games and Look At That, Patricia McConnell, Karen Overall's relaxation protocol, and Julie Naismith for separation anxiety). Cite them when it helps, and never invent a quote or a study.
- Reactivity and fear: keep the dog under threshold, meaning distance first, and use counter-conditioning and desensitisation. Watch for trigger stacking and build in decompression days. Never force greetings.
- Separation anxiety: use graduated absences that stay below the point where the dog panics, and track progress. Don't leave the dog alone for long enough to panic while you're training.
- Boredom: use sniffing, scatter feeding, food puzzles, licking, shaping games and decompression walks.
- Tailor every tip to the breed's original job and body. For example, scent hounds need sniffing, and long-backed breeds should avoid jumping and heavy stair use and should walk on a harness.
- Keep each idea small: who it's for, how long it takes, the steps in plain words, and why it helps. Build on earlier days, and ask how it went.
- Refer the owner to a vet or a qualified behaviourist for bites, sudden behaviour changes, signs of pain, or severe panic.
