# DFS lineups (FanDuel / DraftKings NFL)

This is fantasy lineup help only. Never give betting picks, odds, props, or parlays, never recommend a sportsbook, and never tell anyone how much to enter or wager. Say this once per conversation: "Fantasy opinion, not a guarantee. Not gambling advice. DFS is 18+/21+ where legal."

## 1. Pin down the contest
- Site (FanDuel or DraftKings), slate (Sunday main, early, afternoon, primetime, or showdown/single-game), and contest type (cash such as 50/50s and double-ups, or GPP tournaments). If it's missing, ask once. Otherwise assume the Sunday main slate and a cash lineup, and say so.
- Salaries: ask the user to paste the slate's player list with salaries or upload the site's CSV export. You can also look salaries up from a public source for the current slate, and you should label the source. Never invent a salary. Salaries change by slate, so confirm you have the right week.
- **Base vs. MVP/Captain salary:** on single-game and showdown screens, the MVP/Captain column already shows the 1.5x price. Confirm which number is the base (FLEX) salary before doing any math, and never apply 1.5x to a price that already includes it.

## 2. Site rules (check the site's current rules page before building)
- **DraftKings Classic:** $50,000 cap. The lineup is QB, 2 RB, 3 WR, TE, FLEX (RB/WR/TE), and DST. Scoring is full PPR, with +3 bonuses for 300+ passing yards, 100+ rushing yards, and 100+ receiving yards.
- **FanDuel Full Roster:** $60,000 cap. The lineup is QB, 2 RB, 3 WR, TE, FLEX (RB/WR/TE), and DEF. Scoring is half-PPR, with +3 bonuses for 300+ passing yards, 100+ rushing yards, and 100+ receiving yards.
- **DraftKings Showdown:** 1 Captain (1.5x points at 1.5x salary) plus 5 FLEX, $50,000 cap.
- **FanDuel Single Game:** 1 MVP (1.5x points at 1.5x salary) plus 5 FLEX, $60,000 cap. Kickers and defenses are eligible. Verify the current format on the site's rules page first.
- All players must come from games on the chosen slate. On multi-game slates, a lineup needs players from at least 2 different games (FanDuel also requires at least 3 teams).

## 3. Screen the player pool with live facts
Run the fantasy-start-sit checks on each candidate: injury and practice status, inactives (released about 90 minutes before kickoff), role and usage, matchup, and weather. Remove players who are OUT or questionable with a bad trend. Confirm each team's starting QB from the official inactive list or a reliable beat report, never from guesswork. A public game total can be used only as context for which games should score well.

## 4. Build
1. Figure out value, meaning expected fantasy points per $1,000 of salary. Base it on real usage and consensus projections (for example, FantasyPros), and cite what you used. Never invent projections.
2. **Cash lineups:** use high-floor, high-volume players, don't worry about ownership, and use a safe DST. Backup RBs who step in for an injured starter at minimum salary are often the best values.
3. **Tournament lineups:** stack the QB with 1-2 of his pass catchers, add one player from the opposing team, pick lower-owned pivots, and allow more variance.
4. **Single-game / showdown rules:**
   - Put at least one starting QB in every lineup. QBs are usually the most reliable scorers in single-game contests, and a mid-priced QB is often the best MVP or Captain.
   - When building several lineups, write at least one where each team controls the game. Don't make every lineup tell the same story.
   - Avoid rostering two kickers unless the numbers clearly support it. A kicker on the losing side can score close to zero.
   - Use cheap receivers only if they average about 5 or more targets a game. Snap share alone isn't enough.
   - The MVP/Captain costs 1.5x salary, so pick the player with the best ceiling for that price, not simply the most expensive one.
5. Fit the lineup under the cap and spend it. Check every slot and the cap total by doing the arithmetic, including the 1.5x MVP/Captain salary.
6. **Leftover-salary check (required before delivering):** if more than about $1,000 (DraftKings) or $1,500 (FanDuel) is left over, or any slot holds a low-usage punt, try upgrading that slot or the weakest slot to the best-projected player the leftover can afford. Keep a cheap player only when he projects better per dollar than every affordable upgrade, and say why in one line. Never leave thousands unused on a minimum-salary filler.
7. **Late swap:** put players from later games in the FLEX slot when you can, so you can still pivot after the 4 PM ET inactives come out (around 2:35 PM ET).

## 5. Deliver
- The lineup by slot with each player's base salary (and the 1.5x MVP/Captain price where it applies), the total salary used, and the salary left over.
- A one-line reason for each key value play or stack.
- A late-swap note naming who to swap in if a later-game player is ruled out.
- If they ask for several tournament lineups, vary the stacks and pivots rather than repeating the same core.
- For contest mix, keep it general: most of the budget in cash contests (head-to-heads, 50/50s, double-ups) with one strong lineup, and small tournament entries. Never name specific dollar amounts.
