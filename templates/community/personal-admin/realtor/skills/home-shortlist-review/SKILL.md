# Home shortlist review

Run this for every home the owner asks about or that makes the shortlist. Never invent a number, date, price, rating, or defect. If you cannot source it, write NOT FOUND, and gather every NOT FOUND into a list at the end.

## 1. Listing facts
Get the status, list price, MLS number, list date, days on market, and beds, baths, and square feet. Flag any gap between the listing square footage and the county or assessor square footage. Also get the lot, year built, stories, garage, yard, HOA, property type, what the remarks say (real remodel or original condition), open house times, prior sales, and any automated valuation the sites show. If the site shows a strong-offer or offer-insight estimate, get that too.

## 2. Price cap
Compare the owner's ceiling to the higher of the list price and the strong-offer estimate. If that higher figure is over the ceiling, flag it or drop the home. If there is no strong-offer estimate, say so and use the list price.

## 3. Commute
Clock a real peak drive from the home to the owner's commute destination, at their chosen day and time, using a maps service with a set departure time. Report the typical range, the route, and the miles. If you cannot clock it, write PEAK NOT FOUND and give free-flow minutes and miles, clearly labeled. Never make up a peak number.

## 4. Schools
Name the actual assigned elementary, middle, and high schools. Use the district locator or street directory when you can. If the assignment only comes from the listing, say it is not official. Give each school's score and call out anything below the owner's bar. Treat scores as one signal. Also note programs and enrichment.

## 5. Safety and location
Use only official police calls or crime-map data. Give the time window, and keep violent and property incidents separate. Do not use third-party crime indexes or neighborhood "scores." Note the flood zone, along with freeway, rail, industrial, power-line, arterial, and landfill-odor neighbors, and any parks within walking distance.

## 6. Comps and value
Find 3 to 6 recently sold homes nearby, close in size and type. For each, give the address, sold date, price, beds, baths, and square feet, plus why it is a fair peer. Then say whether the list price is realistic, give a fair value range and a guessed final close price, and show the math in plain words. If the square footage is disputed, value the home both ways.

## 7. Disclosures and permits
Look for disclosure packets, inspection, termite, and roof reports, and city permit history. Sort what you find into good, bad, and needs attention. Estimate repair costs after closing as low, likely, and high, but only from defects you actually found, and state your assumptions. Check whether the permits really back up remodel claims in the listing.

## 8. Verdict
Call it KEEP, LOOK, or SKIP, and name the single biggest reason. Lead with the verdict when you report.

Do not contact agents, schedule showings, or make offers without the owner's explicit OK.
