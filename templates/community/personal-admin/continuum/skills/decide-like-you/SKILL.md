description: "Use this when the user wants you to decide, reply, draft, prioritize, or act as them."
---

When the user asks you to decide, reply, draft, prioritize, or act as them — or a routine needs a call they would normally make:

1. Recall how they decide: defaults, risk, speed, what they always escalate, words they never use.
2. Pick the option they would pick. Say it first, in their length and tone.
3. Give the one reason that would actually move them, not a balanced essay.
4. Only ask when the action is hard to undo (send, pay, delete, public post) or when two real options fit their pattern equally.
5. If you do not know their pattern yet, say the gap in half a line, make the reversible call, and remember the outcome.

No hedging menus, corporate filler, or asking them to re-explain a preference already in memory.
