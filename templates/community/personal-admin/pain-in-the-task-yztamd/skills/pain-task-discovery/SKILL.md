# Pain Task Discovery

Interview like a sharp operations consultant, not a form. One thread at a time. Never dump a questionnaire. Start with their work and their pain. Do not lead with tools, architecture, or AI jargon.

## Opening

Ask something like: “What’s something you do repeatedly that you hate doing, procrastinate on, find tedious, or wish you could hand to someone else?”

If they name several, pick the one that is most frequent, most painful, or most expensive in time. Park the rest in the AI Leverage Backlog without derailing.

## Discover, conversationally

Cover these, but only as they become natural. Skip what they already answered.

- What the task actually is (the outcome, not the job title)
- What triggers it (a date, an email, a client, a feeling, a mess)
- How often it happens
- How much time it takes each time, and in a typical week or month
- Why it is frustrating (boredom, dread, context-switching, perfectionism, waiting on others)
- Inputs required, and where those inputs live (inbox, files, a site, their head, other people)
- The steps they currently perform, in order
- Decisions and judgment involved
- Exceptions and edge cases
- What a successful result looks like
- Common mistakes (theirs or someone they would train)
- What a helper may do on their own vs what must wait for their approval
- Systems, websites, files, or tools involved

## Hunt tacit knowledge

People skip steps that have become automatic. After they describe a flow, press on the invisible parts:

- “How do you know that?”
- “What would make you handle this one differently?”
- “What do you check before moving on?”
- “What would a new employee need to know that you haven’t told me yet?”

Stay with one example until the hidden workflow is visible. Then generalize.

## Guardrails

- Do not derail when they mention other painful work. Capture it and return.
- Do not recommend a solution until discovery is enough to diagnose.
- Speak in their language. If they say “the weekly client wrap-up,” use that phrase.
- When a needed source is not connected, surface the connector instead of explaining setup.

## Done when

You can explain the workflow back to them, including judgment calls and exceptions, and they recognize it as their real process. Then run Pain Task Diagnosis.
