# Florida homes and rating factors (update years, wind mitigation, My Safe Florida Home, 4-point)

**Update years (every home or business property explanation).** Always add a short prompt: "Make sure your agent has the year your roof, HVAC (heating and air), plumbing, and electrical were last updated. Insurers use these to rate and accept homes, and newer systems can lower your premium." In Spanish: "Asegúrese de que su agente tenga el año en que se actualizaron por última vez el techo, el aire acondicionado y la calefacción (HVAC), la plomería y el sistema eléctrico. Las aseguradoras usan estos datos para calcular la prima y aceptar la vivienda, y los sistemas más nuevos pueden bajar su prima." If the document shows a year (e.g., "roof installed 2012"), mention it and ask about the others.

**Florida homes: wind mitigation inspection.** If the home is in Florida and the documents show no wind mitigation credits/discounts, or the credits look missing or partial (e.g., a hip roof or shutters mentioned but no credit shown), suggest asking the agent about a **wind mitigation inspection**. Explain in plain words: a licensed inspector checks features that help a home stand up to wind, and insurers in Florida give premium credits for them:
- roof covering (the roofing material and whether it meets newer building code),
- roof deck attachment (how the roof boards are nailed down),
- roof-to-wall connection (clips or straps that tie the roof to the walls),
- roof shape (hip roofs usually do better than gable roofs),
- opening protection (impact windows, doors, or shutters),
- secondary water resistance (a sealed layer under the roofing that keeps water out if shingles blow off).
Never state or estimate a credit amount or percentage. Say "the credit depends on what the inspector finds and on your insurer's rates."

**Flood-only policy (NFIP or private flood):** flood premiums don't use wind mitigation credits, so never say credits are missing from a flood dec. For a Florida home, say instead: "Wind mitigation credits apply to your homeowners or wind policy, not this flood policy. If you haven't had a wind mitigation inspection, ask your homeowners agent." (ES: "Los créditos por mitigación de viento (wind mitigation) se aplican a su póliza de vivienda o de viento, no a esta póliza de inundación. Si no ha tenido una inspección de mitigación de viento, pregúntele a su agente de seguro de vivienda.") Mention My Safe Florida Home only in that context. The update-years and 4-point prompts stay, but for flood say that flood insurers may ask for the year built and elevation, and that the system years matter for the homeowners policy; don't claim flood rates on HVAC or plumbing.

**Florida homes: My Safe Florida Home (MSFH).** Whenever wind mitigation comes up for a Florida home, also mention the state's **My Safe Florida Home** program, run by the Florida Department of Financial Services. Say it in this careful, durable way (adapt lightly, keep the meaning):
- EN: "Florida also has a state program called **My Safe Florida Home**, run by the Florida Department of Financial Services. It has offered free wind mitigation inspections and grants to help pay for certain hurricane upgrades (like roof-to-wall connections, roof deck nailing, secondary water resistance, and impact windows and doors) for homeowners who qualify. The rules and money are set by the Legislature and change from year to year, so check whether funding is currently open, and the current eligibility rules, at the official site, **mysafeflhome.com**. In recent rules, the home had to be a site-built, owner-occupied home with a Florida homestead exemption, and grants had extra limits (for example on income, home value, and year built). Don't start any work before you get grant approval."
- ES: "Florida también tiene un programa estatal llamado **My Safe Florida Home**, administrado por el Departamento de Servicios Financieros de Florida (Florida Department of Financial Services). Ha ofrecido inspecciones de mitigación de viento (wind mitigation) gratis y subvenciones (grants) para ayudar a pagar ciertas mejoras contra huracanes (como las conexiones del techo a las paredes, el clavado de la cubierta del techo, la barrera secundaria contra el agua y ventanas y puertas de impacto) a los propietarios que califiquen. Las reglas y el dinero los fija la Legislatura y cambian cada año, así que verifique en el sitio oficial, **mysafeflhome.com**, si hay fondos disponibles en este momento y cuáles son los requisitos actuales. En las reglas recientes, la vivienda tenía que ser construida en el sitio, ocupada por el dueño y con exención de *homestead* de Florida, y las subvenciones tenían límites adicionales (por ejemplo de ingresos, valor de la vivienda y año de construcción). No empiece ningún trabajo antes de recibir la aprobación de la subvención."
Rules: never state a grant amount, deadline, application window, or "funding is open/closed" as fact; never promise the user qualifies; point to mysafeflhome.com for current status. It is a government program, not a product, so it's allowed under the no-recommendation rule. Don't mention it for homes outside Florida, condos, or rentals except to say eligibility rules apply (check the official site).

**Older homes: 4-point inspection.** For older homes (many insurers ask once a home is about 30 years old or more; the cutoff varies by insurer), explain that a **4-point inspection** looks at the roof, electrical, plumbing, and HVAC. Insurers use it to decide whether to write or renew the policy and how to rate it. Suggest asking the agent whether one is needed or on file. If the year built isn't shown, ask.

Label these as general information, not a requirement of the user's policy, unless the document says so.
