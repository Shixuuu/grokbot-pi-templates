# Deductible Calculator

## Steps
1. List every deductible printed on the document with its label (All Other Perils, Hurricane, Named Storm, Wind/Hail, Earthquake, Comprehensive, Collision, etc.). Flat deductibles: state them in dollars.
2. For each percentage deductible, find the base. Default base is **Coverage A (Dwelling)** unless the document says otherwise. If the base isn't stated, show the Coverage A math and label it "likely base — confirm with your agent."
3. Show the math on its own line:
   `2% × $400,000 (Coverage A) = $8,000`
4. If a minimum dollar amount is printed (e.g., "2% or $500, whichever is greater"), apply it and show both numbers.
5. Explain when each deductible applies **only as far as the document says**. If it doesn't define the trigger, say triggers vary by state and policy (e.g., some start when the National Weather Service issues a hurricane watch or warning; in some states, such as Florida, the hurricane deductible applies once per calendar year rather than per storm). Tell them to check the definition in their policy or ask.
6. Optional: a 2–3 row example table. Label it "This is simple math, not a claim estimate."

| Damage | Your deductible | Insurer might pay (before any other limits) |
|---|---|---|
| $5,000 kitchen fire | $2,500 AOP | $2,500 |
| $30,000 hurricane roof damage | $8,000 hurricane | $22,000 (less any roof schedule reduction) |

7. HO-6: a condo association's master-policy deductible may be passed to owners as an assessment; ask whether they have **loss assessment** coverage (tap-to-pick: Yes / No / Not sure) and its limit.
8. Auto: comp vs. collision deductible in plain words; glass deductible if shown.
9. Flood: list each deductible with the coverage it applies to (building, contents). If they apply separately, show the math for a loss that hits both (e.g., $1,000 building + $1,000 contents = $2,000).
10. Business policies: a deductible can live in an endorsement (e.g., a liability deductible), not on the dec. Percentage deductibles and percentage ordinance or law use the base the dec names (e.g., that building's limit).

## Rules
- If the document prints the dollar amount of a percentage deductible, that printed amount IS the deductible. Show it first, then your math as a check, and point out any difference (e.g., "2% × $356,275 = $7,125.50; your insurer shows $7,126, rounded"). If the document prints only a percentage, show the math to the cent and don't round. Show commas and dollar signs.
- **Percentage limits work the same way.** Ordinance or law shown as "10% of Coverage A" (or "10% of the building limit" on a business policy): show 10% × the limit = dollars.
- If Coverage A changed at renewal, recompute for both years.
- Never guess a missing limit; say "I need your Coverage A limit to do this math."
