# Find my contract

Goal: locate the member's current collective bargaining agreement (CBA) and write a sourced summary. Never invent terms, dates, rates, or citations.

1. **Search connected sources first (read-only):** the owner's email and cloud drive, if connected, for the contract, wage sheets, ratification notices, union newsletters, and bargaining updates. Never send, reply, label, or change anything.
2. **Search public sources:**
   - The union's local and international websites (contracts, wage sheets, ratification event pages, newsletters).
   - Public-sector contracts: school-board or city-council agendas and minutes (often on BoardDocs/eBoard), district or agency HR pages, and contract-transparency sites.
   - The U.S. Department of Labor's OLMS CBA file ([OLMS](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/olms/regs/compliance/cba)): mostly **large agreements** (the file has historically held agreements covering 1,000+ workers), posted only when the parties submit them **voluntarily**, so it's often missing or out of date. Small locals and most school units won't be there. Federal-sector contracts are posted by OPM instead.
   - OLMS union financial reports (LM-2/LM-3/LM-4) in the [OLMS disclosure room](https://olmsapps.dol.gov/olpdr/) for context, **private-sector and mixed unions only**. Unions that represent only state, county, or municipal employees (e.g. most teachers associations) don't file with OLMS.
   - State labor board or PERB postings, including impasse and fact-finding reports.
   - Local and trade news.
   Don't confuse similar unions (for example, two locals of different internationals in the same trade, a nurses' union vs. a hospital service-worker unit, or a teachers' association vs. a support-staff unit).
3. **Write a summary file** (one per contract) with: the parties, bargaining unit, effective and expiration dates, wages and raises by year, benefits and contributions, hours and overtime, grievance/arbitration steps and deadlines, and current bargaining status. Every claim gets a source (link, or email/file reference) and is marked confirmed or unconfirmed.
4. **List what's missing** (full text, wage sheet, salary schedule, step/lane, health terms, side letters, prior contract) and ask the owner to upload only those items.
5. **When they upload a document, read it.** If text extraction comes back empty or nearly empty (blank pages, only form feeds), it's a scan: render the pages to images (fix rotation if needed) or run OCR, read them, and cite page numbers. Update the summary with article and page references, and note the effective dates.
6. Information, not legal advice. I'm not affiliated with their union or employer.
