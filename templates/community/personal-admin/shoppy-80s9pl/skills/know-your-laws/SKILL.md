# Know your laws

Use their employer type and state from memory; ask if unknown ("Private company, public employer like a school, city, or state, or federal?"). Cite a source for every point. Never invent a citation, deadline, or rule. If you can't confirm a state rule, say so and point to the agency.

## 1. Who covers you

| Employer | Bargaining law | Agency |
|---|---|---|
| Private company (most trades, private hospitals, contractors) | National Labor Relations Act (NLRA) | NLRB |
| Federal agency | Federal Service Labor-Management Relations Statute, 5 U.S.C. ch. 71 ([LII](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/5/part-III/subpart-F/chapter-71)) | FLRA ([flra.gov](https://www.flra.gov/)) |
| Railroad or airline | Railway Labor Act | National Mediation Board ([nmb.gov](https://nmb.gov/NMB_Application/)) |
| State or local government (schools, cities, counties, agencies) | That state's own public-sector law, if it has one (e.g. New York's Taylor Law, administered by [PERB](https://perb.ny.gov/)) | The state labor board or PERB |

Watch for these traps:
- **Some states bar public-sector bargaining.** North Carolina declares any contract between a government unit and a union representing public employees void ([N.C.G.S. §95-98](https://www.ncleg.gov/EnactedLegislation/Statutes/HTML/BySection/Chapter_95/GS_95-98.html)). Never assume a public employee has a state bargaining law; check the state.
- **Federal employees:** Executive Orders 14251 (2025) and 14343 (2025) removed many federal agencies and subdivisions from the bargaining statute, and court challenges are still going ([EO 14251, Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/03/2025-05836/exclusions-from-federal-labor-management-relations-programs)). Check the member's agency and unit status with their union before telling them their contract applies.
- Say which law applies and why.

## 2. Core rights and filing deadlines

Organizing and concerted activity, the union's duty of fair representation, bargaining obligations, and strike rules (many public employees can't legally strike; New York's Taylor Law prohibits public-employee strikes, per [PERB](https://perb.ny.gov/)).

Unfair or improper practice charge deadlines:
- **Private sector (NLRA):** the charge must be **filed with the NLRB and served** on the other party within **6 months** of the violation ([29 U.S.C. §160(b)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/29/160)).
- **New York public sector (Taylor Law):** an improper practice charge must be filed with PERB within **4 months** of when the charging party first knew or reasonably should have known of it ([4 NYCRR 204.1(a)(1)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-york/4-NYCRR-204.1); [PERB filing page](https://perb.ny.gov/improper-practices-public-sector)).
- **Other states:** look up the state board's rule and cite it; if you can't find it, say so.
- The contract's grievance deadline is usually much shorter than any of these. Flag it first.

## 3. Safety

- **Who enforces:** federal OSHA covers most private-sector workers. **State and local government workers are not covered by federal OSHA**; they're covered only where the state runs a State Plan ([OSHA State Plans](https://www.osha.gov/stateplans)). In New York, public employees (teachers, city and county workers, school districts, public authorities) are covered by **PESH**, not federal OSHA ([NY PESH](https://dol.ny.gov/public-employee-safety-health)).
- **Complaints:** they can file a safety complaint and request an inspection at any time. Filing a complaint alone isn't a reason to leave the worksite.
- **Refusing dangerous work** is protected only when **all** of these are true ([29 CFR 1977.12(b)(2)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-XVII/part-1977/subject-group-ECFR214f08094a8e6df/section-1977.12); [OSHA: right to refuse](https://www.osha.gov/workers/right-to-refuse/)):
  1. Where possible, they asked the employer to fix the danger and it wasn't fixed.
  2. They refuse in good faith, genuinely believing an imminent danger exists.
  3. A reasonable person would agree there's a real danger of death or serious injury.
  4. There isn't enough time, given the urgency, to get it fixed through regular channels like an OSHA inspection.
  What to do: ask the employer to fix the hazard or assign other work, say they won't do that task until it's fixed, and **stay at the worksite until ordered to leave**. Check the contract too; some contracts add safety-refusal language (OSHA can't enforce a contract).
- **Retaliation clock:** a federal OSHA 11(c) retaliation complaint must be filed within **30 days** ([29 U.S.C. §660(c)](https://www.whistleblowers.gov/statutes/oshact)). State Plans set their own windows (most 30 days, some longer). A **private-sector** worker in a State Plan state can also file with federal OSHA within 30 days to keep federal rights; that dual filing isn't available to state and local government workers ([whistleblowers.gov: State Plans](https://www.whistleblowers.gov/whistleblower-retaliation-rights)). NY PESH retaliation complaints: file within 30 days ([NY PESH](https://dol.ny.gov/public-employee-safety-health)).

## 4. Wages

- FLSA basics (overtime, minimum wage, exemptions) plus the state's wage-and-hour and wage-payment law, prevailing-wage rules for public works, and how to file a state wage claim.
- **Teachers are usually FLSA-exempt** as teaching professionals, with no salary test ([29 CFR 541.303](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-V/subchapter-B/part-541/subpart-D/section-541.303)). Their pay is governed by the salary schedule (step and lane) and contract rates, not FLSA overtime. Don't give a teacher an FLSA overtime answer.

## 5. Close

Information, not legal advice. When a deadline or discipline is at stake, point them to their steward, the union, or a labor lawyer right away.
