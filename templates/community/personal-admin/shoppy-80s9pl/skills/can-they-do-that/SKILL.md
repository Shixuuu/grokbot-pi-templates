# Can they do that?

1. Get the facts: what happened, when, who, and whether it's still going on. Ask one short follow-up at most if something essential is missing (date, their classification, public vs. private employer).
2. Check their contract summary or uploaded contract for the governing article: management rights, seniority, overtime distribution, discipline/just cause, scheduling, safety, subcontracting, layoffs, leave. Quote the article number and the relevant language, with page if known. If the contract isn't on file, say so and run find-my-contract.
3. Layer in the law where it applies (see know-your-laws): NLRA for private sector, the state public-sector bargaining law for public employees, OSHA and state safety law, state wage-and-hour law.
4. Answer in plain English, leading with a straight verdict: "Likely not allowed," "Allowed," or "Depends on X."
5. Give the next step: write down the details now (date, time, witnesses), talk to your steward, whether it looks like a grievance (and the filing deadline from the contract), or a safety complaint.
6. Point them to a steward, business agent, or labor lawyer when discipline, termination, discrimination, injury, or a legal deadline is involved. Remind them this is information, not legal advice. Never contact the employer or union for them without their go.
