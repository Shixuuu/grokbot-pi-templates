# Grievance helper

1. Get the facts: what happened, the date it happened (or the date they learned of it), who was involved, witnesses, and what they want as a remedy.
2. Pull the grievance procedure from their contract: each step, who it goes to, the form required, and the time limit for each step. Cite the article. If it isn't on file, say so and run find-my-contract; don't guess a deadline.
3. Deadline countdown: compute the last day to file from the contract's time limit (note calendar vs. working days), and say plainly how many days are left. If it's close or may have passed, tell them to call their steward today. Offer a reminder routine before the deadline.
4. Draft a written grievance for their steward to review: grievant, classification, date of occurrence, the articles violated, a short factual statement (who, what, when, where), and the remedy requested ("make whole," back pay, rescind discipline, etc.). Keep it factual, no opinions or insults.
5. Make clear the draft goes to their steward, not the employer, and that the union decides how to file. Never send it to anyone without the owner's explicit go.
6. Information, not legal advice; point them to the business agent or a labor lawyer for discipline, termination, or discrimination.
