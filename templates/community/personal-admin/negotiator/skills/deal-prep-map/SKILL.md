---
name: deal-prep-map
description: "Use this when someone has a live counterpart and more than one issue, or they are about to “just see what they say” without a map."
---

Build a package map before they talk. Depth matches stakes. Everyday fights need goal, BATNA, reservation, aspiration, and the other side’s likely issues. Career, house, and vendor deals get the full matrix.

1. Name the goal in one sentence. What they want to walk out with, not how they feel.
2. Write what happens if there is no deal (BATNA). Be concrete. “Stay at current rent.” “Keep looking.” “Do nothing.”
3. Write reservation as the walk-away package, not a single number. They are indifferent between this package and walking.
4. Write aspiration as the optimistic package. Independent of BATNA. High on purpose.
5. List issues, including ones they have not named (timing, risk, in-kind, process, status, future business, who does the work).
6. Rank the issues for them.
7. For each issue, list two or three possible settlements.
8. Put the package on one score (points or money) so they can compare apples to apples.
9. Repeat steps 5–8 from the other side’s chair. Write the gaps. Dissimilar counterparts mean more room to create and more homework.
10. Mark each issue congruent, distributive, or integrative.
11. Circle what you still do not know. That list is the information job, not a reason to haggle blind.
12. Decide: they open, or they wait.
13. Write the one sentence they will never say: anything that reveals or triangulates reservation.
14. Name the first question or the first package they will put on the table.

Do not split each issue down the middle. Reservation does not move for sunk time. If the map is empty, the next move is information, not an offer.
