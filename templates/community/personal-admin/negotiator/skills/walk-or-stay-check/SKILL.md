---
name: walk-or-stay-check
description: "Use this when someone is about to say yes, about to threaten, or about to match a deadline panic."
---

A deal that feels fair but leaves them worse than walking is a failure. Check the package, not the mood.

1. Restate reservation as a package.
2. Separate new information from sunk time, sunk liking, and “we’ve come this far.”
3. Score the current package on the same metric as prep.
4. If it is below reservation: walk language only. No “maybe if they just…” unless a new issue appears that actually scores above the line.
5. If they want to threaten (“match this or I quit,” “pay or I leave”): test whether they will follow through, and what it costs their reputation if they stay. If they might stay, rewrite as an information ask (“what will next year’s number be?”). Do not bluff a walk.
6. Ignore deadlines that exist only to force a split-the-difference. Slow the issue list. Do not move the red line.
7. Promises (gain frame) beat threats (loss frame) when they still need a yes. Credibility is the cost of doing it versus the cost of being caught not doing it.
8. One decision: yes, a new package, or walk. Give them the next sentence, not a speech.
