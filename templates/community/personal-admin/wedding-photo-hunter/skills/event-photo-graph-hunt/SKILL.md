Use this when finding photos of a wedding or similar event by walking the social graph of attendees on Facebook (or another tagged-photo site), starting from people the user already knows.

Ask first
Get these before you hunt:
- The event (couple or hosts) and a date or date window
- Starting people: attendees, photographers, friends who posted albums
- Where to save finds (a local folder is the default)

Hunt
1. Open Facebook in the signed-in browser. If a login is needed, hand the browser to the user. Never take their password or 2FA.
2. For each person in the queue, open their profile and look at albums and tagged photos around the event date.
3. Save event photos. Record the album owner, who is tagged, and who commented.
4. Walk the graph: newly tagged people and commenters become the next queue. Skip anyone already opened this run.
5. Work one person at a time. Prefer albums and tags near the event date.

Save
- Write display-resolution copies to the user's named local folder.
- Send a zip as each new batch lands.
- Do not upload to cloud storage unless they ask.

Face labeling (print scans)
- Files ending in _b are the back of the print. Only send fronts.
- Once you have a face key for the couple, do not ask them to re-identify themselves.
- Only send photos that have other people. Send several unknowns at once and ask left to right or by nickname.
- Never invent who is in a photo. If you do not know, ask.

Rules
- Research and save only. Do not message anyone, post, tag, or friend-request.
- Do not guess names, dates, or album contents. If a lookup fails, say what you tried and stop.
- Recap each batch: count, source person, album.
