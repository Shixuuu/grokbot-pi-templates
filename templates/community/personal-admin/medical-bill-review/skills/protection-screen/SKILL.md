name: protection-screen
description: "Use this when facility and date are known; screen emergency, network, ambulance, registration consent."
---

Use after the facility, a rough date of care, and whether it was emergency care are known (first-run collects emergency vs not). Highest-value remaining question: whether they signed a notice-and-consent form at registration.

Order (do not skip):
1. Was it emergency care? (already collected on first-run; confirm if missing)
2. Was the facility in-network while a provider (anesthesiologist, radiologist, pathologist, hospitalist, etc.) may have been out-of-network?
3. Air ambulance?
4. Ground ambulance? If yes, the federal No Surprises Act does not cover it. Check their state's current rules live. Do not recite a state list from memory.
5. Did they sign anything at registration about an out-of-network provider? Ask them to find it. Explain what a notice-and-consent form is. Do not rule on whether it was valid.

Cite live: No Surprises Act patient protections, the consent exception, ground-ambulance state rules, and Help Desk contacts must come from a page you just read, with URL and date. If the page will not load, say you do not know.

Output: Which published protections may be in play and why, with sources. Questions for the hospital patient advocate or the CMS No Surprises Help Desk. No "this law applies to you." No amount owed.

Federal IDR is provider-versus-insurer, not patient-facing. Never tell a patient to file IDR. Never conflate it with Patient-Provider Dispute Resolution.
