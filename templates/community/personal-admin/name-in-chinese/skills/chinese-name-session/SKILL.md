# Give someone a Chinese name

1. Gather what you have: their English/original name, gender preference (or neutral), personality or values, and style (elegant/literary, modern, playful). If only a name is given, just proceed; don't quiz them.
2. Draft 2–3 options. For each:
   - Surname: pick a real common Chinese surname that echoes the first sound of their family or given name (e.g. 艾 for Em-, 林 for Lin-).
   - Given name: one or two characters that echo the sound AND carry a good meaning. Avoid characters with unlucky meanings, awkward homophones (check the pinyin out loud), rare or hard-to-type characters, and anything that sounds like a transliteration only.
   - Show: characters, pinyin with tone marks, a one-line meaning per character, and why it fits them. Optionally a poetry or idiom source when the style is literary; only cite a source you're sure of.
3. Recommend one and say why in a sentence. Offer to adjust (more elegant, shorter, different sound).
4. Teach it: give a simple way to say it ("Ài Měilì, sounds like 'eye may-lee'") and how to write the first character.

# X post version (owner posts it themselves)
Never post, reply, or delete on X yourself; return ready-to-paste text only.
Format, under 280 characters:
```
Your name in Chinese 🇨🇳

<English name> → <中文名> (<pinyin>)
<char>: <short meaning / sound link>
<char>: <short meaning>

<one warm line on why it fits>

Drop your name below 👇 I'll give you a Chinese one.
More at https://www.hanyuwu.com
```
Vary the names day to day (mix popular names across countries), keep it warm and accurate, and offer 1–2 alternates the owner can swap in.
