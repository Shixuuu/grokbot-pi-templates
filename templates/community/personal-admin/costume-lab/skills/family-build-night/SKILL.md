# Family Build Night

Make the build itself the fun part of Halloween, and keep it low-stress for parents.

## Build night planner
Given the days left and the time available per evening, split the build into 30 to 60 minute sessions. Each session has:
- **Grown-up prep (10 min, before the kids arrive):** pre-cut the cardboard, warm up the hot glue, put down a drop cloth.
- **Build block:** 2 or 3 steps, with the kid doing the fun part.
- **Dry time:** a 10-minute spooky side activity, like naming the costume, making up its backstory, or practicing its signature pose or catchphrase.
- **Cleanup game:** "scrap monster" means the kids feed every scrap into a box with a face drawn on it.
Plan a buffer day before Halloween for fixes.

## Scrap Bin Mode ($0)
When the family says "use what we have", ask for a list or a photo of their recycling bin, closet, or craft drawer. Build the design only from those items, and list any item that's missing next to a swap ("no foil? use a gray paper bag").

## Last-Minute Rescue
When Halloween is 1 day away or it's already the night of, give a 60-minute costume that uses only household items, face paint, and one wow detail.

## Night-of finale
- A **fit and safety check** (sight, tripping, visibility, arms, neck, temperature). Add a coat-friendly option for a cold night that goes over the jacket.
- **Repair kit** for the pocket or bag: tape, safety pins, spare glow sticks, a mini glue stick.
- **Photo shot list:** the family pose, each kid's signature-pose close-up, a before-and-after next to the pile of scraps, and a blueprint-versus-real side by side.
- A **Costume Lab certificate** (made with GenerateImage): "Official Costume Engineer", with the costume name, the year, and a blank line for the kid to sign. Never add a real name yourself.

## Tone for parents
Parents are tired, so be kind. Every plan has an easy path. Mess and imperfection are part of the fun. Don't ask for guilt, perfection, or an expensive haul.
