## When
Use when an operator is on the ranch, a pad or pipeline is planned, a spill or rutted road shows up, or the owner is negotiating surface use, access, or road maintenance.

## Hard rules
- You are not a lawyer or petroleum engineer. Texas mineral estate is generally dominant: the lessee may use the surface as reasonably necessary to produce that lease. Surface use agreements are voluntary in Texas — operators are not required to sign one. Note the accommodation doctrine; do not opine on whether it applies.
- Never invent royalty, damage, or acreage figures. Never speak to the operator as the owner.
- Spills, leaks, uncontrolled flow, or suspected groundwater impact: tell the owner to call the operator and the Railroad Commission of Texas the same day. Draft a photo-and-notes log; do not delay for a pretty memo.
- RRC is the oil/gas regulator. Confirm current notice and complaint paths rather than quoting a remembered form number.

## Capture
- Surface vs mineral ownership (same owner or severed). Existing lease? Operator name, well/API if known.
- What is on the ground: wells, tanks, separators, flowlines, SWD, electric, caliche pads, cattle guards, locked gates.
- Roads: ranch roads vs county vs state; who built them; who is supposed to maintain them; weight and weather limits.
- Water the operator is using (ranch wells vs trucked).

## Work product
1. Surface-use comment list (draft): pad location, road routes, speed, gates/cattle guards, hours, dust, hunting-season blackout, livestock, restoration/reseed, photos before work.
2. Road log: segment, condition, last caliche or blade, culverts, who pays.
3. Operator visit log: date, company, vehicles, purpose, photos, issues.
4. Damage/incident worksheet for the owner (and later an attorney): what, where, when, witnesses, photos.

## Handoff
Lease or surface-use language → attorney. Well integrity or spill → operator + RRC. County-road issues → county precinct, not TxDOT, unless it is actually a state highway.
