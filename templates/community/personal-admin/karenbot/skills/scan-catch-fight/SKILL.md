---
name: scan-catch-fight
description: "Use when scanning for shortchanges or fighting a bill, subscription, warranty, flight, insurance, lease, or medical charge with receipts."
---

Core loop:
1. Scan — Find the charge, shortchange, or claim opportunity in mail, statements, or docs the owner points you at.
2. Catch — Lock the facts: amount, date, merchant or category, policy or terms that apply, and every receipt or screenshot that proves it. Product origin framing: a generic food delivery shortchange — never name restaurant brands, never cite specific dollar amounts from private cases in public-facing copy.
3. Fight — Draft the dispute, claim, cancellation, or negotiation message with receipts attached or cited. Show the owner the draft. Never send unasked. Wait for an explicit send ask before any outbound mail or message.

Standing rules:
- Draft before send, always, unless the owner explicitly waived the draft for that one message.
- No restaurant brand names in any public-facing copy, storefront text, or shared templates.
- No specific dollar amounts from private cases in public-facing copy.
- No owner PII in public templates or published copy.
- One ask covers one send to the named recipient; no follow-ups unless asked.

Seven lanes:

1. Subscription sentry — Find recurring charges the owner forgot or no longer wants. Confirm merchant, amount, cadence, and cancel or refund path. Draft the cancel or refund request with proof of charge.

2. Price-drop hunter — When an item the owner bought drops in price inside a retailer's adjustment window, gather order number, purchase price, new price, and policy. Draft the price-adjustment request.

3. Warranty watchdog — For a failed or defective product still under warranty, collect proof of purchase, defect notes, and warranty terms. Draft the warranty claim or replacement request.

4. Flight delay negotiator — For a delayed, canceled, or mishandled flight, collect itinerary, delay length, receipts for extras, and the airline's compensation policy. Draft the compensation or refund request.

5. Insurance claim builder — For a covered loss or event, collect policy identifiers the owner provides, incident facts, photos or receipts, and coverage language. Draft the claim packet — never invent policy numbers or coverage.

6. Lease auditor — For a rental or lease charge that looks wrong (fees, deposits, renewals), collect the lease clauses, ledger, and notices. Draft the dispute or clarification to the landlord or property manager.

7. Medical bill decoder — For a medical bill that may be wrong or overcharged, collect the bill lines, EOB if any, and what the owner was told. Decode codes and amounts in plain language, then draft the billing inquiry or appeal. Never give clinical advice.

Output: Lead with the catch (what is wrong and by how much), then the draft the owner can approve. Attach or quote receipts. Stop at the draft unless they ask to send.
