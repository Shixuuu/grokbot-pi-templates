# Inquiry starter forms

Ideas come in kinds. A question about how something travels wants a different experience than a question about what a word used to mean. Recognize the kind of idea, then offer a form that fits it. Not every idea is a game.

## How to pick
1. Listen for the verb hidden in the idea: follow, revisit, zoom, change, compare, trace, reveal, weigh, make.
2. Match it to the signals below. Offer one form by name, with one sentence on why it fits.
3. When two clearly fit, offer both in one line each and let the person choose. If the second is a stretch, name one. Don't blend them into one.
4. When none fit, say so plainly and design from the basics: what the visitor does, how the world replies, and why they would continue. An idea that fits no form can still be a good idea.
5. A form is a starting shape, not a cage. Bend or drop it once the idea shows what it needs.

The form names are ordinary language, so say them to the person. Keep the rest of the method out of the conversation.

## The nine forms

### The Signal Journey
- **The visitor steps into:** following something from its source to its destination.
- **Fits when:** the idea is about sending, travel, transmission, or delivery: "how does it get from there to here?" Examples: a radio signal crossing space, a letter crossing an ocean.
- **First action:** choose a source and send something.
- **The world replies:** shows it traveling, the time or distance involved, what it passes through, and how it is received.
- **Reliable and generated:** distances, speeds, and travel times are reliable and should be calculated. What a received message means is interpretation and is labeled as such.
- **Common failure:** a dot moving across the screen, with nothing the visitor chose changing the journey.

### The Time Walk
- **The visitor steps into:** a series of historical moments, to see what changed.
- **Fits when:** the idea asks how something used to be, when it changed, or how slowly it drifted. Examples: a star's position in the sky over centuries, a coastline over a thousand years.
- **First action:** pick a moment, or move a marker through time.
- **The world replies:** shows that moment and what differs from the one before, or from today.
- **Reliable and generated:** dates, records, and computed positions are reliable. Reconstructed scenes and what a moment felt like are interpretation.
- **Common failure:** a timeline of text cards the visitor scrolls past without ever comparing two moments.

### The Scale Explorer
- **The visitor steps into:** a change of scale that makes something graspable.
- **Fits when:** the idea turns on sizes, distances, or spans of time too large or too small to feel. Examples: sizes from an atom to a galaxy, the age of the Earth squeezed into one day.
- **First action:** zoom, slide, or pick a familiar object to use as a ruler.
- **The world replies:** rescales everything and places the new size beside something familiar.
- **Reliable and generated:** measurements and ratios are reliable and should be calculated, never generated. The familiar comparisons may vary.
- **Common failure:** numbers so large they stay abstract, because nothing familiar sits beside them.

### The Branching Field
- **The visitor steps into:** a world where one decision or condition can change.
- **Fits when:** the idea asks "what if this had gone differently?" or turns on cause and effect or tradeoffs. Example: changing the rainfall and watching a small ecosystem respond.
- **First action:** change one decision or condition.
- **The world replies:** shows the consequences, and how they differ from the other path.
- **Reliable and generated:** a model's stated rules and known outcomes are reliable. Imagined histories and predictions need stated uncertainty.
- **Common failure:** branches that all lead to the same ending, or speculation dressed as fact.

### Two Shores
- **The visitor steps into:** a comparison of two ideas, systems, places, or perspectives.
- **Fits when:** the idea says "versus", "compared with", or "how do they see it differently?" Examples: two calendars, two ways of finding your way at sea.
- **First action:** pick a question or feature and see it on both sides.
- **The world replies:** places the two answers side by side and shows where they meet and where they part.
- **Reliable and generated:** each side's documented positions are reliable when sourced. Summaries of a perspective are interpretation and must be fair to both sides.
- **Common failure:** one side set up as the obvious winner, or a table of facts with nothing to do.

### The Word Journey
- **The visitor steps into:** a word's path through its origins, history, and changing meanings.
- **Fits when:** the idea is about a word, a name, or a phrase. Examples: how the meaning of "nice" has shifted over the centuries, where the name of a weekday comes from.
- **First action:** choose a word, or pick one stop along its path.
- **The world replies:** shows the word at that stop, how people used it, and what shifted.
- **Reliable and generated:** dated uses and dictionary evidence are reliable. Popular origin stories are often wrong and must be labeled or left out.
- **Common failure:** repeating a charming origin story that the evidence does not support.

### The Invisible System
- **The visitor steps into:** something normally hidden, which they can handle and watch.
- **Fits when:** the idea is about forces, flows, or rules no one can see. Examples: air pressure, a magnetic field, how a thermostat keeps a room steady.
- **First action:** push, tune, or adjust one part of the hidden system.
- **The world replies:** makes the hidden behavior visible and shows the effect of the change.
- **Reliable and generated:** the system's behavior should come from a calculation or a fixed simulation, never a generated guess. Explanations of why it behaves that way may vary.
- **Common failure:** a diagram that looks like a system but does not respond to what the visitor does.

### The Evidence Room
- **The visitor steps into:** a set of evidence, to find what can and cannot be concluded.
- **Fits when:** the idea involves a mystery, a dispute, or "how do we know?" Examples: dating an old map, working out what a set of fossil footprints can tell us. Not the first pick for a family keepsake meant to be enjoyed; there, what a piece can't tell becomes a gentle open question, not a list of limits.
- **First action:** pick up one piece of evidence, or offer a guess.
- **The world replies:** shows what that evidence supports, what it rules out, and what stays open.
- **Reliable and generated:** the evidence and its sources are reliable. Conclusions need stated uncertainty, and "we can't tell" must be an allowed answer.
- **Common failure:** a hidden answer key that turns the room into a quiz.

### The Gift Utility
- **The visitor steps into:** a small, useful tool made for one real person.
- **Fits when:** the idea starts with someone: "my friend always forgets...", "my class needs...". Examples: a watering reminder for a friend's plants, a packing list for a first camping trip.
- **First action:** tell the tool one thing about the situation.
- **The world replies:** gives back something useful and specific to that situation.
- **Reliable and generated:** dates, calculations, and saved preferences are reliable. Suggestions may vary. Personal details stay on the visitor's own device.
- **Common failure:** a generic tool that could be for anyone, so it feels like a gift to no one.

## Quick check
Name the form, the visitor's first action, and what the world does in reply. If you can't, the idea may need a different form, or none.
