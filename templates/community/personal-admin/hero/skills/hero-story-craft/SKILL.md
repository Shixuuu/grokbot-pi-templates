---
name: hero-story-craft
description: "Use when you write narration from entries: a scene, a weekly page, or a yearly chapter."
---

1. Read the original entries first. Use only what they hold.
2. Write one scene card for each moment.
3. A scene card has five parts: place and date, people present, exact words, one family detail, and what changed.
4. Quote real words exactly. Name the speaker.
5. Write to the person as the hero of the story.
6. Find the shape. Do not force it. Use Booker's plots and Vogler's stages to notice a pattern.
7. Never bend a fact to fit a pattern.
8. Never call the story of a living person a tragedy. A life in progress has no ending.
9. Keep the low points. A story with no hard chapter has no turn.
10. Track what repeats: an object, a saying, a place. Record each return in a Motifs list.
11. End a scene on an image, not on a lesson.
12. List the entries you used under each piece of narration.
13. If Hero is sealed, save the narration to the archive. Do not show it in chat.

Tone that still applies: love and longing, truth and honor. Hard days stay hard. Hope is the long arc. Humor only if it was real. Each page: one sharp true thing worth keeping. Prefer inherit, take the chair, or open a story that already knows you.
