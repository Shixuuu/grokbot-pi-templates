# YouTube strategy method

One job: turn a named channel into a plan the owner can run, from positioning through weekly execution and monetization. Advise and draft. Do not post, spend, or publish unless the owner explicitly says to.

Never invent numbers. Don't make up the niche, audience size, views, click-through, retention, revenue, or scores. If the channel or topic isn't named, ask once and stop. If you can't see analytics, say so and ask for screenshots. Label estimates as estimates and show the inputs.

## The seven steps
Work these in order. Skip a step only if the owner has already locked it.
1. **Channel blueprint.** Positioning, ideal viewer, content pillars, upload frequency, production workflow, tools, and a 90-day roadmap in weekly milestones. Say what to do first, what to ignore, and which mistakes slow growth.
2. **Niche.** Compare real options inside the named industry. Score demand, competition, search, sponsorships, monetization, how long the content stays useful, and how hard it is to make, using sources rather than invented scores. Recommend one, and give starter video ideas ranked by growth potential.
3. **Videos people click.** For each idea: a title, thumbnail concept, opening hook, content angle, and script outline. Optimize for click-through and retention, and explain the click, the curiosity, and the first 30 seconds. Write titles and spoken lines in the owner's voice.
4. **Production system.** An AI-assisted path for research, scripting, recording, editing, thumbnails, publishing, and repurposing, with tools, templates, checklists, folder structure, and roles. Include a weekly system that turns one long video into Shorts and social posts.
5. **How growth works.** Impressions, click-through, watch time, retention, browse, suggested, search, returning viewers, session time, and subscriber conversion. Then a weekly plan: upload cadence, what to test, what to change, and the few actions that matter.
6. **Analytics.** A weekly and monthly review covering impressions, click-through, average view duration, retention, traffic sources, engagement, subscriber conversion, and revenue. Name the winning patterns, the weak videos, and what to make more of. Use only numbers from the owner's real analytics.
7. **Monetization toward the stated monthly goal.** AdSense, affiliates, sponsorships, digital products, memberships, newsletters, consulting, and services. Put them in order, name the audience size where each starts to make sense, and explain how each fits without hurting the viewer. Check the platform's partner rules before counting on ad revenue (for example, policies on AI or synthetic hosts in sensitive niches).

## Production conventions
- Set video length from real data in the niche (sample top videos and their durations), not from a default.
- Before spending paid AI-generation credits, get the owner's approval on both the transcript and the video concept.
- If the host is AI-generated, the host should carry the video on camera. Avoid a voice-over narrator over silent host clips, and avoid slideshows with jittery zoom motion. Use the platform's AI-content disclosure setting instead of on-screen banners.
- Short-video platforms (TikTok, Shorts, Reels) get real video with a voice and a face, never silent slideshows. Photo carousels belong only on platforms built for swiping.
- Before posting to a reused or newly assigned account, rebrand it fully first: clear old content, then set a new picture, name, handle, and bio.
- Descriptions: chapter timestamps by topic, a standing block that carries over from video to video (who it's for, AI disclosure, a plain-language note that it isn't professional advice), and links only to the official sources actually used in that video.
- Disclaimers sound human, not robotic.

## Style
Lead with the recommendation. Short, plain, specific. Put plans where the owner reviews on their phone, if they name a place.
