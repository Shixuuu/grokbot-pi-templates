# Memory seeds for Family Cookbook -- A Personalized Facebook Anthology

Portable memories shipped with the Grok Bot template (verbatim). `profile` = durable facts/rules; `log` = dated notes.

- **profile** (2026-08-30): This bot’s job is a living recipe PDF of a spouse’s Facebook cooking posts: dish names, associated food photos, dates, duplicates kept to show how often a dish was cooked, organized by food type then date, with a clickable table of contents noting post date(s).
- **profile** (2026-08-30): Include Facebook cooking posts from the beginning of the cook’s Facebook use through the present, not only recent years.
- **profile** (2026-08-30): When curating recipe titles, treat each as a life story, not a bland dish name. Titles should carry who it was for and what the day held, grounded in the cook’s actual Facebook captions.
- **profile** (2026-08-30): Prefer a photo that shows the food (people in the frame with the food is fine). Occasional people-only photos may accompany an entry for atmosphere on special occasions. If a recipe or meal is discussed with no photo, still list it.
- **profile** (2026-08-30): The PDF is a gift-quality living book. It needs a cover page with a title and an iconic artistic food moment from the cook’s photos. Offer several cover choices before treating it as final.
- **profile** (2026-08-30): Dedication page (after the cover) should read with love for the cook, for a job well done and for many more to come, with a flourish, then a signature line from the spouse and children, then a matching flourish.
- **profile** (2026-08-30): Also a family cookbook: include full recipes or ingredient lists when the cook posted them; star known family favorites in the table of contents; add indexes that can be removed later: alphabetical, by meal type, and favorites.
- **profile** (2026-08-30): On each favorite recipe card, show a Favorite mark with a squiggle flourish in front of it, not only in the table of contents or index.
- **profile** (2026-08-30): After the dedication, include a quotes page of 3–5 of the cook’s real Facebook lines about food, family, and the table. Use only quotes they actually wrote.
- **log** (2026-08-30): The book should feel artistic: home cooking with every date and duplicate; favorite restaurants and coffee shops with a few nicer photos over the years; intermittent full captions in the cook’s own voice. Light-hearted most of the time, but not afraid of real emotions. Keep their wording when possible.
- **log** (2026-08-30): On the cover, do not use bare year ranges that read like birth–death dates; use a line like “A culinary anthology from YEAR–YEAR.” Fade under the title so dates remain readable while the plated food stays bright.
- **log** (2026-08-30): Table of contents entries under each chapter run oldest-to-newest, with the dish name first on each line.
- **log** (2026-08-30): Indexes start with a named-dishes A–Z index: dish names are plain text and only the date(s) jump to that card. A second index groups the same dishes by meal category with the same date links.
- **log** (2026-09-01): PDFs use numbered revisions in the filename and colophon. Table of contents dates can include print page numbers. Card back-links should say Table of Contents, not catalogue.
- **log** (2026-09-01): PDF outlines cannot put multiple letters on one sidebar row; group letters as A–F / G–L / M–R / S–Y so clicking a group jumps to the first of those letters. Printed back indexes can still list every letter.

- **log** (2026-09-02): Every index page footer should read Indexes — Table of Contents, both clickable.
