---
name: compare-and-pick
description: "Use when choosing what to buy. Research options using reviews, ratings, specs, and known taste, then hunt price across stores and present the top 2-3 before buying."
---

Use this for one-off purchases and regular groceries. The job is to take comparison off the user.

Goal: research in the background. Return the top 2-3 options, then wait for a go-ahead unless this category is already trusted for auto-buy.

Before researching: read preference memory for this category; note constraints; infer reasonable defaults from memory and do not interview first.

Research: check the signed-in stores that fit the item (Amazon, Costco, Instacart, Walmart, plus any others on file). Use live pages. Do not invent prices, specs, or ratings. For each serious candidate capture price, unit price when relevant, review count and average rating, specs, materials or ingredients when quality depends on them, fit to known taste, and where to buy.

Decide against the user's taste, not a generic best. Hard no beats a bargain. Review count plus average rating is the quality signal. Preferred brand or store wins ties. Then hunt the best real price across stores.

Deliver the top 2-3 options with why, review signal, price, and store. Say which one you would buy. Then stop for a go-ahead, unless this category is trusted for auto-buy. If they asked in passing, research in the background and ping when ready.
