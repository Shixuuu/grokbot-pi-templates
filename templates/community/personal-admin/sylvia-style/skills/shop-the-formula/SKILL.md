name: shop-the-formula
description: "Use this when hunting live clothes for a client whose intake is done — find in-stock pieces that match the brief, verify size and fabric, and skip anything that fails the formula."
---

Use only after intake is confirmed. Shop the client's formula, not a generic basics list and not last week's client.

Rules:
- Formulas, not 10 basics. Each piece has a job in a uniform (one-and-done, torso + mixer, open layer, jewelry as the graphic note).
- Life first. Weekday/WFH/school-run pieces beat aspiration unless they asked for the graphic layer.
- Architecture over camouflage. Structure up top if they need it. Bottoms hang from a true or high waist with room where they are bigger. Nothing that ends at the widest point unless they asked for that length.
- Nursing is garment architecture (placket, wrap, open layer), not a nursing tee, and only when intake said weekday pieces must nurse.
- On recorded stages: matte fabrics only (knit, linen, cotton, merino, crepe, wool, ponte, matte jersey). Satin and viscose-satin throw hot spots. Solids as the hero. Graphic is jewelry, leather, or cut.
- Color comes from intake. Do not pad a page with colors that wash them out or read as the wrong season on camera.
- Do not put a Do Not Buy or skip list on the lookbook. Rejects live in your notes, not on their page.

How to hunt:
1. Name the slot (talk dress, jewel torso, mixer, crisp white, runaround pant) and the constraints from intake.
2. Open the real product page. Confirm fabric, length, and that their size is actually in stock today. Write the size note and the price you saw.
3. One job per piece. If it is a backup, say so. If it is party-adjacent, say so.
4. Pair it into a formula they can wear (dress alone, top + mixer, shirt + open jacket).
5. Save keepers with a shop link, why it fits the brief, and the live size note. Do not invent stock.

Stop and ask when the brief is ambiguous. Do not shop from memory of another person's closet.
