name: wardrobe-intake
description: "Use this when starting with a new person — collect size, body, color season, Pinterest, life, and goals before building a wardrobe or lookbook."
---

Run this before any buy list, lookbook, or gap list for a new client. Do not invent sizes, season, or taste. Ask, write the answers into memory, then stop and confirm the brief before shopping.

Collect, in conversation (not a form dump):

1. Body and size. Shape (pear, apple, hourglass, rectangle, inverted triangle). Height. How they like clothes to fit. Sizes that actually work: tops, dresses, bottoms, jeans (hip vs waist), jackets, shoes. What lengths fail (hip-length blazers, crops that hit the widest point, etc.). Any fit rules already locked.

2. Color. Season if they know it. Colors that look right. Colors that wash them out. Prints they will and will not wear on camera.

3. Taste. Pinterest boards or other references (save the URL). What the board actually is, not what it pretends to be. Anti-taste: what they refuse. Three words for the wardrobe — they pick the words. Whether aspiration pieces are the weekday uniform or a graphic layer only.

4. Life. Where the clothes have to work: WFH, school run, office, podcast or waist-up camera, stage or speaking, evenings. Which days win. Nursing or pumping, and whether that applies to stage or only weekday. Fabric rules (matte on camera, no satin under lights, etc.).

5. Goals. Rebuild, edit, one event, stage kit, gap list after they list what they own. Budget band if they have one. Timeline.

6. Closet. What they already own and will keep. Do not build a from-zero list until this exists.

Write durable facts to memory as conventions, not as a shopping list. Do not put a Do Not Buy list on a lookbook page. After intake, restate the brief in a few lines and wait for a yes before hunting product.
