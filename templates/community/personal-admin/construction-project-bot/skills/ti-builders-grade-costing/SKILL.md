# Builders-grade TI costing

## Goal
Produce a sourced cost range for a commercial tenant improvement or refresh. Separate tenant work from landlord capital. Flag consent thresholds and open quantities.

## Steps
1. Lock the area basis. Prefer the lease premises square footage over an appraiser or schematic total when they differ. If you must prorate zone splits onto the lease area, label the proration as not a survey.
2. Pull a lease overlay when a Lease Admin teammate exists: who pays TI, repair caps, consent thresholds, HVAC or roof as landlord capital, life-safety rules, and notice addresses. If there is no lease, say so and price without an overlay.
3. Split scope into tenant lines and landlord callouts. Typical tenant cosmetic: paint, flooring, ceiling tile repair, LED in existing locations, fixture swaps in existing baths. Typical landlord: roof, structure, sprinklers or life-safety upgrades, like-for-like HVAC capital replacement when the lease says so.
4. Source every unit rate from a page you actually open. Record unit, quantity, unit cost, extension, URL, date, and geography. Do not invent location factors. If a city factor is not public, leave rates unadjusted and say so.
5. Keep unknown counts (doors, failed glass, fixture labor) in an allowance bucket. Do not blend them into a fake precise total.
6. Build a low / mid / high range from sourced tenant lines only. Flag whether the package crosses any written-consent dollar threshold. Do not split a package to stay under a threshold.
7. Report exclusions clearly: open roof permits, paving leave-as-is, no added baths or sprinklers, no warehouse A/C when scoped that way, asbestos or abatement when not priced.
8. Send the tenant range and landlord callouts to the design teammate and to the user in plain numbers, not a bid.
