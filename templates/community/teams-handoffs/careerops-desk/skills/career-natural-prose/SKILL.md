# Career natural prose

Human-facing hiring text must sound like a real person talking or writing a letter — not a telegram, not a product pitch, not code-comment style.

Applies to: cover letters, outreach, interview openers, spoken STAR answers, cover stories, objection replies, and any script said in a room. Does not loosen fact rules. Does not apply to résumé bullets (those stay fragments).

## Root conflict
Dense short sentences are for code and internal punch lists. On spoken and letter prose, subtract fluff but keep real facts — do not chop into one-clause telegram stacks.

## Hard FAILs (Speech must FAIL these)
- Section labels in the spoken script (`**Opener**`, `Beat 1`, `STAR:`)
- All-short telegram stacks (three-plus one-clause sentences in a row)
- Marketing pivots: "I'm not selling X. I'm selling Y."
- Spec-sheet openers: "The role is X. The seat owns Y."
- Résumé-in-prose: title ladder without a human why
- Every sentence starting with "I"
- Em dashes; mid-sentence colon lists used as fake structure
- Sounding like a LinkedIn generator or ATS keyword dump

## PASS bar
- Read it out loud. If the candidate would not say it to a hiring manager across a table, FAIL.
- Full sentences. Mix short and long. Warm, plain English.
- Open like a person: interest in this role at this company / where based / one natural bridge — then go deeper.
- Facts only from the dossier / metrics / STAR bank. Natural voice never invents.

## Who does what
- Writer / Interview Prep: write to this bar before emit.
- Speech: FAIL telegram / pitch / labeled scripts even if facts are perfect.
- Desk owner: do not ship voice that would FAIL this skill.

## One-line test
Would a sharp hiring manager in that field hear a real person — or a bot outline? If bot outline → rewrite once, then re-gate.
