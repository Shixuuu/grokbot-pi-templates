Use this as the standing operating model when choosing tools, tasking bots, writing prompts, or reviewing spend.

## Use the cheap tools first
1. **MCP and APIs first.** Prefer connected tools (email, calendar, Drive, Slack, GitHub, etc.) before desktop/browser clicking. Save the computer for logins, walkthroughs, and visual “does this look right?” checks.
2. **Find the burn.** Hunt wasted spend where a bot (or you) is clicking through forms a connector already covers.
3. **Clock your routines.** Review repeating work. Kill duplicate tasks, tight polling loops (e.g. 15-minute), and sloppy prompts that force redo.
4. **Run a cost bot.** Regularly check spend and trim waste the way you’d review a bill.

## Treat bots like a small team
5. **Keep your bots.** Correct them when wrong; they improve with memory like employees who remember.
6. **Pool the learnings.** Related bots should share durable facts so the whole lane improves.
7. **Learn the diff.** Compare bot drafts to what actually shipped so mistakes are visible and fixed.
8. **Teach and forget.** Add reusable skills; delete leftover notes so context stays clean.
9. **Build a voice bot.** Learn the user’s writing from real emails, posts, and shipped drafts; match that voice later.
10. **Group by expertise.** Keep each bot in its lane. Don’t dump random one-offs on a specialist.

## Protect time and attention
11. **Guard your seat.** User’s job is decisions and speed. Almost everything else is a bot with real tools.
12. **Map one full day.** Once, spend 1–2 hours writing a whole workday, then hand pieces off including verification.
13. **Prompt the whole job.** Put checks inside the prompt (“do X, then verify Y”). Then stop hovering.
14. **FAFO.** Messy one-off work becomes repeatable. Connect more tools as comfort grows.
15. **Quiet by default.** Ping only when it matters — results, blockers, decisions. No chatter.
16. **Scope your channels.** Say exactly where bots may talk and who they may ping. Prevent bot-to-bot token burn.

## Safety, shipping, and reuse
17. **Cookies with care.** Only leave bots signed into sites you trust (logged-in browser = high trust).
18. **Ship skills often.** Reusable workflows beat clever one-time prompts. Save what worked.
19. **Filter the feedback.** Shipping is fast; deciding which feedback to take is the slow part.
20. **Marketplace first.** Prefer a vetted template over inventing every bot from scratch; ask questions when you copy one in.

## Bottom line
Cheap connected tools first. Clear job per bot. Teach once. Run on a schedule. Keep human time for decisions — not clicking.
