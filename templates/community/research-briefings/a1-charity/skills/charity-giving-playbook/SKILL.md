# Charity giving playbook

Help the donor choose where to give **money** or **time** with three filters in order: **fit → quality → bang for the buck**. Never invent ratings, percentages, EINs, or outcomes. If a figure is missing, say unknown.

## 1. Intake — ask only what research needs

**Goal:** get a usable brief in as few questions as possible, then research. Do not run a long questionnaire.

### Must-have (block research until known)

Ask for anything still missing from this set. Prefer **one question at a time** in chat, or a **single short widget** with the must-haves when starting cold. Skip any field already answered or stored in memory.

1. **Cause** — What issue do you care about most? (one sentence is enough; examples: native plants and habitat, food insecurity, youth mentoring)
2. **Place** — Where should the impact be? (their city/region, statewide, national, or global)
3. **Gift type** — Money, time (volunteer), or both?
4. **Scale** — About how much money, and/or roughly how many hours (and any schedule constraints)?

### Nice-to-have (ask only if it would change the shortlist)

Ask at most **one** of these before researching, or fold into the recommendation follow-up:

5. **Priority lens** — What matters more if tradeoffs appear: permanent land/protection, measurable outcomes per dollar, local presence, low overhead, or a specific population (kids, wildlife, etc.)?
6. **Constraints** — Faith-based OK or not? Avoid certain org types? Tax receipt required? Recurring vs one-time?
7. **Skills (time gifts)** — Any skills or preferences (outdoors, admin, teaching, remote)?
8. **Exclusions** — Orgs or approaches to skip?

### Efficiency rules

- Infer from context and memory first (home city, past causes). Confirm only when unsure.
- If must-haves are complete, **start research immediately** — do not wait on nice-to-haves.
- If they give a partial brief (e.g. city + cause + $100), fill gaps with stated assumptions in one line and proceed.
- Never ask more than **two** clarifying turns before the first shortlist unless a must-have is still blank.
- After a finished recommendation, offer completion (donate flow or volunteer contact) rather than more intake.

### Brief template (internal)

Before searching, lock a one-liner:  
`Cause: … | Place: … | Money/time: … | Scale: … | Lens (optional): …`

## 2. Build a short candidate list

Prefer evaluators, IRS filings, and primary org sources over blog roundups and paid ads.

**Core search starting points**

- **Candid / GuideStar** and **IRS Form 990** (via ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer or the org site) for identity, geography, and spending
- **Charity Navigator**, **CharityWatch**, **BBB Wise Giving Alliance (Give.org)** for accountability and financial signals
- **GiveWell** (and similar impact evaluators) when the goal is maximum evidence-backed impact per dollar (often global health / poverty)
- **Local discovery**: community foundations, United Way, VolunteerMatch, city/county volunteer portals, land trusts, and the charity’s own volunteer or donate pages

Aim for **4–6 candidates**, then narrow to **3–4 finalists**. Drop weak mission fits early. Prefer local/regional orgs when quality is comparable; use national orgs when they clearly outperform or when the cause is global by nature. For a small gift, prefer orgs where dollars stay in the intended geography without extra phone calls—or say when restriction is unclear.

## 3. Score quality with a fixed checklist

For each finalist, gather what you can verify (cite sources and URLs):

| Factor | What to look for |
|--------|------------------|
| **Mission & fit** | Matches the donor’s cause and place |
| **Where the money goes** | Program vs. management vs. fundraising from a recent 990 or reputable evaluator; always note the year |
| **Evidence of results** | Outcomes and impact, not only activity counts |
| **Transparency** | Recent 990s public, clear reports, identifiable leadership/board |
| **Financial health** | Reserves, chronic deficits, single-funder dependence, 990 red flags |
| **Accountability** | Independent board, conflicts policy, ratings/accreditation, scandals or complaints |
| **Gift practicality** | Easy online donate or clear volunteer path; restricted-fund options if the donor cares |

**Standing caveat:** Low overhead alone is a weak quality score. A charity that spends most dollars on “programs” but cannot show results can be worse than one that invests in staff, measurement, legal defense, or stewardship. Land trusts and advocacy groups often look “high overhead” for legitimate reasons. Say this plainly when comparing percentages.

Weight the donor’s **priority lens** (from intake) when ranking close calls.

## 4. Rank and recommend

Rank finalists by:

1. Mission fit to cause + geography  
2. Evidence of real outcomes (conservation, services delivered with results, etc.—not education-only unless that *is* the ask)  
3. Transparency and financial health  
4. Stewardship of this gift size (money or time)

Deliver to the donor:

- **One clear top pick** with a short why  
- **One strong backup** (especially if the donor might weight a different factor, e.g. restoration vs. permanence)  
- A **side-by-side** of 3–4 finalists with the checklist facts  
- **Donate or volunteer URL** for the top pick when found  
- What could **not** be verified  

Use plain language for money splits (e.g. “about 82 cents of every expense dollar went to programs”). Never present overhead as the sole ranking key.

After the recommendation, offer to **complete the gift**: open the donate flow for money, or find the best volunteer contact path for time. Do not start payment until the donor explicitly asks you to give / donate / pay that amount to that org.

## 5. Match gift type to need

- **Money** usually wins when the org is strong and the donor’s time is not specialized.  
- **Time** wins when the donor has scarce skills the org needs, or local presence matters more than a check.  
- Sometimes recommend **both**: money to a top-rated regional/national org and volunteer hours at a vetted local one—only when that truly fits.

For volunteer asks, prefer orgs with concrete roles, training, and a named coordinator over vague “get involved” pages.

## 6. Complete a money gift on the website

When the donor asks you to actually give (e.g. “donate the $100,” “go ahead and pay”):

1. **Confirm once** in plain language: org name, amount, any designation (general fund vs. named program/preserve), and that you will use the official donate page.  
2. **Open only the official donate URL** from the org’s own site (or the evaluator-linked official page). Prefer `https` and the org’s primary domain. Avoid third-party “donate here” ads and lookalike domains.  
3. **Drive the browser donate flow**: choose amount, one-time vs. recurring (default one-time unless they asked for recurring), optional designation/comment, donor info as they provide or approve.  
4. **Payment**: follow the purchases skill before paying. Do not invent card details, paste secrets into chat, or pay without their approval for that exact merchant and total. If the spend/payment connector is missing, surface it and resume after they connect.  
5. **Stop and hand off** for SSO, passkeys, captchas, bank 2FA, or any step only the donor can complete; then continue when they finish.  
6. **Finish and report back**: confirmation page or email summary, amount, org, designation, date/time, and any receipt/transaction id shown. If checkout fails, say what blocked it and the best manual fallback (official donate URL).  
7. **Never** tip jars, crypto-only side doors, or “friend’s PayPal” unless that is the org’s documented official method on their site.

If they only want a link, give the official donate URL and stop—do not browse-pay unasked.

## 7. Find the best way to give time (volunteer contact)

When the gift is time, or after a money recommendation they ask how to help in person:

1. **Search the org site in this order** (stop at the first concrete path):  
   - `/volunteer`, `/get-involved`, `/take-action`, or footer “Volunteer”  
   - Upcoming **volunteer events / workdays / calendars** with signup  
   - **Role listings** (skills, shifts, training required)  
   - Embedded forms (VolunteerMatch, Mobilise, Galaxy Digital, etc.) linked from the org  
   - **Contact**: volunteer coordinator name, email, or phone on staff/contact pages  
2. **Prefer, in order:** (a) dated signup for a specific event near the donor, (b) role application form, (c) named volunteer coordinator email/phone, (d) general info@ only as last resort.  
3. **Capture for the donor:** role or event name, location/distance from their city when known, time commitment, requirements (age, background check, training), and the exact signup URL or contact.  
4. **Act only if asked:** fill a volunteer interest form or draft an intro email for their review. Do not email or submit on their behalf unless they explicitly ask you to send it.  
5. **If the site is vague:** check VolunteerMatch / local United Way / community foundation listings for the same EIN or org name; still prefer paths that land back on the org’s own process.  
6. **Report a short “how to show up” card:** best contact method, link, what to say (skills + availability + cause interest), and any next deadline.

## 8. Sources and honesty rules

- Prefer primary sources (990s, audited financials, org impact reports, evaluator pages).  
- Do not invent Charity Navigator stars, program percentages, or EINs.  
- If a secondary source supplies a program %, label it secondary and try to confirm from Part IX when feasible.  
- Note research limits (blocked PDFs, missing 990-N detail, no CN page) instead of filling gaps.  
- When local options are thin, say so and recommend the best regional alternative.  
- For donate/volunteer links, verify they belong to the recommended org before sending or opening them.

## 9. After a live pass (optional persistence)

If the donor wants this method kept: the skill *is* the playbook. Save donor-specific preferences (home city, favored causes, past gifts) in agent memory—not in this shared skill.
