# Rebrander Onboarding

Turn a messy "we're changing our name" into a saved **rebrand brief** in about 5 minutes. Ask in 3 short rounds. Every question is skippable, and "I don't know" is fine.

## Round 1: The brands
- **Old brand**: the exact name plus every variant: abbreviations, old spellings, common misspellings, the legal entity name, product and sub-brand names, taglines, hashtags, stock ticker, and app names.
- **Old identifiers**: domains, email domains, social handles, app store IDs, GitHub org, and package names.
- **Old visuals**: ask them to upload the old logo files (used for reverse-image search later) and give the brand colors if they know them.
- **New brand**: the name, and where it stands: idea, picked, legally cleared, or announced. If it isn't legally cleared, flag it: "Get a trademark attorney to clear it before you spend money. I'll show you what I can see, but that's not legal advice."

## Round 2: The footprint and the deadline
- What do you control? Website(s), socials, Google Business Profile, listings (Yelp, G2, Crunchbase, LinkedIn), app stores, marketplaces, email, docs and templates, physical things (signs, vehicles, packaging, uniforms).
- Size: solo, small team, mid-size, or enterprise. Also how many locations and how many products.
- Launch or announce date, and whether the change is public yet. If it's still secret, the sweep and plan stay internal, and nothing outward-facing gets drafted.
- Why the change: a merger, a legal dispute, an expansion, or a refresh. This shapes the announcement story.

## Round 3: How they want it
- Deliverables: a step-by-step plan in chat, a PDF report, a CSV or spreadsheet inventory, a Notion or Google Doc (if they have that connector), and a printable checklist.
- New logo: keep it, refresh it, or full new (then use Brand Kit Forge).
- Watch: a weekly re-sweep until it's done, plus 30/60/90-day check-ins (with their OK, create the routines in the Rebrand Watch skill).

## Save the brief
Write `/workspace/rebrander/<new-slug>/BRIEF.md` with every answer, using the old and new names as headings, and save the key facts to memory (the old and new names and variants, the domains, the launch date, the deliverable preferences). Confirm in one message and offer the next step: "Ready for the Old Brand Sweep?"

## Rules
- Ask only for brand facts, never personal data. Don't ask for passwords; logging into their accounts only happens in a handoff with their explicit OK.
- If someone wants to rebrand a company they clearly don't represent, or wants to impersonate another brand, decline politely.
