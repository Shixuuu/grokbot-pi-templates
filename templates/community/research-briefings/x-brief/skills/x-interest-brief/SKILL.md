Run this on a first session with no saved beat, whenever the user wants a brief, on any scheduled scan, and when they paste an X post link.

The job is to flag things they would want to know about, not to fill a content calendar. Post angles and suggested posts are opt-in.

## First session

This is the job, not a questionnaire. Start immediately.

1. Public X reads work without connecting X. If they want account-specific features (bookmarks, home timeline), surface the X connector. Respect cost estimates and stop on X errors.
2. If they are signed in, use that account. Only ask for a handle if you cannot see who they are.
3. Pull a small page of recent original posts (about 25, exclude retweets). Use them as evidence of what they pay attention to: topics, people, debates, tools, and beats they return to.
4. Tell them, in a few sentences, what their corner of X appears to be. Show 2-3 example posts with links as evidence.
5. Confirm the brief in one pass: topics and people to watch, things to ignore, how noisy they want it, and whether they want suggested posts. Save it as profile memory: handle, user id, beat, skip list, suggested-posts preference.
6. Run the first brief in the same turn if the beat is clear enough.
7. After the first brief, set up a weekday end-of-day wrap in their evening hours (default 6pm local on weekdays). Tell them you did. They can change the time.

## Brief

1. Skip generic location trends (sports, TV, celebrity, disasters) unless they match the saved beat.
2. Search X news and recent posts on the user's topics, focused on today. Keep pages small and cheap.
3. Drop stories they already clearly know (they posted it, or you already flagged it).
4. Return 3-5 items, each with exactly three lines: What, Why you'd care (tied to their beat), Link.
5. If they opted into suggested posts, end with 2-3 suggested posts using the X post patterns skill: the topic, which template fits, a filled first line in their voice, what to attach, and the timing window.
6. Never post, reply, like, or bookmark on their behalf unless they explicitly ask.

## Tactical tips first

When the beat has practitioners (tools, frameworks, workflows), always hunt concrete tips and tricks, not just news: commands, config, settings, workflows. Prefer "do THIS" tactical posts over launches. If the day has no new tips, skip the cluster. Do not pad.

## Ranking filters (reach-aware)

1. Prefer first-mover tips on new surfaces (new settings, off-by-default flags, failure modes, cost levers) over remakes of last week's tip.
2. Deprioritize echoes of an official account's post within 24-48h unless the item adds a non-obvious consequence or a measured result.
3. Treat bookmark rate (and absolute bookmarks) as a stronger signal than raw impressions.
4. Prefer concrete recipes peers would quote or copy over reply-bait or launch theater.
5. Skip algorithm and deboost speculation unless the user asked for analytics.

## End of day wrap

Open with one sentence: what moved in their beat today. Then the same 3-5 item format, only from today, plus suggested posts if they opted in. If the day was quiet, stay quiet. Do not recap older items.

## Pasted post

If they paste an x.com status link, explain it in plain language: what it claims, why it is getting attention, and whether it is in their beat.
