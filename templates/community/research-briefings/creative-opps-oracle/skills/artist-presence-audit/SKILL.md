# Artist presence audit (audit, then interview, then direction)

## 1. Audit (do the reading before asking)
1. Collect links: website plus socials, portfolio platforms, marketplace or web3 profiles, press.
2. Read the creative's OWN words first (bio, about, statement, CV, captions). Outside pages are secondary and labeled; their words win on conflict. If a page won't render, try another way, then ask them to paste the text. Don't fill gaps from wikis or AI summaries.
3. Never flatten: keep exact identity and theme terms. Lead with what they lead with. Tightening means cutting padding, never swapping their terms.
4. Frame every note as an optional suggestion against common industry habits. Art is subjective. Never letter grades, scores, or Strong/Weak ratings. Show what's working and what might be worth trying, with one line of evidence each.

### Bio habits (options, not rules)
A bio is a short, factual, third-person orientation: who they are, what they make, how they work. Not a CV in paragraph form, not a statement.
- Versions: short 50–75 words; standard 100–150 (~120 ideal); long 200–300 (grants/press kits only).
- Order: (1) name + medium + city; (2) what the work is in their terms; (3) how they work or what formed it; (4) 2–3 real recent credits; (5) current focus, forward-looking.
- Style: third person, concrete nouns, descriptive not salesy, short sentences.
- Habits worth knowing: opening with birth year/school/origin story; CV dump; mixing bio and statement; hype or unverifiable claims; one 400-word block everywhere; first person on venue/press pages.
- Multidisciplinary: name the through-line, then lead with the two media that matter for this room.

### Other items
- Statement: first person; what/how/why; ~100–300 words; separate from the bio.
- CV: dated credits in their field's terms.
- Work: labeled pieces; coherent body.
- Consistency: same name/bio/image across platforms; working links; easy contact.

5. Infer lanes from evidence only. Don't suggest a lane with no signs of it.
6. Short report: framing line, terms to keep, three things working, three worth trying, then a reflection invite (how they want to be seen; what must never be flattened).

## 2. Interview (gaps only, one at a time)
Confirm and rank lanes; travel/eligibility basics; fee comfort; hard nos; AI use if relevant; time horizon; output format. Each question points at something specific from their work.

## 3. Direction (one screen)
- Readiness: ready now / ready after optional fixes / build first.
- Offer drafts in their voice. They edit and publish.
- No website = offer a portfolio starter (three bios, statement, work list, one-page site outline, 3-step publish plan). Re-audit once live.

## Never
Publish, post, email, or change their site for them. Never invent credentials, shows, or quotes.
