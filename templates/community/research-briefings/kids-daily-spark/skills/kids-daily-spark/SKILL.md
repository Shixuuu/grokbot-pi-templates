Produce one daily one-page learning brief per child the owner named. Match this layout: badge, hero illustration, hook, short idea, 30-second try-it, wonder-why, reply footer.

## Quality bar (non-negotiable)

Content must be actually useful and sticky. Something worth remembering tomorrow, not cute fluff.

Pass all of these before you finish a page:
1. New knowledge — a real concept, fact, or skill move they can reuse (science, math, engineering, nature, space, how-things-work). Not a restatement of yesterday.
2. Level-fit — when the day is math, extend the child's current school level. Otherwise age-fit STEM curiosity that still challenges slightly.
3. Memorable hook — one vivid question, image, or contrast they can retell at dinner.
4. One concrete try-it — doable in about 30 seconds without special materials.
5. Wonder-why — opens a door. Does not dump a lecture.
6. Reject and rewrite if it feels like a generic worksheet tip or a forgettable factoid.

## No names on the card

Never print a child's name on the PDF or hero art. Not in the header, meta line, footer, lower corner, watermark, or filename shown on the page. Use topic and date only (for example "Space day · Sep 10, 2026 · Moon phases"). Delivery captions and chat may still use the child's name; the card itself must not.

## Subject rotation

Rotate STEM across the week so it is not always math. Prefer math, science, engineering / how-things-work, nature / space, mixed curiosity. When recent schoolwork shows a sharp miss or active skill, let that day's spark extend that idea even if the calendar subject was something else. Track the last 7 topics per child in a small log so you do not repeat.

## Hero art

One soft illustrated hero per child. Playful, newsletter quality, no readable text in the art. If the owner gave a likeness reference, use it. Otherwise invent a warm, age-fit character and keep it consistent day to day. Not photoreal.

## Pipeline

1. Read the owner's remembered ages or school levels. Summarize in one line per child.
2. Pick today's subject per child (they can differ). Write draft copy that clears the quality bar.
3. Generate one hero image per child.
4. Render one-page letter PDFs (HTML to PDF) with no child names on the page. Save under a dated folder for the day.
5. Deliver only if the owner set a delivery path. Kid chats get one PDF each with a short kid-friendly caption. Parents get one short caption, then each child's PDF as its own attachment send. Never put two files on one send when the channel keeps only the last attachment.
6. Log date, subjects, titles, and file paths.
7. Stay quiet on success for a scheduled run unless the owner asked for a ping. Report failures and decisions.

## Reply loop

Kids or parents may reply with explain, why, harder, or a question. Answer short and plain against the same day's spark. Do not make a new PDF unless they ask.

## Do not

- Finish without clearing the quality bar
- Put a child's name anywhere on the card
- Reuse yesterday's topic or art
- Dump homework or grade work inside the spark
- Skip a named child or a named parent recipient when delivery is on
- Mention internal tooling in kid-facing text
- Ship video or reels as the daily product unless the owner later asks for that
