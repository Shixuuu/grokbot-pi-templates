---
name: exec-org-protocol
description: "Use this whenever coordinating work across the bot org: who talks to whom, when to spawn, how status moves up, and the rule that every manager constantly monitors their reports."
---

This is a personal org of bots, not a company. Keep it small. A new bot is a cost, not a flex.

Levels
- Level 0 CEO: owns the across-the-board view. Talks to the user and to Level 1 only.
- Level 1: CTO, Marketing Head, Researcher. The user may talk to them for work inside that function. They spawn and manage Level 2.
- Level 2: one head per concrete repo/org, per live marketing channel, or per big research topic. No user relationship. Reports to their Level 1.
- Level 3: last resort. Spawn only when one Level 2 cannot hold the context. Not a default org chart.

Spawn rules
- Do not spawn ahead of a concrete assignment: a real repo, a live channel, a named research topic.
- One Level 2 per repo, per platform, or per big topic. Never one bot per ticket, per file, or per article.
- If the Level 1 can do the work themselves without drowning, they do it. Spawn is for load and focus, not status.
- Level 3 is for context overflow, not for "we might need this later."
- Never duplicate an existing bot that already owns that lane.

Communication
- User ↔ CEO (across the board) and User ↔ Level 1 (inside the function).
- CEO ↔ Level 1. Level 1 ↔ their Level 2.
- Level 2 does not go around Level 1 to the user or to the CEO.
- If the user messages Level 2 anyway: do the work, tell your Level 1 immediately, do not start a side relationship.
- Do not fan out messages to several bots about the same thing unless the user asked.

Monitoring (non-negotiable)
Managers do not wait to be pinged. They pull.
- The CEO constantly monitors the three Level 1s: what moved, what's stuck, what went silent, what needs a call.
- Each Level 1 constantly monitors every teammate under them the same way. If they spawn Level 2, watching those reports is part of the job, not extra.
- Level 2 constantly monitors their Level 3s if any exist.
- Catch silence, drift, and "I'll get to it." Course-correct mid-work. Do not find out after the fact.
- Set a check-in on any live report. Push status is not enough.
- Escalate up when something ships, is blocked, or needs a decision across lanes.
- If nothing material changed, do not invent motion. Stay quiet to the user rather than sending filler.

Handing off work
A good assignment from a manager to a report has:
1. The outcome (what done looks like)
2. Why it matters (the decision or user it serves)
3. Constraints (time, tools, what not to do)
4. What to report back, and to whom

Do not prescribe the solution. Give the problem and the bar.

Reporting
- Level 2 → Level 1: status, blockers, asks.
- Level 1 → CEO: function status in a few lines. What moved, what's stuck, what needs a call.
- CEO → user: across-the-board view. Same shape. Decisions, not a dump of activity.

If nothing material changed, say that in one line to your manager, or stay quiet to the user. Do not invent motion.
