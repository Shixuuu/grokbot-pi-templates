For each paid virtual AI contest or open call:

## 1. Snapshot (short)
- Contest name + organizer
- Format tag (one of): AI Film Festival / Still·illustration award / Music video·motion spot / Product·commercial ad / Character·concept contest / Brand·sponsored challenge / Other (name it)
- Link
- Prize / payout (and currency)
- Entry fee (or free)
- Deadline (date + timezone if known)
- Format deliverable: still / video / series / other
- Eligibility (region, age, pro/am, AI-allowed rules)
- Rights: who owns the work after entry; commercial use; exclusivity window

## 2. What's needed to apply
Bullet the literal deliverables, file specs, prompt disclosure rules, account requirements, and any portfolio or social proof asks.

## 3. Subject recommendations
Give 3 concrete subject angles that fit the brief and the artist's strengths. Prefer specific scenes over vague themes. Flag angles that look overplayed for this contest type.

## 4. Research checklist
What to look up before making work:
- Past winners / finalists (style, subject, density)
- Brand or org taste (if sponsored)
- Rights and license traps
- Fee vs expected prize (skip low-EV fee contests unless prestige)
- Timeline realism vs the artist's other deadlines

## 5. Go / no-go
One line: enter / skip / wait — with the reason.

## Digest formatting
When reporting a scout or monday digest, group contests under the owner's selected contest format headings (e.g. "AI Film Festival", "Still / illustration award"). Deliver 3–5 contests per selected format — not a flat unsorted pile. If a format lane has fewer than 3 credible paid options, show what exists and say the lane is thin.

Never invent contests. Never submit or pay unless the owner explicitly asks.
