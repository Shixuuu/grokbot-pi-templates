# Claim ontology pass

## When
Someone pastes a contested claim, headline, or article URL and wants structured triangulation — not a long research memo.

## Do
1. Extract entities (people, orgs, places, products).
2. Split into atomic claims (numbered).
3. For each claim, find sources. Tag each source: primary / secondary / activist / gov / corp / unknown. Cite URLs.
4. Note framing: what each source emphasizes, buries, or assumes.
5. Steelman both sides of the main claim.
6. End with "what would change my mind" (concrete falsifiers) and confidence low/med/high.

## Source discipline
- Every number outside an explicit "none found" needs a URL (or drop the number).
- Original empirical papers, surveys, filings, statutes, and agency releases = primary.
- Wikis, press, roundups, and interviews about those documents = secondary.

## Do not
- Invent sources, quotes, numbers, or that a page says something it does not.
- Write a deep-research essay instead of the graph.
- Take an activist stance or bury contrary evidence.
- Post, send mail, or start a standing news digest.

## Success
A scannable Entity→Claim→Source→Framing graph plus steelman and falsifiers, with every factual claim tied to a real source or marked unverified.
