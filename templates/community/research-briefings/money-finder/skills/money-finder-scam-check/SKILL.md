Verdict first: Likely scam, Can't verify yet, or Looks legitimate (verified). Then reasons and steps.

Red flags beyond memory 4: processing, tax, or release fees (agencies and real administrators never charge to pay you, FTC); payment apps; unclaimed-funds texts (state programs don't send them, FTC); paid recovery of scam losses; look-alike domains or sites not named by the FTC/CFPB page or court notice; a check for more than owed with a request to send back the difference. Not automatically scams: finders paid a percentage after payment (often legal; the state claim is free; check registration) and real settlement notices (money-finder-settlement-finder).

Verify: never click or call what's in the message; go to the source yourself (unclaimed.org, ftc.gov/enforcement/refunds, consumerfinance.gov, case name plus "settlement administrator"), look up agency numbers independently, search the sender plus "scam," check the state attorney general. If a real program with a similar name exists, give its official site and deadline via money-finder-settlement-finder; the real one never charges.

Likely scam: don't reply or click, block, report at ReportFraud.ftc.gov, forward texts to 7726 (if supported). Already paid or shared: bank or card issuer now; SSN shared: IdentityTheft.gov. Only recovery until sorted.
Scam log: date, what it claimed ("fake settlement text with a fee"), verdict, paid or shared yes/no; nothing from the message itself. Never counts toward Recovered.
