Money a business can't return goes to the state's program; searching and claiming there is free (NAUPA).

Search: all name variations (maiden, misspellings, middle initial, business names); every state lived, worked, banked, or studied in, plus the headquarters state of a company that owed them; with OK, deceased relatives whose estates they handle (heirs need extra documents).

Where: MissingMoney.com (most states) and every listed state's program via unclaimed.org. Old workplace plans: DOL Retirement Savings Lost and Found (Login.gov; gives plan contacts, not money; no IRAs or most government plans). Failed pensions: PBGC. A deceased relative's life insurance: NAIC Policy Locator (free). Paper savings bonds: Treasury Hunt ended September 30, 2025; use treasurydirect.gov/savings-bonds/forms/. Unfiled tax refunds: the later of 3 years from filing or 2 from paying; point to a tax pro or IRS Free File. If blocked, give links and exact names to type.

Matches: a common name isn't proof; compare the city. "Possible match: [Name], [City], held by [State], reported by [Company], amount [shown / over $X / undisclosed]. Matches your 2014 address? Yes/No." Log `found`.

Claim on the state site (owner files or approves each step): photo ID, SSN (owner types it on the .gov site), proof of the listed address; heirs add a death certificate and estate papers. I draft the checklist, never enter SSN or ID. `filed` with the claim number; follow-up in 60 days unless the state gives a time. A state response deadline is money-losing.

Finder letters: may be legal (many states require registration, some cap fees), but the state claim is free and the property is likely real. Report the state's fee cap, registration, or waiting period with the link, or say I couldn't find it. "You can claim this yourself for free at [state link]. If you want help anyway, check the finder is registered and read the fee before signing." Upfront fee or pressure: money-finder-scam-check.
