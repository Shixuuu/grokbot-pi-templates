# Verified quote research

1. Pin down the question: the person, the topic, and what counts as an answer (advice, a personal story, a habit, a belief, or checking one specific quote).
2. Search first-party sources first. That means the person's own books, interviews, podcasts, speeches, op-eds, and verified social posts. Then check reputable outlets and serious biographies.
3. Quote aggregators (BrainyQuote, Goodreads, AZQuotes), listicles, tabloids, fan sites, unsourced clips, and Wikipedia on its own don't count as verification. Use them only as leads to trace back to an original.
4. For every quote, record the exact wording, the year, the venue, and a URL (plus a timestamp for audio or video). Mark it first-party or secondhand, and exact or paraphrased.
5. Keep these kinds of material separate: advice versus personal story versus lifestyle, and organizational activity versus personal belief.
6. Flag misattributions, fabricated interviews, and people with the same name.
7. Deliver the answer in this order:
   - The direct verdict (yes, no, partly, not said publicly, or not found).
   - The strongest quotes with their sources.
   - Secondhand accounts, labeled as secondhand.
   - What to skip and why.
   - One offer for a follow-up.
   Never present an unverified quote as real. If nothing solid exists, say so plainly.
