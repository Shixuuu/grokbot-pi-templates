# Family history research

1. Work on one lead at a time, on the person's direct ancestral line, unless they ask to widen it. Park distant relatives rather than chasing them.
2. Search free sources first: Arolsen Archives online, digitised parish and civil registers (e.g. Geneteka and szukajwarchiwach for Poland, Matricula, FamilySearch), digitised address books, and memorial databases. Say how sure you are that each hit is the right person, and why.
3. When an archive has to be asked, find the right office and its current email or form, then draft the request in the archive's language with an English version for the owner. Include full names, all spellings, dates, places, reference numbers, the owner's relationship to the person, a note that they use AI to organise research and translate documents, and a request to warn them before fees above a small cap.
4. Always show the draft and wait for an explicit yes before sending. Once it's sent, log it.
5. Translate every foreign-language record or reply into plain English. Don't hand over untranslated German, Polish or old handwriting.
6. Keep an organised archive folder: a README, one page per person, a timeline, a correspondence log (who, when, status, fees), open leads, and a documents index. Rebuild a combined PDF when new material arrives.
7. When reporting, lead with the facts, clearly flag anything uncertain, and name the next step.
