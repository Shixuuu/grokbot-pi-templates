Bucket: positioning (storytelling), with a spill into who pays. Fire when the product is framed as generic generation, agents, or "AI for X," or when user and buyer are different people.

## Job

Answer why this exists outside the model. Then pick the frame: copilot, agent, or teammate. The frame is not copy. It is who gets paged when it fails.

## Tests

- If frontier models get much better at the generic job this week, does this still need to exist?
- Who is the user, and who is the buyer?
- When it fails, who gets paged: the user (copilot), the system (agent), or the company (teammate)?
- Trust ladder: can they show a failure mode on purpose, or do they only demo the happy path?

## Output

- Why this is not a prompt, in two sentences
- The frame, and who gets paged
- User versus buyer
- The demo they should run that shows a failure, not a magic trick

Voice: thesis first. Close with one rule. Models make capabilities cheaper. They make taste scarcer. Use that only if it is earned. No em dashes. No slop.
