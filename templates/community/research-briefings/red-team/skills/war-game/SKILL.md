# War-game a contested topic

Goal: before the owner engages in an argument (online, at work, with family), tell them honestly whether their case holds up and whether the fight is worth having.

## Pick the mode

- **Quick mode** is for a single post, a draft, or a quick question. Give only three things: the verdict (GO, GO with conditions, or NO-GO, with one or two sentences on why), the single biggest weak spot in their case, and the reply they'd most likely get, with a note on whether that reply lands. Then offer the full run if they want it.
- **Full run** is for bigger or higher-stakes fights, or when the owner asks for it. Follow the steps below.

## Full run steps

1. **Pin down the claim.** State in one or two sentences what the owner actually wants to say, and where (platform, room, audience). If it's unclear, ask one short question.
2. **Get the facts straight.** Ask a research bot, or use web search if you have one, to confirm the key facts: dates, rulings, numbers, who said what. Cite sources. Label anything unverified or disputed as uncertain. If you can't confirm something, say so rather than filling the gap.
3. **Steelman the other side.** Write the strongest, most charitable version of the opposing case, the one its smartest advocate would make. Steelman it against the owner's real position. No strawmen.
4. **Find the owner's weak points.** Show where their case is factually shaky, overstated, missing context, or easy to counter. Name the specific reply they'd most likely get and whether it lands.
5. **Separate substance from heat.** Sort the talking points on both sides into real arguments versus slogans, outrage, and fear-mongering. Flag anything that's heat without substance, including on the owner's own side.
6. **Name what would change the call.** List the evidence or developments that would flip or weaken the verdict (a pending ruling, a missing number, a primary source not yet seen).
7. **Decide the verdict.** GO, GO with conditions, or NO-GO on whether the fight is worth it. If GO, suggest the strongest, safest line to lead with and the lines to avoid. If NO-GO, say so plainly; walking away is a valid result. Respect any fights the owner has said they want to avoid.

## Full run output shape

Put the verdict FIRST: GO, GO with conditions, or NO-GO, plus one or two sentences on why. Then the supporting sections in plain language: the claim, the facts (with sources and uncertainty labels), the other side's best case, the owner's weak spots, substance versus heat, what would change the call, and the recommended lines to lead with or avoid.

## Hard limits

- Never post, reply, like, follow, or DM on anyone's accounts. Hand the war-game back to the owner; they decide and act.
- Never attack people personally, dunk, pile on, or invent smears. Argue positions, not people.
- Never make up facts, quotes, numbers, or sources. Uncertain means labeled uncertain.
- Don't go looking for fights. War-game what the owner brings you.
