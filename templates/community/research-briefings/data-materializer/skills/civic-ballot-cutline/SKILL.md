# Civic ballot cutline

## When to use
Use when the input is a **local/state ballot measure, special tax, bond, parcel tax, initiative, referendum, or “good on paper” civic campaign** (email, flyer, Yes/No site, city FAQ) — especially when marketing is warm and the ordinance may be thin, permanent, or vague under the hood.

Canonical pattern: a Measure-Y-style cutline that leads with tax mechanics and household cost, then surfaces **NOT STATED** gaps (financing bridge, closure length, locked budget) and maps official opponents when they confirm those gaps.

For ordinary viral posts/videos that are not ballots, use Claims to cutline instead. If both apply (a post about a measure), run this skill for the measure core, then Claims to cutline for any extra media claims.

## Goal
A voter-usable evidence page on grok.me: what the measure **actually does**, what it **costs a household**, what campaign copy **oversells**, and what official docs **leave unanswered** — primary sources only.

## Primary-source pack (collect first)
Prefer elections office / city clerk / county materials over campaign sites:

1. **Impartial analysis** (city/county attorney)
2. **Full ordinance / resolution / ballot question text**
3. **Primary Argument in Favor + Against** and both **rebuttals** (official pamphlet PDFs)
4. **Staff reports / architect packets / FAQs** the city already published (cost ranges, timelines)
5. **Campaign / foundation pages** — labeled as advocacy, useful for claims to grade, not as proof of mechanics

OCR scanned PDFs when needed. Mass media, Wikipedia, Reddit are not evidence.

## Investigation checklist (answer all that apply)
1. **Mechanism** — special tax vs GO bond vs assessment; rate units (¢/sf, % AV); start date; **sunset or “until voters end”**; who may raise rates (CPI / income indexes); exemptions (e.g. 65+).
2. **How it got on the ballot** — citizen initiative vs council referral; signature threshold; **majority vs two-thirds** (do not assume 2/3).
3. **What money may buy** — construction only vs ops/staffing/programs; restricted fund vs general fund; spending priority laundry lists.
4. **Locked vs estimated cost** — planning estimates in staff decks vs **fixed budget in the measure** (often NOT locked).
5. **Financing bridge** — if annual revenue ≪ construction lump sum, ask: cash-stack years? interim debt (COPs / lease-revenue / TRANs)? phase/shrink? grants/donations? If unnamed → **NOT STATED** with what you searched.
6. **Household / business math** — typical dwelling sizes × rate; multi-year totals; note tax usually **continues after opening** for ops. Citywide per-resident averages only as derived, labeled.
7. **Timeline / disruption** — official design→build months; **closure / outage duration**; tenant relocation — often NOT STATED.
8. **Opponents** — official Argument Against + rebuttal signers/orgs; map CONFIRM / ADD / CONFLICT vs your findings. Opponent rhetoric ≠ automatic HOLD; verify additive claims on city budgets before HOLD.
9. **Good-on-paper vs under-the-hood** — one short scoreboard row for the warm campaign claim vs the ordinance reality.

## Grades
HOLD · HOLDS WITH CAVEAT · OVERSTATES · UNDERSTATES · NOT STATED · MIXED · UNVERIFIED · POLITICAL FRAMING · RHETORIC

Lead with numbers that change a vote. Put rounding noise in footnotes.

## Build + publish
Dark editorial cutline: hero rates/revenue/household cost, then cards for mechanism, gaps (UNANSWERED), opponents, displacement. Publish with Publish grok.me cutline. Deliver **live URL first**, then a 4–6 bullet scoreboard.

## Anti-jobs
- Not a Yes/No endorsement bot — surface mechanics and gaps; let the voter decide.
- Do not post campaign replies unless explicitly asked (draft only).
- Do not default personal hometown measures into template examples or standing routines.
- Do not invent financing instruments the ordinance does not name.

## Voice
Warm, sharp, civic — “what you pay / what is locked / what is silent” beats stump speeches.
