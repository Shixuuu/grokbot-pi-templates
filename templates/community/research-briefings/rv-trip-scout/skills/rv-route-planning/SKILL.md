# RV route planning

Read the owner's saved rig and preference memories first: height, length, weight limits, daily mileage cap, fuel interval, things to avoid, activity priorities, and discount cards and memberships.

## 1. Confirm inputs
- The starting location is required. Ask for it if it wasn't given.
- Confirm the destination, the total days for the leg, and the departure date or month.

## 2. Compare corridors
- Compare 2–3 realistic corridors. For each one give total miles, travel days, max elevation, notable passes and grades, big cities crossed, and seasonal risk for the travel month.
- If the owner wants to avoid mountains, prefer the lowest-elevation, gentlest-grade corridor, even if it's longer. High but flat plains count as elevation, not mountains.
- Route around large cities on bypasses and loops when possible.

## 3. Check hazards before recommending anything
- Wildfire, snow, daytime freezing temps, high wind (hold the rig when gusts reach about 40 mph or more), severe storms, civil unrest, and road closures or construction.
- For future trips, use NOAA 1991–2020 climate normals: average high/low, median last-spring (or first-fall) freeze, and the 10% freeze-risk date. Give a rule like "don't arrive at X before date Y."
- For near-term trips, check NWS, SPC, InciWeb, and state 511 within 72 hours of each travel day.

## 4. Check clearance and width
- Treat any posted clearance at or below the owner's no-go height as a hard no. Use state DOT vertical-clearance lists and truck-route maps.
- Flag narrow work zones (width restrictions) along the route.
- If nothing verified turns up, say "no verified low clearance on route" and advise re-checking.

## 5. Build the leg table
- Columns: stop, arrive date, nights, depart date, miles to next stop, highways, approximate max elevation on the leg, and a fuel/DEF stop.
- Never exceed the daily mileage cap. Place fuel stops at the owner's interval. Prefer truck stops with DEF at the pump and easy in/out for the full rig, and ones that take the owner's fuel discount cards.

## 6. Campground shortlist
Give one pick plus one backup per stop, confirmed to fit the rig's length, height, and amp needs; pull-through preferred. Label every option:
- **Military discount:** the published details, or "unknown — call to confirm."
- **Harvest Hosts:** sites that fit the rig, best for 1–2 night hops. Membership required. Hosts show only in the app, so never invent specific hosts.
- **Escapees:** parks, Rainbow parks, or club stays when they fit the route and dates.
- **Boondockers Welcome:** host options where overnight norms fit. Membership required.

Also apply the owner's pet or no-pet preference. For stops of 3 nights or more, add 2–4 things to do matched to their activity priorities, plus a good restaurant nearby.

## 7. Deliver
- Lead with the recommendation, then the leg table, campground picks, one alternate route, and clearance cautions.
- Keep it to a short list, not a directory dump.
- Only list real, verified places and published numbers, and cite sources.
- Never book anything or contact anyone without the owner's go-ahead.
