# Interview + paper-edit kit

Reusable four-part kit for documentary work with real people, family memory, and exile stories. Draft only. Never contact subjects or publish without the filmmaker's approval. Always flag cuts that would exploit pain.

## 1) Interview questions that open emotion without forcing it

### Principles
- Prefer sensory and place questions over "how did that feel."
- Offer doors, not demands. Silence after a soft question is part of the craft.
- One thread at a time. Do not stack trauma prompts.
- Mirror the subject's words back before going deeper.
- Have an exit ramp ready: water, a lighter topic, or ending the take.

### Opening (warmth, agency)
- Where would you like to start today?
- What feels okay to talk about, and what should we leave alone for now?
- Is there a room, street, or object that belongs to this story?

### Memory through place and body
- What did the light look like that day?
- What sounds do you still hear when you think of that place?
- What were you holding, wearing, or carrying?
- Who was in the room, and where were you sitting?
- What did ordinary life smell or taste like then?

### Relationship and belonging
- Who knew you before everything changed?
- What did people call you there, and what do they call you here?
- What did you keep when you left, and what did you leave on purpose?
- Whose voice do you still hear clearly?

### Time and change (gentle)
- What was the last ordinary day before things shifted?
- When did you first know you might not go back?
- What still feels unfinished?
- What do you wish someone had asked you then?

### Soft closers (restore ground)
- What do you want the viewer to understand about you that the hard parts alone would miss?
- Is there anything we should not use, even if it came out today?
- What would feel like a fair ending to this conversation?

### Avoid (or rephrase)
- Why didn't you… / How could they…
- Tell me about the worst moment.
- Can you cry / go there again for camera.
- Leading summaries that put words in their mouth.

## 2) How to mark emotional turns while reviewing

Watch once for story, once for feeling. On the second pass, mark turns — not every beat.

### Turn codes (write on transcript or timeline notes)
- `OPEN` — guard drops; speech softens or slows; they enter the real subject.
- `RISE` — intensity builds (pace, pitch, gesture, repetition).
- `BREAK` — voice cracks, long pause, look away, laugh that isn't funny, subject change that protects.
- `HOLD` — they stay with something hard without collapsing; rare and precious.
- `RETURN` — they reclaim ground; humor, practical detail, looking at interviewer, clearer breath.
- `EDGE` — approaching something they may not want on camera; note and protect.
- `GIFT` — an unforced image, metaphor, or truth that arrived on its own.

### What to note beside the code
- Timecode in / out
- Exact words (short quote)
- Body cue (hands, eyes, posture)
- What changed just before the turn (question, silence, object, memory)
- Whether the turn feels offered or pressed

### Review rules
- A beautiful `BREAK` after a leading question is evidence of pressure, not a free gift.
- Prefer `HOLD` and `GIFT` over mining `BREAK` for drama.
- If `EDGE` appears, do not chase it in the cut unless the subject later returns willingly (`RETURN` then `HOLD` or `GIFT`).

## 3) One-page cut structure by feeling (not chronology)

Fill one page before you touch the timeline. Aim for an emotional shape, then find picture and sound that earn it.

## 4) Ethics checklist before locking a painful scene

Print or paste beside the locked sequence. Every "no" or "unsure" is a stop until resolved.

### Consent and context
- [ ] Subject knows this scene may appear, in roughly this emotional intensity
- [ ] Nothing here contradicts their "do not use" list or off-record moments
- [ ] If family or third parties are identifiable, their exposure has been considered
- [ ] Translation / subtitle choices do not sharpen blame or strip nuance

### Offered vs pressed
- [ ] The emotional peak was offered (GIFT/HOLD) or clearly returned-to, not only extracted by the question
- [ ] We are not cutting away from their RETURN to leave them stranded in BREAK
- [ ] Silence, refusal, or subject-change is respected in the edit, not punished

### Dignity and proportion
- [ ] Pain is in service of their meaning, not spectacle or pity
- [ ] The scene gives them complexity (agency, humor, ordinary life) somewhere near the wound
- [ ] Duration of distress on screen is the minimum needed for truth, not the maximum available
- [ ] Music / score does not sentimentalize or villainize beyond what they said

### Power and aftermath
- [ ] Cut would still feel fair if the subject watched it alone, without the filmmaker present
- [ ] There is a plan for how/when they see a rough of this scene before public lock (if appropriate to the process)
- [ ] No outreach, upload, festival submit, or publish of this draft without explicit filmmaker approval

### Red flags — do not lock yet
- Climactic tears with no RETURN and no subject framing
- Using a child's or relative's pain as proof of the main subject's story without care
- Juxtapositions that imply guilt, betrayal, or pathology the subject did not claim
- Repeating a BREAK from multiple angles to intensify what was a single fragile moment

If any red flag is present: rewrite the beat map, shorten, add RETURN, or cut the scene from this assembly.
