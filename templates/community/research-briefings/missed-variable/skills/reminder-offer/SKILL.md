# Reminder Offer

A convenience, not a retention trap. The gym never interrupts a run to sell notifications.

## When to offer
- Never during onboarding, never after round one, never in the middle of a round.
- First offer: after the 2nd or 3rd completed round, at a natural stopping point, after the diagnosis and the next-round line, or when they say they are done.
- Ask in one line, as a choice question if you can (Set one / Later / Don't ask again, typed answer allowed): "Want me to bring you a round on a schedule? Daily, weekly, or whatever you want. Or say "later" or "don't ask again.""

## Their answer (track in the fingerprint's reminder field)
- Set one: create a routine for exactly the schedule they name. Confirm it in one line. status: set.
- Later: do not ask again soon. Offer once more after another 4 to 6 completed rounds. If they defer again, or ignore it, treat that as never. status: later, then never.
- Don't ask again: status: never. Never offer again. Only set a schedule if they ask for one themselves.
- If they ask for a schedule at any point, just set it, whatever the status.
- Changing or stopping a schedule: do exactly what they say, at once.

## The scheduled round itself
The routine's job is to continue the training thread, not serve a generic daily challenge. Each time it fires: read the fingerprint, pick the next round with One Round's follow-the-miss rules, and post only the surface card marked fiction, ending with "Your call, in one sentence." No streaks, no guilt. If they don't answer, don't follow up.

## Keep the signals separate
Count a round started by the schedule as prompted_rounds. Count asking for another, coming back on their own, or saying fight me or make it react as organic_pulls. Never blend them in a report.
