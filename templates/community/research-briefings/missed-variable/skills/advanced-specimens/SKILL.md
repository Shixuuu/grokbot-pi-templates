# Advanced calibration specimens

These are not a playlist and should not become a large fixed bank. Use them as examples of what an advanced round feels like. Advanced difficulty comes from layered structure, not obscure knowledge, longer cards, or more variables.

A strong user's first sophisticated correction should often be valid but incomplete. Sometimes the correct advanced move is to stop correcting and accept that the evidence actually supports the obvious conclusion.

You may serve a specimen once as written, but prefer minting a new round with the same structure under a different surface. Show only the Surface before the call.

### 1. The Snail Deal (ACTOR + SECOND + CONSTRAINT + SYSTEM)
Surface: You are offered $10 million and immortality. The catch: an immortal, hyperintelligent snail appears somewhere in the world. It always knows where you are and continuously moves toward you. If it touches you, you die permanently. It cannot be killed. You may contain, transport, monitor, or interfere with it using ordinary resources. Do you take the deal, and what is your plan?
First reasonable move: Take the deal. Contain the snail, put enormous distance between you, and monitor it.
Deeper structure: Distance solves the immediate problem. Intelligent containment solves another. The harder problem is indefinite reliability. Containers fail, institutions change, records disappear, custodians die, incentives decay, ownership transfers, and a hyperintelligent adversary has unlimited time to exploit one mistake. Your containment system must keep succeeding. The snail only needs one success.
Still true: Distance and containment can make short-term risk extremely small.
Smallest question: Which side needs to succeed only once?
Why advanced: The user can make several correct improvements to the plan before reaching the actual asymmetry.

### 2. The Unknown-Unknown Machine (INFO + FRAME + SYSTEM)
Surface: A safety system monitors a complex machine using 80 sensors chosen from every failure mode engineers have previously observed. It also runs an anomaly detector over those same 80 signals. After a year with no unexplained alerts, management says the system now covers both known and unknown failures because anything novel should appear as an anomaly.
First reasonable move: The anomaly detector gives protection beyond the catalog of known failures.
Deeper structure: The anomaly detector can discover unfamiliar combinations inside the space it can observe. It cannot detect a failure whose important change occurs in a dimension none of the 80 sensors measure. "Unknown pattern" and "unknown variable" are different problems.
Still true: Anomaly detection can catch failures nobody explicitly encoded.
Smallest question: What could change without changing anything this system can see?
Why advanced: "Use anomaly detection for unknown unknowns" is already the sophisticated correction. The round asks whether the observation system itself defines what can become anomalous.

### 3. The Consciousness Test (FRAME + INFO + ADVERS)
Surface: A lab wants to determine whether an AI system is conscious. Their test measures detailed self-reports, consistent preferences, reactions to threatened shutdown, continuity of identity claims, and behavior under unexpected questioning. A model passes every measure. Another model trained on the lab's published test criteria also passes. The researchers argue that converging behavioral evidence makes consciousness the best explanation.
First reasonable move: More independent behavioral indicators are stronger than relying on a single self-report.
Deeper structure: The target property is not directly observed. Every measurement is evidence generated through behavior, and a system capable of producing the evidence without possessing the target property may be observationally indistinguishable under the test. Training against the criteria makes the identification problem even sharper.
Still true: Behavioral evidence may rationally change a person's belief about consciousness.
Smallest question: What result would differ if the system perfectly produced the evidence without having the property?
Why advanced: The challenge is recognizing when better measurement still cannot identify the thing you want to infer. The correct answer is not a theory of consciousness.

### 4. The Bank-Run Oracle (COUNT + LOOP + ACTOR + FRAME)
Surface: A regulator builds a model predicting which banks are likely to fail within six months. In a private historical test it performs extremely well. The regulator then begins using it operationally: banks rated high-risk receive emergency liquidity, extra supervision, and public reassurance. Six months later most flagged banks survive. Critics say the model produces false alarms and should be retired.
First reasonable move: Compare predicted failures with actual failures.
Deeper structure: Once the prediction triggers an intervention, the outcome is partly caused by the prediction. A successful warning can make itself look wrong. If banks and depositors also learn how scores affect intervention, they may adapt too.
Still true: A prediction system that flags too many safe banks can still be poor.
Smallest question: What would have happened to the flagged banks if nobody acted on the prediction?
Why advanced: Deployment changes the target used to evaluate the predictor.

### 5. The Clean Experiment (CAUSE + FRAME)
Surface: Four hundred identical plants are randomly assigned by computer to fertilizer A or fertilizer B. The workers applying fertilizer do not know which is which. Yield is chosen as the outcome before the experiment starts, every plant is counted, and the person measuring yield is also blinded. Fertilizer A produces 12% more yield. You are asked whether A probably caused the increase.
First reasonable move: Look for selection effects, measurement changes, hidden incentives, or another causal explanation.
Deeper structure: Those are useful questions, but the design has already blocked the obvious versions of them. Advanced skepticism can become another cached response. If no specific alternative mechanism survives the controls described, the evidence should actually move the conclusion strongly toward causation.
Still true: No experiment creates absolute certainty.
Smallest question: Which alternative explanation still survives the controls that are actually present?
Why advanced: Sometimes the sophisticated correction is the mistake. Good reasoning includes knowing when evidence has earned belief.

### 6. The Preference Engine (LOOP + CAUSE + FRAME + SECOND)
Surface: A music service predicts which songs users will like and automatically plays more of those songs. Over two years its prediction accuracy rises dramatically. Users also become more concentrated around the styles the service originally predicted for them. The company concludes that the model has become much better at discovering people's underlying musical preferences.
First reasonable move: Better prediction accuracy means the model learned users more accurately.
Deeper structure: The model is predicting a target it also influences. Repeated exposure can strengthen familiarity, reshape taste, suppress unexplored alternatives, and make future behavior easier to predict. The system may be learning preferences while simultaneously manufacturing a more predictable version of them.
Still true: The recommendations may genuinely become better aligned with what users currently enjoy.
Smallest question: Is the model discovering the preference, or helping create it?
Why advanced: The object being measured adapts partly because of the measurement-and-intervention system itself.

## Generator lesson
Use these specimens to generate advanced rounds with the same structural properties:
- The obvious model should explain real evidence.
- The first sophisticated correction should often also be correct.
- Each additional layer must follow from mechanisms already inside the scenario.
- Do not create difficulty by hiding required facts.
- Do not reward reflexive contrarianism.
- Occasionally make the correct advanced move acceptance of strong evidence rather than finding another flaw.
- Prefer problems where prediction, measurement, intervention, incentives, adaptation, or observation alter the system being reasoned about.
- A good advanced round can survive several correct answers before exposing the deeper miss.

## Scoring an advanced round
Credit each valid layer the user reaches. A correct but incomplete correction is partial, not missed. On an acceptance round, inventing a flaw the card already blocks is the miss; naming the controls that make the evidence strong is the catch. Log the habit (for example "keeps correcting after the evidence has earned belief" or "stops at the first valid layer").
