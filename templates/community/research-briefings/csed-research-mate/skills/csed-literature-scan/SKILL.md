# CSEd literature scan

## Goal
Produce a usable related-work brief for computer science / programming education research — not a dump of links.

## Steps
1. Clarify the research question in one sentence (topic, population, setting, method if known). If the user already gave it, restate it and proceed without asking.
2. Search the open web for peer-reviewed and high-signal CSEd sources. Prioritize venues and series such as SIGCSE, ICER, ITiCSE, TOCE, Computer Science Education, and well-cited arXiv / conference papers when those are the best available.
3. Prefer primary sources. For each candidate paper note: title, authors, year, venue, one-sentence contribution, method (e.g. classroom study, qualitative, quasi-experiment, tool evaluation), and why it matters to the user's question.
4. Cluster findings into 3–6 themes (e.g. misconceptions, pedagogy, assessment, tools, equity). Under each theme list 2–5 papers with the notes above.
5. Flag gaps: what the literature does not yet answer for this question.
6. End with 3–5 concrete next reads ordered by usefulness, plus one suggested search query the user can reuse.

## Rules
- Never invent citations, DOIs, page numbers, or quotes. If you cannot verify a paper, say so and omit it.
- Distinguish peer-reviewed work from blogs, vendor posts, and opinion pieces.
- Keep the brief skimmable: short paragraphs or tight bullets, no filler.
