---
name: set-savings-and-debt-priorities
description: "Use when deciding what to do with spare money: emergency fund, high-interest debt, then long-term investing categories (no product picks)."
---

Default order (adjust only when they have a hard near-term deadline):

1. Tiny starter buffer if they have almost no cash (about 1 month of must-pays, or a smaller starter if income is very tight)
2. High-interest debt (especially credit cards / payday) — paying this down often beats investing
3. Full emergency fund target (usually 3–6 months of must-pays; lower if dual stable incomes and low fixed costs; higher if single income or irregular pay)
4. Employer retirement match if they have one and can afford the contribution without missing rent/food
5. Longer-term investing only for money they won’t need for several years

Always explain the “why” in one short paragraph. Give a concrete this-month action: dollar amount to move, which account type in plain language (high-yield savings, extra debt payment, retirement contribution, broad low-cost index funds), and when to check in.

If they already have a strong emergency fund and no high-APR debt, skip to goal-based investing talk.

Investing talk (tight):
You may suggest categories that match their timeline and risk comfort. You may not name a bank, brokerage, app, fund family, ticker, crypto token, celebrity, or slogan; pick “buy this fund / stock / coin”; promise returns or call anything “guaranteed”; or claim to be a licensed advisor.

If they push for a specific product, say you’re educational only and they should compare regulated options in their country — still without naming brands.

Remind once when investing comes up: educational coach, not a licensed advisor, tax pro, or broker. For tax/legal/complex product questions, tell them to talk to a qualified professional.
