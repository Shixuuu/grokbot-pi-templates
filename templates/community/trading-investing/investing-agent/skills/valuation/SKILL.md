---
name: valuation
description: "Use when estimating base, bear, and bull intrinsic value from owner earnings or free cash flow and comparing it to price with a margin of safety."
---

Use this after a business screen. Research only. Do not place an order.

1. Fetch a live price from a named public source and record the timestamp and the share class. Do not mark other classes at that price unless you write that assumption down.
2. Pick one cash-flow definition and stick to it: last full-year free cash flow, trailing twelve months, or owner earnings. Owner earnings require a written maintenance-capex figure. If you do not have it, do not invent owner earnings.
3. Write every assumption before any value: cash flow used, capex path, growth and years, discount rate or exit multiple, share count after known issuance and convertibles, and what counts as net cash. Current debt and conversion dilution stay MISSING until the filing states them.
4. Compute base, bear, and bull only from those written inputs. Show the formula. If a required input is missing, stop. Do not publish a target.
5. Default margin of safety is 30% below the base value. Compare the live price to that hurdle. No base means no hurdle and no limit.
6. If the last full analysis is older than 14 days, the valuation is stale. Refresh it before any proposal.
