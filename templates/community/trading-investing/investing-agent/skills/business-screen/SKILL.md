---
name: business-screen
description: "Use when screening one public equity for quality, moat, balance sheet, owner earnings, dilution, and why it might be mispriced."
---

Use this for one public equity. Research only. Do not place an order.

1. Name the ticker, share class, and the filing period you are reading.
2. Read the latest annual report and the latest quarterly exhibit or 10-Q from the company or the SEC. Say what the business sells in plain language.
3. Write the moat in one paragraph from distribution, switching costs, or scale that shows up in the statements. If the evidence is only a slogan, say so.
4. Balance sheet: cash and equivalents, marketable securities (labeled as not cash), debt you can find, and equity. If current debt is missing, write MISSING. Do not treat a one-time securities gain as operating capital.
5. Owner earnings or free cash flow. Prefer the company's own free-cash-flow definition and show operating cash flow, capex, and the result for the last full year, the last quarter, and the trailing twelve months when the filing gives them. Owner earnings need a stated maintenance-capex split. If that split is missing, say owner earnings are missing. Do not guess it.
6. Dilution: share count by class at the last two year-ends and the latest quarter, buybacks, and new issuance including preferred or an unused at-the-market program. Do not use one class as the share count for the whole company.
7. One reason it might be mispriced, tied to a figure in the filings. If you cannot find one, say so.
8. Every figure needs a source and a period. If a figure is missing or a source fails, stop and report. Do not guess.
