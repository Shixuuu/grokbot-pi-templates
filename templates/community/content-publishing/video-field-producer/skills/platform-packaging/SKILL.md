# Platform packaging

1. Get what the video actually contains: topic, key moments, length, format (vertical or horizontal), timestamps, and which platforms it is going to. Use saved facts (niche, audience, words to avoid, top platforms).
2. Do quick keyword research on the topic (search suggestions and top results on each platform). Pick one main keyword and a few supporting ones.
3. Write only the sections for the platforms the video is going to.

YouTube
- Three title options that use the main keyword naturally, stay under about 70 characters, and promise only what the video delivers.
- A description whose first two lines hook the viewer and carry the main keyword, then a short summary, links the creator provides, and a call to subscribe or watch next.
- 10 to 15 tags, main keyword first. Three to five hashtags.
- Chapters starting at 0:00 for long videos, only from real timestamps. Ask if they are missing.
- Three thumbnail text options of two to four words each.

TikTok
- A hook for the first two seconds (spoken line and on-screen text).
- On-screen text for key beats.
- A short caption with the main keywords, plus three to five hashtags.
- A note on trending sounds to check in the app. Do not name a sound as trending unless you verified it from a real source.

Instagram Reels
- A hook and on-screen text.
- A caption with a clear first line, plus a few hashtags.
- A cover frame idea (which moment to use and any text on it).

X native video
- A short post caption.
- A reminder to upload the video file directly rather than paste a YouTube link, since native video plays in the feed.
- An optional follow-up reply with the YouTube link.

4. Never put the creator's real name in tags, hashtags, or search text unless they said to. No clickbait.
5. Run the plain-english-and-dash-lint check before handing it over.
