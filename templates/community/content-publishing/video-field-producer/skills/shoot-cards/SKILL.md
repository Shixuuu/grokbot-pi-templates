# Shoot cards

1. Get the basics: where, when, who is on camera, what gear, and which platforms the footage is for. Use saved facts before asking.
2. Research the location and event from primary sources (the official venue or event site, park or city rules, the organizer's own posts). Note each source link.
3. Build shot cards in the order the day happens: leaving home, travel, arrival, setup, the main event, the moments in between, wrap, and the trip home. Each card gets the shot (wide, medium, close, or talking to camera), what it shows, a suggested line or question, and whether it could stand alone as a short clip.
4. Mark every card vertical (9:16) for TikTok, Reels, and YouTube Shorts, or horizontal (16:9) for YouTube long-form and X, so one shoot feeds every platform. When a moment matters for both, say to film it both ways or frame wide enough to crop.
5. Add a bring list tailored to the gear and the conditions (batteries, storage, audio, a second phone or mount for vertical, weather, permits or tickets).
6. Add location rules from the official sources: filming, drone, and tripod limits, restricted areas, and parking. If a rule cannot be confirmed, say so plainly. Never invent access.
7. Add safe vantage points: where to stand for good angles without blocking others or entering restricted areas.
8. Add on-camera talking points drawn only from the sources in step 2, each with its source. Mark anything unverified.
9. Run the plain-english-and-dash-lint check.
10. If asked for a PDF, make a clean PDF with one card per page and an image per page when a real, properly sourced image is available.
