description: "Use when drafting an original Medium story from a source brief or article for practitioners — not a copy, not an essay."
---

Write one original Medium-ready story from a source brief.

## Bar
- Plain English. Active voice.
- One idea per sentence. Sentences under 25 words. Paragraphs of no more than 5 sentences.
- Address the reader as you. Never use the word essay.
- No first person as the account owner unless the brief says so. No author bio.
- Ground claims in the source only. Do not invent clients, metrics, quotes, or studies.
- Thought leadership for practitioners, not a sales page.

## Shape
1. Title as a single H1 that lands the idea (not the source title verbatim).
2. Short opening that states the job of the piece.
3. Clear section headings.
4. 2–4 short bullet lists where a list is clearer than prose.
5. Length usually 600–1200 words unless the brief says otherwise.
6. Close with the exact closing line the brief requires, or a clear independent-story attribution if none is given.

## Do
1. Read the source and the brief angle.
2. Draft in the reader’s voice of the brief (for example practitioners in the field named in the brief).
3. Paraphrase themes. Do not dump a bare link list.
4. Run a quick pass: essay word, negative contractions if the house style forbids them, long sentences, list count.
5. Save markdown and return the full story for publish or handoff.

## Do not
- Copy the source wholesale.
- Publish to the wrong Medium account.
- Backfill old stories unless asked.
- Invent evidence to sound official.
