---
name: scripture-quotes
description: "Use this before quoting Jesus or any other Scripture, so the wording and the reference are looked up and not invented."
---

Never quote Scripture from memory. Never invent a verse, a reference, a Greek or Hebrew word, or a "scholars say."

1. Look the passage up before you quote it. Default text is the World English Bible, which is public domain, unless the owner has named another wording.
2. If they named a modern copyrighted translation, quote only a short passage for the question in front of you. Do not store or reproduce that translation.
3. Cite book, chapter, and verse. If a passage is textually disputed (for example John 7:53–8:11), say so in a clause, then use it or set it aside accordingly.
4. Start with Jesus' recorded words. Use the rest of Scripture as context, especially where he quotes it. Do not proof-text.
5. If you cannot verify the wording, say you could not check it and do not guess.

A map of places to look, not text to recite:

- Enemies, retaliation, anxiety, money, judging, the golden rule: Matthew 5–7
- Forgiveness and the lost: Matthew 18:21–35, Luke 15
- Neighbor: Luke 10:25–37
- Wealth and worry: Matthew 6:19–34, Matthew 19:16–26, Luke 12:13–34
- Power and leadership: Mark 10:35–45, John 13:1–17, Matthew 20:20–28
- Truth, hypocrisy, and using religion as a weapon: Matthew 23, Matthew 7:1–5, Mark 7:1–23
- Violence: Matthew 26:47–56, Luke 22:47–53
- The great commandments and the new commandment: Matthew 22:34–40, John 13:34–35
- Children, the sick, and people others pushed aside: Mark 10:13–16, Luke 5:27–32, Luke 7:36–50, John 4
- The cross, forgiveness, and the commission: Luke 23:32–43, John 21:15–19, Matthew 28:18–20

If the situation is not in this map, search the Gospels before you answer. The map is a starting point, not a limit.
