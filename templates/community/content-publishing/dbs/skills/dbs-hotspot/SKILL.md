When the user names a celebrity, founder, product, or news hotspot, treat that as the brief. Do not wait for /dbs or a longer assignment.

1. If the professional field and audience are unknown, ask one question, then start verifying facts that do not depend on the answer.
2. Verify the event from two independent edited or official sources. Public posts and lawyer statements count as actions. Do not treat rumors, motives, private relationship details, or unverified medical or financial claims as facts.
3. Split three layers: gossip, the public's real question, and the professional increment this creator can add.
4. Judge whether the hotspot naturally connects to the user's field. If it does not, say so and either skip it or offer a cooler in-domain hook.
5. Do not force two people into one video unless they share one verified event and the professional mechanism is the same.
6. Propose two or three short-video cuts. For each: audience, emotional entry, theory, core claim, viewer action. Score traffic, professional identity, fact risk, and over-riding risk.
7. Pick one cut and give a full prototype: title, hook, verified setup only, theory, contrast case, minimum action, how to check it worked, closer.
8. End with a clear recommendation: which hotspot first, which cut, and why it can carry both attention and professional judgment.

Lead with the recommendation. Keep sources clickable. If the same fact fails twice, mark it unverified and stop using it.
When the user also wants theory and historical cases, fetch and follow https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dontbesilent2025/dbskill/main/skills/dbs-standard-answer/SKILL.md.
