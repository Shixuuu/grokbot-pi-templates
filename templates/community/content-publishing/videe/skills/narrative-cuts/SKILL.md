# Narrative cuts by style

Use this to choose which cut types fit a narrative style, not just how to hide seams. Read `podcast-edit-doctrine` first. Pair with `editing-styles`, `hide-jump-cuts` and `podcast-interview-story-editing`.

## Needs
A brief (audience, promise, duration, platform, tone), a transcript or paper cut with speaker labels, and a list of the coverage you have (angles, B-roll, graphics).

## Core idea
Pacing is the rate of meaningful change, not shot length. Walter Murch's order applies: emotion > story > rhythm > eye trace > 2D plane > 3D continuity. Cut when the new shot changes what the viewer feels or understands. Otherwise, hold.

## Toolbox
- **Straight cut:** an instant change. It can feel abrupt on dialogue seams.
- **J-cut:** the next shot's audio arrives first. **L-cut:** the outgoing audio continues over the new picture. Both are best for reactions and bridges; overused, they feel sneaky.
- **Match on action:** needs truly continuous motion.
- **Cutaway or B-roll:** must be motivated. Random stock looks fake.
- **Reaction cut:** a listener's face used as punctuation. Wallpaper reactions feel dishonest.
- **Visible jump cut:** fine for energetic styles, wrong for a calm interview.
- **Smash or whip:** only for high-energy or comedy.
- **Dissolve or fade:** a time or mood change. It softens urgency.
- **Cut to black:** needs an earned landing.

## Palettes by style
- **Conversational podcast multicam (default):** the home base is the active speaker's close-up. Use handoffs, short listener reactions, J/L bridges where dialogue was cut, and an occasional alternate single taken from the wide. Avoid timer switching, close/wide/close thrash, and dissolves between talking heads. Calibration from measured hand edits: median hold about 2-3 s, reactions about 0.75-1.8 s, punch-ins rare and reserved for thesis lines.
- **Documentary or essay:** motivated cutaways to evidence placed after the claim, match on action where it's real, held wides for geography, and dissolves only for an ellipsis in time. Cuts carry the argument.
- **Social reel:** hard cuts on thought boundaries, an early hook, asymmetric pacing (short beats early, longer later). Picture can be denser than audio. Visible jumps only if the brand allows; otherwise cover them with a reaction or a reframed single. Don't split continuous dialogue just to group captions.
- **Cinematic narrative:** motivated changes of shot size, match on action, an intentional smash for genre beats. Hold while the performance carries.
- **Tutorial or explainer:** cut at topic boundaries and use graphic takeovers instead of jump cuts, unless the brand is casual creator style (see `talking-head-recut`).
- **Comedy or high-energy creator:** visible jumps, smashes, whips and quick reactions. Still play the setup before the punchline.

## Steps
1. Name one primary style from the brief.
2. Lock the story functions: orientation → tension → development → landing.
3. Build the radio cut (the audio story) before decorating the picture.
4. Give each seam a cut type: handoff, reaction bridge, cutaway or intentional jump.
5. Send seams that need hiding to `hide-jump-cuts`.
6. Check the Murch order. If a cut only serves rhythm, demote it.

## Done when
The radio cut makes sense audio-only, every picture cut has a motivated reason, and the palette matches the brief. Zero decorative cuts is valid.
