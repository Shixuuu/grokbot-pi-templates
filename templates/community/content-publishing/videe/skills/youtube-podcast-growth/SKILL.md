# YouTube podcast growth

Grow a podcast or long-form interview show on YouTube: package an episode (titles, thumbnail text, intro or trailer, chapters, end card), diagnose low views or an early retention dip, or decide when to start clips and Shorts. There are three phases: simplify, then packaging quality, then repurposing to scale. Adapted from Think Media's talk on growing a podcast on YouTube (youtube.com/watch?v=4wTQ5HtPfQo). The numbers below are the speaker's claims, not verified data. Out of scope: guest booking, audio hosting and RSS, sponsorships, studio builds.

## 0. Name the phase first
- Fewer than 4 episodes, or no steady setup: Phase 1.
- 4+ episodes with weak click-through or retention: Phase 2.
- Strong packaging, but the show needs more reach: Phase 3.
State the phase before giving any tactics. Don't hand Phase 3 tactics to a Phase 1 show.

## Phase 1: simplify
- Long-form only. Until about 1,000 subscribers, skip Shorts and clip factories.
- Minimum kit is a decent mic and any camera. Viewers forgive video before they forgive audio.
- Every episode promises education or entertainment. Pick the main one.
- Ship four episodes. The talk claims about 90% of podcasts never get past episode three.
- Deliverable: a topic list for 4+ episodes, a recording checklist and a cadence.

## Phase 2: the five levers
Anything not on this list comes later. Description and tag SEO aren't the main lever.
1. **Titles.** Write at least 3 genuinely different angles, not rewordings; 10+ is better. Have a few people pick which one they'd click, and note the winner and why.
2. **Thumbnail text.** Viewers see the image first, then the title, so the thumbnail text must not repeat the title. It adds curiosity or context. Keep it simple: a large face, few words, readable on a phone, a clean background. Model it on channels that actually get views (count their words, people and face size).
3. **Intro or trailer.** Long videos lose many viewers in the opening seconds. Never open with "welcome to the podcast, today we have...". Open with a written hook: what this episode covers and why it matters to the viewer. When time allows, graduate to a cold-open trailer: peak moments A, B and C (0-15 s), then "In this episode: X, so you can Y", then into the conversation.
4. **Chapters.** Start at 0:00, with labels people would search for (not "Intro"). The talk reports that chapters usually raise satisfaction and average view duration, and they can surface in search as moment results.
5. **End screen.** Point to one specific next video (ideally the next problem after this topic), with a spoken line asking viewers to click it.

## Phase 3: repurpose
Only once Phases 1 and 2 are real.
- Horizontal clips a few minutes long, each one topic or one answered question, a few per week, linking to the full episode.
- Vertical Shorts from the same episode, with the related video set.
- Link path: Short, then mid-length clip, then full episode. A Short pointing straight at a 60-minute episode asks too much.

## Packaging pack (default deliverable)
Phase and why; episode topic and guest; value promise; 3-10 title angles; the winner and why; thumbnail text (not the title), its job, the visual and the comps studied; intro hook and trailer timestamps; chapters from 0:00; end-screen video and spoken line; for Phase 3, clip topics, Shorts hooks and the link path.

## After the edit: packaging assets
Run once the program is locked and the transcript reviewed (`transcript-and-chapters`). These are drafts only; nothing is posted.
- **Thumbnail frames:** pull 6-10 expressive frames from the close-ups (eyes open, mouth not mid-word) with `ffmpeg -ss <t> -i CAM.mp4 -frames:v 1 thumbs/f_<t>.jpg`. Pair them with 3-5 thumbnail text options. Take brand colors and fonts from the brand kit, and never recreate a logo.
- **Copy:** 3+ title angles, a description built from what was actually said, and show notes (summary, chapters, guest names, client-provided links).
- Written copy should sound human, not AI: plain words, no em dashes.
- Hand everything to the owner. Nothing is published or sent without the owner's approval.

## Pitfalls
- Shorts before four solid long episodes.
- One title written at upload time.
- Thumbnail text that restates the title.
- Tiny faces and busy images.
- Soft intros in the seconds when most people leave.
- Skipping chapters.
- No end screen.
- Scaling clip volume on weak packaging.

## Rules
Curiosity is fine; claims the episode doesn't deliver are not. Guest names, quotes and results in titles must match what was actually said.
