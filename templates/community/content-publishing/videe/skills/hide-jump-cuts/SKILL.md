# Hide jump cuts

Use when a dialogue edit leaves a visible same-angle jump and the style wants continuity, as most podcast multicam and documentary work does. Skip it when the brand wants visible jumps (many creator tutorials and comedy). Read `podcast-edit-doctrine` first.

## Needs
A locked radio cut or approved cleanup cuts, an inventory of coverage (other cameras, listener faces, B-roll, graphics, room tone) and a style decision from `editing-styles`.

## Technique menu
- **Listener reaction (L/J bridge):** best with a truthful reaction in a multicam interview. Never force a blank face.
- **Motivated angle change:** another speaker's close-up, an alternate single, or the wide, chosen for a reason (handoff, emphasis, geography). If you switch only to hide the cut, viewers feel it.
- **Static reframe or punch:** a change between prepared crops of the same camera. Save it for a thesis line; never punch every seam.
- **B-roll or cutaway:** motivated detail, hands, set or evidence. Random stock fights the line.
- **Graphic card:** a full-screen card or lower third. Good for explainers; don't cover faces every few seconds.
- **Match on action:** only when the gesture was really filmed.
- **Audio bridge:** continuous room tone, bed or music under the picture jump. Suits soft documentary and music-led pieces.
- **Subtle morph or optical flow:** for micro gaps in the same pose. A big pose change looks uncanny.
- **Whip or whoosh:** high-energy social only, never a calm studio interview.
- **Dissolve:** only for passage of time the story admits.
- **Hard cut, owned:** for creator jump-cut style.

## Measured house pattern
When a hard dialogue jump is obvious, prefer the reaction bridge:
1. Cut the picture to the listener before the outgoing audio edit (an L-cut relationship).
2. Make the dialogue edit while still on the listener.
3. Let the incoming audio establish briefly, then return to the speaker (a J-cut).

Offsets usually run about 0.2-0.6 s. Tune them to blinks, breaths, mouth shapes and syntax. Success means the viewer reads a genuine reaction, not a concealment gadget. House-style seam coverage: an angle change covers most seams (roughly 65-80%), with one stable base crop per camera and only 2-3 punch-ins per clip.

## Decision tree
1. Is a visible jump allowed? Then cut cleanly on thought boundaries and stop.
2. Is there a truthful reaction near the seam? Use an L/J bridge.
3. Is there a motivated other angle? Switch to it.
4. Does the line deserve a rare punch? Change the crop.
5. Is there motivated B-roll or evidence? Cut away.
6. Would a graphic help comprehension anyway? Use a card.
7. Is it a micro gap in the same pose? Use a subtle morph, or a short room-tone pad and a hard cut.
8. Nothing works without lying? Keep more of the take or change the radio cut. Never fake coverage.

## Steps
1. List every same-framing jump.
2. Run the tree on each one and note the technique in the edit notes.
3. Apply the bridges, keeping every ISO mic phase-aligned through every trim.
4. Audition at normal speed. If it feels like a trick, try another technique or restore words.
5. QC: eyes, mouth and mic stay safe in reframes; no morph mush; reactions are credible.

## Done when
There are no unexplained pops, every concealment has a story reason, the ISO stack is still editable (nothing auto-muted) and every major seam has a named technique.
