# Podcast, interview, documentary and testimonial story editing

A pure judgment skill that works in any NLE. Meaning comes before polish, the audio story before visual concealment, evidence before endorsement, and verification happens on a real full-length export. House rules, camera policy and run order are in `podcast-edit-doctrine`.

## Ask for
- Original files and a timecoded transcript with speaker, camera and mic labels.
- A brief: audience, promise, duration, platform, tone, required facts, prohibited claims, CTA.
- The delivery spec.
- For testimonials: verified identity, a signed release and usage scope, any incentive disclosure, and evidence for each claim.
- The show's brand kit (design, voice, captions, lower thirds, don'ts). If it's missing, ask before the final render and never invent assets.

## Procedure
1. **Story contract and ethical floor.** One sentence each for audience, promise, central question, stakes, feeling and action. Classify the piece (news is strictest, then documentary, then conversational or branded). Lock what can't change: qualifiers, chronology that matters to causality, uncertainty, attribution, consent limits.
2. **Ingest.** Keep the originals and version the sequences. Sync every camera and mic, checking lip sync at both the head and the tail. Label where any archival, stock or AI material came from. Keep room tone and every ISO mic. A transcript is never authoritative until someone has listened to it.
3. **Watch once without cutting.** Mark hooks, turns, evidence, caveats, strong reactions and silences. Log the sensitive selects: speaker, file, timecode, exact text, context, function, claim type, consent.
4. **Paper cut, then radio cut.** The arc runs: earned hook → orientation → question → complications and evidence → turn → consequence → resolution. Join beats with "because / but / therefore", not "and then". The audio-only assembly has to work without pictures. Gates: selects → paper → radio → scenes → assembly → rough → fine → lock.
5. **When to cut.** Pacing is the rate of meaningful change. Murch's order: emotion > story > rhythm > eye trace > 2D > 3D. Cut only when the new shot changes understanding; otherwise hold. Audition each cut early, on time and late.
6. **Shot size by function.** Wide for geography; two-shot for listening and interruption; MCU as the default; close-up for a genuine emotional turn (arrive before the peak); inserts for evidence. Cut to a listener only for a real, synchronized response. Multicam switching follows `camera-direction`.
7. **Retakes, filler and breaths** (proposal-only; ordinary pauses stay). Work in passes: content → cadence → micro-edits → restoration → mix. Use the best complete take. Drop filler only when it carries no meaning or character. Keep natural breaths, meaningful silence and crosstalk that shows rapport. Never remove a qualifier or splice together words that weren't said.
8. **Invisible dialogue edits.** Cut at breaths or low-energy boundaries, keep consonant attacks, use the shortest crossfade, and fill with same-mic room tone, never digital silence.
9. **Hiding jumps.** In order of preference: gesture → different angle or size → honest cutaway → reframe → morph last (`hide-jump-cuts`).
10. **Documentary.** Scenes and natural sound are evidence; interviews are testimony. Never borrow reactions or effects from other moments. Narration orients; it doesn't repeat.
11. **Mix.** Dialogue first. Use the loudness table in `podcast-edit-doctrine`, and never mix standards. Check on headphones, a phone speaker and mono, and check the captions against the final audio.
12. **Talking-head YouTube.** The opening matches the title and thumbnail promise. Keep a stable home frame; every visual change has a job (prove, explain, reset attention).
13. **Testimonials.** One buyer, problem, objection, proof and next step, with the customer as protagonist. Use their actual words; never combine lines into a stronger endorsement. Keep a claim ledger: timecode, baseline, value, timeframe, typicality, qualifier, disclosure, approved wording. No stock or generated "customers". Disclosures carry into every cutdown, and "results not typical" doesn't cure a misleading claim.

## Pitfalls
- Switching mechanically on every speaker change.
- Removing every um and breath, which sounds robotic.
- Hooks that drop qualifiers.
- B-roll wallpaper hiding the speaker.
- Dissolves that advertise a bad cut.
- Assuming one loudness target fits every platform.
