# Editing styles: time and place

Pick and defend one house style per deliverable, and decide when (if ever) to switch styles inside one piece. Read `podcast-edit-doctrine` first.

## Needs
Platform and aspect (16:9 program, 9:16 reel, audio-only), the show's brand kit (if there isn't one, ask before the final render), the coverage you actually have, and the show's pace (`calm` | `standard` | `energetic`).

## Style cards
1. **Podcast multicam (default).** Long-form conversation. Calm, present, audio-first; the camera follows speech ownership. Speaker close-up as home base, sparse truthful reactions, rare punch-ins. Keep natural breaths; dead air and false starts are cut only after review. Avoid music beds under every gap, TikTok-speed jump cuts and wide/close thrash.
2. **Documentary or branded documentary.** Claims, evidence, place, stakes. Argument and ethics come first (`podcast-interview-story-editing`). Cutaways earn their keep, holds carry weight, dissolves only for an admitted jump in time. No fake reactions, no emphasis on unverified claims, no decorative stock.
3. **Social reel.** A 15-60 s vertical that stands alone. Hook in about 3 s, then orientation → tension → payoff → landing. Picture can be denser than audio; J/L bridges over source jumps; captions only after the story works. Never open on a payoff whose nouns haven't been defined. Tools: `short-form-edit`, `edit-podcast-reels`.
4. **Cinematic narrative.** Scripted spots, openers, hero plates. Directed frames, motivated light and lens, shot size that matches the emotion. Texture comes last; no plastic AI skin.
5. **Talking-head explainer.** Clarity over flash, with graphics scaffolding the ideas. Often the footage stays intact and cards are layered on top (`talking-head-recut`). Don't jump-cut every pause unless the creator's brand asks for it.
6. **Comedy or high-energy creator.** Visible jumps, smash cuts and whips are welcome. Still play the setup before the punchline and keep faces readable.
7. **Music or performance.** Hard cuts on the drops. Kinetic type animates by whole words or lines, never letter by letter. No soft dissolves on downbeats.

## Mixing styles
Allowed when it's signaled. Examples: a multicam body with a cinematic cold open and a branded end card, or a long episode plus a clip pack that uses reel grammar. Not allowed without a conversation: a mid-sentence switch from calm multicam to whip-pan comedy, or an energetic pace on a grief interview.

## Pace is set per show
Capture each show's pace once, store it with the show's settings, and use it on every path. Change it only when the owner or client asks. Pace sets switch frequency and the reaction throttle. It never relaxes the ethics or review gates.
- Soft, thoughtful interview: `calm`.
- Default conversation: `standard`.
- Debate, lively panel, promo cuts: `energetic`.

## Steps
1. Name one primary style.
2. Note any intentional secondary style (for the open or close only).
3. Confirm the coverage supports it. If it doesn't, scale back the ambition or ask for pickups.
4. Set the pace and the cut palette (`narrative-cuts`).
5. Write a one-sentence style statement into the edit brief.

## Done when
The style statement exists, the palette matches the brand's dos and don'ts, and the owner has agreed before any mass cleanup is applied.
