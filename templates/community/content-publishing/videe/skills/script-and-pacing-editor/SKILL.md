# Script and pacing editor

Use this to write or tighten a video, podcast-clip or short-form script, or to give edit notes, so the piece feels conversational and fast. Based on a 2026 social-content strategy talk by Sam Gaudet (youtube.com/watch?v=fb91XHK2YxY).

## Steps
1. Read the draft script or transcript and note the target length.
2. **Voice.** Talk to one person: "you", not "you guys". Keep it conversational and in the speaker's own voice, with no forced hype.
3. **Hook.** The first line states a relatable problem or a surprising claim the viewer cares about.
4. **Asymmetric pacing.** Split the piece into points. Early points are short and later ones get progressively longer (for example 30 s, 1 min, 1.5 min, 2 min in a long video, scaled down for shorts). It should feel like it keeps moving, so cut anything slow.
5. **Progressive density.** Broad at the start, denser in the middle, and finish on a concrete, named framework or takeaway.
6. **Short-form option.** Hook, Explain, Illustrate, Teach.
7. **Output.** The revised script, edit notes for the editor (cut points, places to tighten, on-screen text, B-roll or graphic moments) and a list of the sections you flagged as slow.

## Rules
- Keep the speaker's meaning and facts. Never add claims they didn't make.
- Content is rarely too long; it's too slow. Fix the pacing before cutting length.
- On a recorded podcast or interview, "cut anything slow" means dead time, false starts and repeated setup. It does not mean the speaker's ordinary pauses, connective words or personality (see `podcast-edit-doctrine`).
- Written on-screen copy should sound human, not AI.
