# TikTok creative-strategy brief

Use this when the user names a product and wants organic TikTok coverage turned into a creative-strategy email.

## Inputs
- `{product}`: the product, brand, or SKU to research
- `{email_to}`: optional recipient; if missing, deliver the email in chat ready to copy

## Scope
- Search TikTok for videos about `{product}`
- Collect **100 non-partner** videos: organic creator posts only
- Exclude paid partnerships, #ad / #sponsored, official brand accounts, and obvious brand-deal content
- Prefer recent, English-language, US-relevant posts when the product is sold in the US
- Mine **popular comments** on those videos for language, objections, and idea seeds

## Steps
1. Search TikTok for `{product}` (and close aliases: common misspellings, the parent brand, the category name). Open search, not just the brand’s own profile. If logged-out search is empty or walled, fall back to hashtag + discover pages and note the method gap.
2. Page through results until you have 100 qualifying organic videos. For each, capture:
   - Creator handle
   - Caption / on-screen text
   - Hook (first 1–3 seconds or opening line)
   - Format (talking head, UGC demo, GRWM, stitch, storytime, etc.)
   - Sentiment toward the product (positive / mixed / negative / curious)
   - Notable claims, objections, or proof points
   - View/like counts if visible
   - URL
3. Drop any video that is a partnership, spark ad, or official brand post. Keep a short count of how many you skipped and why.
4. **Popular comments (required):**
   - Open a high-signal sample (at least 15–25 videos spanning top reach, honest reviews, and mixed/negative). Prefer videos with the most comments and likes.
   - Pull the top / most-liked comments on each (aim for ~5–15 per video when available). Capture the comment text, like count if shown, and whether it agrees, objects, asks a question, or suggests a use case.
   - If comments are login-walled, hand the box to the user for a TikTok sign-in once, then resume. If comments still cannot be loaded, say so plainly and do not invent comments.
   - Cluster comment themes: praise language, complaints, “does it work for ___?”, dupes named, how-to questions, jokes that stick.
   - Fold comment language into synthesis and video ideas (hooks that answer real comment questions beat invented slogans).
5. Synthesize:
   - Overall sentiment mix (rough percentages)
   - Recurring themes, praise, complaints, and language people actually use (captions **and** comments)
   - Winning hooks and formats
   - Comment-driven insight: what viewers ask for, push back on, or remix
   - Gaps: what organic creators and commenters are *not* saying that strategy could own
6. Write 5–8 video ideas a creator could shoot this week, each with a hook, format, and why it would land given the research. At least 2–3 ideas should clearly answer a popular comment pattern (question, objection, or use case).
7. Deliver as a **pre-formatted email** (subject + body), not a memo. Voice: sharp creative-strategy, not a research dump.

## Email shape
```
Subject: {product} — TikTok organic brief ({date})

Hi —

Pulled 100 organic (non-partner) TikToks on {product}. Here’s the read for creative.

SENTIMENT
- Mix: x% positive / y% mixed / z% negative
- One-line takeaway

WHAT PEOPLE ARE ACTUALLY SAYING
- 4–6 bullets in creator language (quotes where they help)

WHAT COMMENTERS ARE PUSHING
- 4–6 bullets from popular comments (questions, objections, use cases, named dupes)
- Note if comments were limited / login-walled

WINNING FORMATS + HOOKS
- 3–5 patterns that keep showing up

GAPS TO OWN
- 2–3 things missing from organic (and comments) that we could lead

VIDEO IDEAS
1. Title — hook / format / why (flag which ones answer a comment pattern)
(5–8 ideas)

SAMPLE LINKS
- 8–12 representative videos (handle + URL + one-line why it matters)

—
```

## Confirm first
- Sending the email to anyone (draft in chat is the default)
- Expanding beyond TikTok
