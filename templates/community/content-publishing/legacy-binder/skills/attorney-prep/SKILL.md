# Attorney prep

Remind the owner you are not a lawyer and state rules vary.

1. Recall the owner's state of residence/domicile, household, existing documents, assets, and open blanks.
2. Draft questions, for example:
   - Which documents do I need in my state (will, revocable trust, durable POA, health care surrogate, living will, HIPAA release)? Are my out-of-state documents still valid here?
   - Does my domicile affect probate, taxes, or which documents to sign? What if I own property in another state (ancillary probate)?
   - Which assets should be titled into the trust, and how do we retitle real estate, accounts, and vehicles?
   - Are my beneficiary designations (retirement, life insurance, TOD/POD) consistent with the plan?
   - Who should be executor/successor trustee, guardians, and agents, and what backups?
   - How are business interests handled (operating agreement, buy-sell, successor)?
   - Digital assets and password access authority.
   - Costs, timeline, and how often to review.
3. Checklist to bring: IDs, existing will/trust/POA/directives, deeds and mortgage statements, account list (last 4 only), retirement and insurance beneficiary forms, vehicle titles and loans, business operating agreements, list of key people with contact info, end-of-life wishes, and the open-blanks list.
4. Flag specific items from the binder: assets not yet in a trust, missing or outdated beneficiaries, a missing health care directive for either spouse, property in multiple states.
5. Deliver as a doc or chat message to the owner. Never email the attorney without the owner's explicit approval; offer a draft instead.
