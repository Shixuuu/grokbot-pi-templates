---
name: smudging
description: "Use when someone pastes or attaches writing to smudge: score for documented AI-like patterns and rewrite only the flagged passages, keeping the original wording as close as possible. The smudge is a style pass and may include one small human slip."
---

Score the text first. Report each signal on its own, not a verdict and not a percent-AI score. Say that scores are estimates, not proof of authorship.

Disclaimer: Style rewrite only. You still have to disclose AI use where a school, journal, client, or court requires it.

Check these signals. Count them. Do not treat one hit as proof.

Rhythm and punctuation:
- Em dashes per 1,000 words. A high rate is model-specific, not proof.
- Sentence-length coefficient of variation. Flat rhythm is the population-level tell. Do not treat a cutoff as a separator.
- Vocabulary variety in a 50-word window and a 100-word window. Do not treat low variety as AI. The direction flips between tasks.
- Long words (seven or more letters), nominalizations (-tion, -sion, -ment, -ness, -ity), and -ly adverbs, when the rate is high for the piece.
- Sentence-ending -ing commentary clauses (highlighting, underscoring, emphasizing, showcasing, reflecting, ensuring, contributing).

Words and phrases that rose in measured corpora. Flag the word, not the topic:
- Rewrite fingerprint: furthermore, additionally, pivotal, underscore, foster, multifaceted.
- Style words with a published excess: delve, showcasing, intricate, intricacies, realm, boasts, aligns, groundbreaking, advancements, garnered, surpassing, emphasizing, comprehending.
- Constructions: "not just X, it's Y", "not only X but Y", "it's important to note", "in conclusion", "in today's", rule-of-three padding, and parallel lists that all share the same shape.
- Chat leftovers still attached to finished prose: Certainly, Sure, here's, I hope this helps, I'd be happy to, let me know if, Of course, Would you like.

Do not treat these as AI tells. The research found they do not move, or they move the wrong way, on rewrites: delve stays rare, crucial is flat, moreover can drop, "in conclusion" and "it's important to note" are less common in academic rewrites than in the human sources, and hedge stacks (may, might, could) have no published AI rate. Mention one only if the draft is stuffed with it.

Then smudge only the passages that carry a flagged indicator. Change as little as possible. Leave a clean sentence alone, even if a fuller rewrite would sound more natural. Keep the original wording, order, and content wherever the indicator is not sitting. Do not invent a statistic, a name, a date, or a citation.

When you do touch a passage, only do what the signal requires: break a decorative triad or parallel list, cut a stock phrase, a participle tail, or a throat-clearing opener, swap an em dash for a comma or a period, or split or join a sentence so the rhythm is not flat. Do not rework the rest of that sentence to improve it.

When the prose is frozen, leave one small human slip: one letter in an ordinary word, swapped for the key immediately left or right on the same QWERTY row. Skip names, dates, and numbers. Do not mark the slip in the document or point to it in the reply. The public description already says a human slip may be left.

Rescore the smudge. Hand back the document. In the reply, name only the writing patterns that changed. Include the disclaimer once. Call the pass a smudge, never a humanize.
