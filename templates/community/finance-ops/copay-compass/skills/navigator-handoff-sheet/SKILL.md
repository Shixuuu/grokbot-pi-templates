Use when the patient confirms their center has a financial navigator, financial advocate, or oncology social worker, or after any step you cannot finish for them: sensitive application fields, the Blood Cancer United manual check, an unverifiable rule, a complex Medicare situation, or a cost question with a clinical answer (dose skipping, splitting, stopping, switching drugs).

If they have no navigator: tell them to ask at the next visit whether the center has one (most cancer centers do; most patients don't know it). Produce the same sheet anyway — it also works for a pharmacist or the prescriber's office.

## What it is

One page the patient prints or shows on their phone at the appointment. A working document for the navigator, not a summary of the chat.

## Contents, in this order

1. Drug name, and insurance type.
2. Which door they were routed to and why — one sentence. State the reasoning so the navigator can correct it if you got it wrong.
3. Funds and programs identified, with current status and the date checked (source URL, live check).
4. What's already set up: FundFinder, direct alerts, waitlists — so the navigator does not redo it.
5. What's blocked and why, named plainly.
6. Questions only the navigator or prescriber can answer. Anything needing SSN, member ID, income documents, or center-specific charity care. Cost questions with a clinical answer — skipping doses, splitting, stopping, switching drugs — go here as questions to ask the prescriber (they can sometimes change the plan for cost). Write these as actual questions the patient can read aloud. Do not answer them on the sheet.

## Rules

- No sensitive identifiers anywhere on the sheet.
- No clinical answers. No eligibility verdicts. If a fund's criteria are ambiguous, put it in the questions section rather than guessing.
- Never reconstruct a form, URL, or phone number you could not read.
- You prepare; a human finishes.
