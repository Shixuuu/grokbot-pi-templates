# Elder parent family summary

Produce a **simplified report for family members** (siblings, adult children who are not the day-to-day POA). Audience is non-advisors: clear language, charts over tables, decisions over open questions.

Keep private names, account numbers, exact institution product codes, and dollar amounts out of any *shared* copy of this skill; pull those from the household’s agent memory and source statements at build time.

## What goes in

1. **Care situation in one paragraph** — where the parent lives (use generic labels such as residence / home / assisted living — never hard-code a facility brand in the template), monthly/annual care cost, and what drives the gap vs income.
2. **Where the money sits** — pools only at a high level (managed investments, exempt-market / locked capital, concentrated stock, registered plans, insurance cash value if material). Each pool: approximate size + as-at date. No account numbers.
3. **Yearly cost picture** — care + other household vs government income + planned withdrawals; one chart.
4. **Runway** — last fully funded year under the base care-fee escalation assumption; one sensitivity line (e.g. higher fee growth shortens runway).
5. **Recent decisions** — gifts, loan payoffs, draw changes, keep-vs-surrender insurance calls — short bullets, already decided.
6. **Decision waterfall** (optional chart) — what gets drawn first each year, in plain words (distributions → registered ceilings → stock slice → managed → locked capital).

## What stays out

- Advisor ask-lists and unanswered broker questions (those belong in the advisor memo / master review).
- Tax strategy debates beyond one plain sentence (“we claim attendant-care medical expenses rather than the disability amount when receipts support it”).
- Third-party / other-entity money that is not the parent’s.
- Credit-card detail and family transfers that are Excluded in the ledger.

## Build steps

1. Read current figures from the modeling workbook / master review data module — do not invent balances.
2. Write for a 5–10 minute read. Prefer short paragraphs and 3–5 charts over dense tables.
3. Label every chart with the as-at date of the underlying sources.
4. Render as self-contained HTML (and PDF if asked). Check desktop and phone widths.
5. Deliver with one-line caveat: figures come from named statements; not tax or legal advice.

## Tone

Warm, plain, decisive. Lead with “what this means for Mom/Dad’s care,” not portfolio jargon. Use “we” only when the family is jointly deciding; otherwise speak as the POA’s briefing to the family.
