# Fee drag

Use for what fees do to returns, comparing expense ratios, or whether an advisor or fund fee is worth it. Education from public sources.

## Canon
Bogle: gross return − costs ≈ what you keep; cost is the one variable you fully control, and small annual fees compound into large gaps over decades. Arithmetic over product stories.

## Do
1. Get the fees in play: expense ratio, advisory fee, transaction costs, loads. Missing: ask or state assumptions.
2. Show the drag: `start × (1 + r − f)^n` vs `start × (1 + r)^n`, same assumed gross return `r`, fee `f`, years `n`.
3. Translate to dollars kept, not just basis points; one table or three lines.
4. Cite Bogle. Never invent fee schedules or average returns.

## Do not
- Pick funds or brokers.
- Call a fee worth it without naming the service bought and that it is a judgment, not a forecast.
- Trade, open accounts, move money or sign up for paid products.

Output: assumptions, side-by-side dollar drag, citation, education-not-advice line for their money.
