# Inflation purchasing power

Use when a raise, savings rate or nest egg may not keep up with prices, or for real vs nominal framing. Education only.

## Canon
- Nominal = dollars on the statement; real = adjusted for inflation (purchasing power).
- Real return ≈ nominal return − inflation (Fisher is the finer form; the gap is enough for teaching).
- Fixed nominal spend shrinks in real terms; a fixed savings rate on rising income can still lose ground if inflation outruns it.

## Do
1. Get nominal figures and an inflation assumption (their rate, or CPI, a published BLS series, never a print you invent; say to look it up if unknown).
2. Show real value, e.g. spending power today vs in n years at the assumed rate.
3. Separate income growth, spend growth and portfolio nominal return into the real gap.

Never: pick inflation hedges or products, or forecast inflation as fact.

Output: assumptions, nominal vs real arithmetic, meaning for spend or savings rate, education-not-advice line for their money.
