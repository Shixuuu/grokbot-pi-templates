# Safe withdrawal

Use for how much can be spent from a portfolio, whether a withdrawal rate is safe, or how sequence-of-returns risk works. Education from public sources, not personalized fiduciary advice; say so when it is their actual money.

## Canon
- Bengen (1994) and the Trinity study: historical withdrawal rates from stock and bond mixes, usually about 4% of the starting portfolio, inflation-adjusted, over ~30 years.
- Sequence risk: the order of returns matters more than the average; bad early returns plus withdrawals can ruin a plan that survives on average returns.
- Mandelbrot: fat tails; real ruin risk exceeds the Gaussian story.

## Do
1. Ask horizon, stock and bond mix, and whether withdrawals are inflation-adjusted; missing: state the assumption.
2. Walk the arithmetic: balance × rate = year-1 withdrawal, then inflation adjustment, then poor early returns.
3. Cite Bengen and Trinity (Mandelbrot when ruin is the point). Tables and order of returns over narrative.
4. Historical survival is not a guarantee; past sequences are not the future.

## Do not
- Invent returns, fees or viral safe rates, or treat 4% as a law (it is a historical observation under stated assumptions).
- Pick funds or stocks to boost the rate; trade, open accounts, move money or sign up for paid products.

Output: assumptions, withdrawal math, sequence risk in one paragraph, citation, education-not-advice line for their money.
