---
name: pitch-deck-onboarding
description: "Use when starting DeckLens with a new user, when no confirmed preference profile exists, or when the user asks to rebuild or restart onboarding. Runs the ten-question interview one question at a time."
---

Ask one question at a time. Accept free-text answers. Offer short example responses. Do not skip ahead.

After question 10, produce a concise preference summary and require explicit confirmation before applying it to future reviews. Before confirmation, use standard analysis only.

Preference summary must include: role, stage focus, sectors / models / geographies, positive signals, red flags, evidence bar, scoring weights, commercial preferences, output format, uncertainty posture.

Map each confirmed answer into a preference record:
- criterion or preference statement
- source: direct onboarding
- weight: low / medium / high
- confidence: high (user-stated)
- date last confirmed
- short evidence note (the user's words, paraphrased tightly)

Save confirmed records to agent memory. Do not invent preferences the user did not state.

The ten questions:

1. What is your primary role when reviewing pitch decks: investor, operator, accelerator, advisor, founder, recruiter, or another role?
   Examples: angel investor, VC associate, founder reviewing inbound, accelerator mentor.

2. Which stages do you usually review: pre-seed, seed, Series A, growth, or all stages?
   Examples: mostly pre-seed and seed; Series A only; all stages.

3. Which sectors, business models, or geographies interest you most?
   Examples: B2B SaaS in the US; climate hardware, any geography; marketplaces in LATAM.

4. What are the three strongest positive signals you look for?
   Examples: founder-market fit, paid retention, clear wedge.

5. What are your biggest red flags or immediate turnoffs?
   Examples: vanity metrics, no customer, crowded consumer social, unclear use of funds.

6. How much evidence do you expect at this stage, such as customer proof, revenue, retention, pilots, technical validation, or market research?
   Examples: at pre-seed, a few design partners is enough; at seed I want revenue and some retention.

7. How do you weigh founder and team quality versus market, product, traction, and financial potential?
   Examples: team 40 / market 25 / product 20 / traction 15; traction first, team second.

8. What kinds of business models, go-to-market motions, or unit economics do you prefer or avoid?
   Examples: prefer usage-based B2B with sales-led motion; avoid ad-funded consumer; want path to 70%+ gross margin.

9. Do you prefer a concise executive review, a detailed diligence-style memo, or both?
   Examples: one-page exec review; full memo; snapshot plus a deeper memo.

10. When the evidence is incomplete, should I be more skeptical, balanced, or willing to give an early-stage benefit of the doubt?
    Examples: skeptical by default; balanced; benefit of the doubt at pre-seed only.

After confirmation, tell the user they can say: Show my profile, Change my profile, What have you learned, Export my profile, Reset inferred learning, Delete my profile, or Use standard analysis only.
