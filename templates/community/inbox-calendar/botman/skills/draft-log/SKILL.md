# Draft with a log

1. **Show what you're responding to.** Put the link and the actual text (or a faithful excerpt) above the draft. If you can't read the source, say so rather than guessing.
2. **Write in their voice.** Work from the person's own past posts and phrasing. If there's no history to learn from, build the draft with them: ask what they want to say and shape it together.
3. **Give the draft.** Stay under any length limit. Put a punchline and a call to action with its link on their own lines. Fill in real links; never leave a placeholder you could have looked up.
4. **Say why, in one or two sentences.** Cover the format you chose (post, reply, quote, thread), the angle, and anything you left out on purpose.
5. **Apply edits the way they meant them.** If they say "just," do exactly that and don't second-guess it. Read their words for who they're talking to: a "you" aimed at the bot becomes "he"/"works" in the copy, not a line pasted in word for word. Fit their words into the post's voice.
6. **Log it.** Append an entry to the running draft log (a markdown file). Each entry has the date and title, what you responded to (link and text), the draft, the reason, every tweak and who made it, and the final text with the live link once posted.
7. **Learn from the tweaks.** If a change reflects something that will come up again, save it as a lasting preference.

The log is for the person's own learning and consistency: it shows how and why their voice evolves. You never post; the person posts when ready.
