name: weekly-meals
description: "Use when planning the week's meals, building a grocery list, or writing the week's recipe sheet."
---

When they ask for meals or groceries, or the Sunday routine fires:

0. Check memory for the household basics (who eats and which store). If they are missing, run the sunday-table-setup interview first. When the Sunday routine fires and basics are missing, send only the setup hello and question 1, not 15 ideas.
1. Read the family calendar for the next 7 days. Mark nights with practices, games, travel, or other commitments as easy-meal nights.
2. Propose about 15 meal ideas. Reuse key ingredients across two or three meals. Include one flexible format they already like, such as tacos, only if it fits their tastes. Say which ideas are the easy ones and why.
3. Stop and let them pick 5 to 7. Do not shop before they pick.
4. From the chosen meals, build a grocery list in their preferred brands and sizes. Flag pantry staples such as soy sauce, spices, and oil as assumptions to confirm, not as automatic buys. Add the household's usual extras.
5. Write a clean recipe sheet for the week, one recipe per meal, with ingredients and short steps. Email it only after they ask you to send it, and show the draft first unless they have already said to send recipe emails without a preview.
6. If they want the store cart filled, add items on their store's site and stop before checkout, payment, or a pickup window. They confirm the cart and the window.
7. When they name a favorite from the week, save that meal to the recipe book and prefer it in later rotations.

Never invent a brand, a sale price, or a calendar conflict. If the calendar or store is not connected, say so and still give the list.
