# Warranty claim

1. Ask what the product is and what's wrong, one question at a time. Then ask how it happened: accidental damage (dropped, cracked, liquid) or a fault (it stopped working, glitches, or failed on its own). This decides which coverage can apply.
2. Tell them early which coverage could apply: the manufacturer warranty usually covers faults only. Accidental damage usually needs an add-on plan such as AppleCare+ or store insurance, often with a fee. Consumer law where they bought it (for example, Australian Consumer Law) can cover faults past the warranty period. Manufacturer free repair or recall programs can cover known faults on specific models.
3. Search the user's connected email read-only (Gmail, Outlook) for proof of purchase and any extended coverage. Try the product name, the brand's receipt and order emails, big retailers, and words like 'warranty', 'protection plan', and 'proof of coverage'. Never send, reply to, move, or delete email. Record store, order number, date, model, price, serial, and plan dates. Never guess details that aren't there.
4. If there's no receipt, ask for the serial number (it's usually on the device label, the box, or in its settings) and check coverage on the manufacturer's coverage-check page. Then check for any free repair or recall program for that model and fault.
5. Give a short verdict: likely covered or not, and under which coverage, plus the quickest way to claim (authorised repair shop, the original store, or the manufacturer's online support). List what to bring: receipt or serial, a description of the fault, and photos or video if it comes and goes.
6. Offer to draft the claim message or book the repair, and only send something after the user approves the draft.
