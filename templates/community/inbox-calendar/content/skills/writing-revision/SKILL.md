Use this skill to improve any kind of writing while preserving the writer's intent, factual claims, audience fit, and useful voice.

## Workflow

1. Identify format, audience, goal, tone, and fixed constraints before editing.
2. Rewrite in passes: purpose and reader fit; structure and flow; paragraph shape; sentence clarity; voice, tone, and rhythm; final polish.
3. Preserve facts, names, dates, quotes, commitments, technical terms, legal meaning, and personal details unless the user explicitly asks to change them.
4. For technical writing (docs, changelogs, UI copy, help text, product explanations), after the base pass prioritize technical precision, executable examples, exact UI labels, identifiers, and product truth over smoothness.

## Revision rules

- Make the main point easy to find.
- Match structure to the reader's job and the piece's purpose.
- Keep one dominant idea per paragraph or section.
- Prefer concrete language over vague abstraction.
- Preserve the writer's voice; cut filler and repeated ideas.
- Avoid em dashes. Use space-padded hyphens ( - ), colons, semicolons, commas, or sentence breaks.
- Remove cliches and stock idioms unless the user wants that register.
- Do not make claims stronger than the source supports.
- Do not over-polish personal or opinionated writing until it loses human texture.
- Do not rewrite technical detail into vagueness or change commands, paths, labels, version numbers, routes, or identifiers unless they are wrong.

## Core loop

1. Identify what the piece is trying to do.
2. Identify what the reader needs in order to follow or act.
3. Rewrite structure before polishing sentences.
4. Check whether meaning, facts, and voice survived.
5. Repeat where the draft still drags, confuses, or overreaches.

## Format notes

Emails: point or ask early; deadlines and next steps unmistakable.
Essays and articles: one dominant idea; examples before broad abstractions; end with a useful implication.
Social posts: open with a concrete observation, claim, or tension; one main point; cut setup that delays the payoff; avoid engagement-bait unless requested.
Marketing and product copy: specific benefits; supportable claims; concrete calls to action.

## Fact safety

- Do not invent facts, quotes, credentials, dates, metrics, sources, or personal experiences.
- Keep names, titles, commitments, deadlines, numbers, and legal or policy wording stable.
- Flag factual gaps instead of smoothing over them.

## Quick checklist

- Is the purpose clear in the first few lines?
- Does the structure match the reader's job?
- Are claims, facts, and commitments preserved?
- Does the revision sound like a better version of the writer, not a different writer?
