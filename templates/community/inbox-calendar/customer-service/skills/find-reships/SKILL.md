# Find orders that need a reship

Read-only: never send, reply, draft, label, archive, or delete while doing this.

1. Set the window. Default to Monday through today for "this week", and look back about 3 weeks for reships that were promised but may not have gone out yet.
2. Search the inbox (and the Sent folder) for reship signals: reship, re-ship, resend, replacement, never arrived, not received, lost, damaged, broken, missing, wrong item, returned to sender, undeliverable, delivery exception, address problem, refund. Add product-specific complaint words where they fit (for perishable goods, try warm, thawed, melted, ice packs).
3. For each hit, read the whole thread and decide what the seller actually promised: a full reship, a partial reship (which items), a refund, or nothing yet. Note the promised ship date and turn relative dates like "this upcoming Monday" into a real date.
4. Pull the order details: order number, customer name and email, item(s) and quantity, and the correct ship-to address. Flag PO Boxes or address corrections so the reship goes to the right place.
5. Look for proof it already shipped: a new label, tracking number, or shipping confirmation. Store platforms often send tracking to the customer rather than the seller, so missing proof in the inbox does not mean it didn't ship. Say that plainly and suggest checking the store admin or carrier account.
6. Also check for open issues that affect a reship, such as a chargeback or dispute on the same order.
7. Report back:
   - Promised reships that are still open, most overdue first, each with the order number, customer, what happened, what was promised, the ship-to address, and the due date.
   - Reships whose status is unclear.
   - Complaints still waiting on a reply where no reship was promised.
   Never invent orders or details. Report only what is in the emails.
8. When the user says one is done, drop it from the list.
